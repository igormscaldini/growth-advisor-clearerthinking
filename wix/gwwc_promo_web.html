🌊 Imagine you're walking past a pond and see a child drowning. You'd jump in to save her, even if it ruined your expensive shoes.

Now imagine the pond is thousands of miles away. Would you still save the child?

That's Peter Singer's famous thought experiment. And the far-away pond isn't entirely hypothetical: malaria kills around 600,000 people a year, and about $7 is what it costs Malaria Consortium to protect one child through a malaria season, by GiveWell's figure. That child is just as real as the one in the pond. You just can't see her.

In principle, distance changes nothing. In practice, it's easy to feel moved and do nothing. Giving What We Can's pledge turns that intention into a plan: you commit to giving a share of your income to the charities you believe can do the most good per dollar, whether in global health, animal welfare, or global catastrophic risks.

More than 11,000 people in 121 countries have made this commitment. Even $1 a day could protect around 40 to 50 children through a malaria season each year.

Igor, who leads growth at Clearer Thinking, took the pledge this spring and is glad he did. Unsure whether it was right for him, he started with the Trial Pledge, a smaller commitment that can help you decide whether to take the full 10% Pledge.
