Conversion points audit: what each prompt and page on Clearer Thinking earns

Generated 2026-11-06 02:52pm · data from GA4, GuidedTrack and Stripe for the 12 weeks from August 14 to November 5, 2026 (the tools floating button: October 21 to November 5)

The short version. In 12 weeks the paid flows produced 34 Navigators, 6 Explorers and 6 Supporters ($3,674 of first-month subscription revenue), 90 PDF sales ($810) and 32 Cognitive Assessment sales ($1,120). The two floating buttons are the best prompts CT has: one of them was clicked before 6 of the 14 purchases that could be traced. They also send a lot of accidental phone taps. The pages themselves convert; the money is lost in three places: an email step before payment that 44% of Navigator clickers never complete, unpaid Stripe checkouts (55 Navigator, 240 Cognitive Assessment), and a product-grid PDF card that sends its clickers to the wrong page.

How often each prompt gets clicked

Share of the people who could see each prompt and clicked it. The denominators differ (site visitors, tool visitors, test finishers), so compare neighbours, not extremes.

1 and 2. The coaching floating buttons

Website button. 102,365 people visited the website outside /coaching and 1,343 of them opened /coaching through the button: 1.31% (95% interval 1.24 to 1.38). The split by device is the story: 2.2% of phone visitors clicked against 0.3% of desktop visitors.

Tools button. It went live on October 21, in the middle of a viral wave on the Who Attracts You study (92,602 tool visitors in three days). Since the wave ended, 1,151 of 27,175 tool visitors clicked it: 4.24%, about 89 people a day. Phones 4.9%, desktop 2.6%.

8. Coaching CTAs inside tools

  • Unique Traits Test, before the report (AI-personalised pitch, live since August 19): 42 clicks from 5,225 finishers, 0.8% (95% interval 0.6 to 1.08). No purchase can be tied to it. Its link also lost its tracking tags around September 17, so GA4 now files these visits under "direct".
  • Member dashboard, My Plan: 37 clicks to /coaching.
  • End-of-tool CT+ banner (the perks image at the end of the personality report): 39 people reached /plus through it, and 1 traceable Explorer purchase followed.

Things that looked wrong, and what they turned out to be

  • GA4's standard reports hide on-site promotions. A session keeps the first campaign it arrived with, so a button clicked mid-visit is credited to Google or to an email. Session reports show 555 visits from the website button; the page URLs show 1,343 people. Every count here uses the URL, not the session.
  • Scanners click the button link too. 870 hits on the website button's link came from sessions that never really opened (email link scanners, the September 1 bot burst). They are excluded.
  • GA4 cuts link addresses at 100 characters, which chops the campaign name off the tools button's link. Counts allow for it.
  • Stripe held 161 test checkouts in the period, nearly all from the daily site health check. Excluded.
  • Tools button clicks (5,873) exceed arrivals on /coaching (4,829 views). About one click in five never loads the page, which fits stray taps closed at once.

What I would do, in order

9/10

Label every sign-up button

Add &src=coaching or &src=plus (plus the tier) to the Sign Up links in Wix. The sign-up program already stores a src value on each run (the daily health check uses it), so nothing else has to change.

In plain words: Each button gets a name tag, so every click and every sale says which page it came from.

Strongest case for: Ten minutes of work, and it ends the main blind spot of this audit: today Navigator clicks from /coaching and /plus cannot be told apart, so neither page can be A/B tested.

Strongest case against: None of substance. It measures, it does not lift anything by itself.

9/10

Send the Sign Up click straight to payment

After clicking Sign Up, visitors land on a GuidedTrack page that asks for their email before Stripe opens. 52 of 118 Navigator clickers (44%) never typed it. Let Stripe collect the email (it asks for it anyway) and keep the email step only for people who say they are already members.

In plain words: Remove the extra page between the Sign Up button and the payment form.

Strongest case for: It is the largest single drop in the Navigator funnel, and two readers wrote in the last two weeks that signing up was confusing. Even recovering a quarter of those clickers at the current checkout completion rate is about 5 more Navigators per 12 weeks.

Strongest case against: The email step is how the program recognises existing members (12 of the 118 clickers already had a plan) and sends them to their account instead of charging them twice. The checkout function is the team's code, so it needs a developer.

8/10

Follow up on abandoned checkouts

55 Navigator checkouts and 240 Cognitive Assessment checkouts were opened and left unpaid in 12 weeks, and each one has an email address attached. Turn on Stripe's abandoned-checkout recovery, or send one plain follow-up email a day later.

In plain words: When someone gets to the payment form and leaves, send them one reminder with a link back.

Strongest case for: These are the warmest leads CT has: they chose a product and typed their email. Recovery emails usually win back a few percent, and at $99 a month each recovered Navigator matters.

Strongest case against: It needs a small change in the checkout code and clear wording so it does not feel like chasing. Some of the Cognitive checkouts came from low-intent ad clicks and will not return.

8/10

Point the grid's PDF card at the PDF page

The PDF card is the most-clicked card in the product grid (632 people in 12 weeks), but it opens the report finder, where the visitor has to enter an email and find their report again. For Ultimate Personality Test finishers, link it to the PDF page with their results; hide the card in tools that have no PDF.

In plain words: People who click 'PDF report' should land on the page that sells the PDF.

Strongest case for: The PDF page turns about 1 visitor in 10 into a buyer and only 906 people reached it in 12 weeks. If the card's clickers landed there and bought at even half that rate, that is roughly 31 extra sales per 12 weeks.

Strongest case against: The grid is one shared program used by several tools, so it needs a way to receive the test results. People who click from the Rationality Test may not have taken the personality test at all.

7/10

Stop the floating button from catching thumbs

On phones, show the button only after the visitor has scrolled or finished a tool, make it a little smaller, and send a GA4 event on every click. Run it for two weeks against the current version before deciding.

Strongest case for: On the website, phones click it 8 times as often as desktops and stay 14 seconds; from the tools they stay 6 seconds. Fewer accidental taps means cleaner /coaching numbers and less annoyance during a test.

Strongest case against: It is in the path of 6 of the 14 purchases that could be traced, more than any other prompt. Anything that hides it risks losing real buyers along with the stray taps, which is why it should be tested, not just changed.

7/10

Publish more members-only articles

The first members-only article produced 5 paying Supporters from 7 checkouts in its first week (October 16 to 22). The /plus page produced 1 Supporter in 12 weeks.

Strongest case for: It is the only thing that has sold the $9 tier in any volume, and it sells it at the moment the reader wants something specific.

Strongest case against: One article is a small sample, and part of the result may be novelty. It also depends on the team writing gated pieces regularly.

6/10

Give /plus more visitors

/plus gets about 65 visitors a week against several hundred on /coaching, yet it was viewed before 6 of the 12 traceable purchases. Link to it from the tools floating button on alternate weeks, the end-of-tool banner and the newsletter footer, then compare.

Strongest case for: Per visitor it is the stronger page, and it offers the cheaper tiers that coaching-curious visitors may accept.

Strongest case against: The purchase sample is small, and visitors who seek out /plus are warmer than visitors pushed to it, so the rate will fall as traffic grows.

5/10

Rebuild or retire the Unique Traits coaching CTA

42 clicks from 5,225 finishers (0.8%) and no traceable sale, despite personalised copy shown before the report. Restore its tracking tags first; then test it after the report instead of before it.

Strongest case for: People who just read about their own difficulties are the natural coaching audience, so a better-placed version could work.

Strongest case against: It has had 12 weeks at the top of the page. The floating button on the same pages already does the job with five times the click rate.

5/10

Find the owner of the Cognitive Assessment ad campaign

Google Ads campaign 20999008925 sent 2,465 paid clicks to the Cognitive Assessment page from August 2 to September 4: 123 checkouts, 0 sales. It is not in the Ads account this project can read, and GA4 is not linked to it.

Strongest case for: Somebody paid for about 2,500 clicks that sold nothing. Knowing who and how much prevents a repeat, and the campaign should not be restarted against this page.

Strongest case against: It stopped on its own in early September, so there is no money to save today.

How this was measured, and how far to trust it

  • Sales are exact. Subscriptions, PDF and Cognitive Assessment payments come from Stripe, with team tests and health checks removed. Three independent counts agree: completed checkouts and paid subscriptions (41 and 41), PDF checkouts and PDF charges (90 and 90), Cognitive checkouts and charges (32 and 32).
  • Sign-up clicks are a floor. They are runs of the GuidedTrack sign-up program; a returning visitor continues an old run, so some clicks are not counted.
  • Visitor counts come from GA4 and miss people who block analytics. Rates built from two GA4 numbers are less affected than the raw counts.
  • Tying a purchase to a prompt. GA4 fires an event on the CT+ thank-you page. 19 such events were recorded for 41 purchases; 14 matched a Stripe subscription by time. For each, I looked for views of /coaching and /plus in the previous 3 days from the same city, device, browser, system and screen size, and read the tag on the link that opened the page. Swapping buyers at random gives a false match about 4.4% of the time at most, so the pattern is real, but 14 purchases is a small sample and "clicked before buying" is not "bought because of".
  • Not measured: which page each sign-up click came from, what happens after the grid's PDF card, and the grid's exact number of views (finishers are used instead).
