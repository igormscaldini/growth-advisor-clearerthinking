## Version E: what $12 a month does

**🐔 Want to be kinder to animals? Here is what $12 a month does.**

By FarmKind's estimate, [$12 a month spares about one animal a day from suffering](https://www.farmkind.giving/pledge-kindness?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking), around 390 a year, when it goes to the charities they recommend. A small gift reaches that many because of scale: [about 112 billion farmed animals are alive at any time, more than 130 times the number of pet dogs and cats](https://www.farmkind.giving/how-to-help-animals?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking), and many of them spend their whole lives in cages or crates. That is why FarmKind's advice is that where you give matters more than how much.

FarmKind exists so that caring about animals can become something concrete without a research project. It picks six charities only from [Animal Charity Evaluators' recommended list](https://farmkind.giving/about-us/what?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking#selection-process): groups that get food companies to commit to taking hens out of cages ([$10 to The Humane League protects about eight hens](https://farmkind.giving/?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking)), improve conditions for fish and shrimp, and fund kinder alternatives. One gift to the Impact Fund is split across all six, and FarmKind is grant-funded and takes no cut; by card, the only deduction is the processing fee, and a bank transfer sends every dollar.

It is a way to act on your kindness toward animals every month, without changing anything else about your life.

Become an animal supporter: [set up a monthly gift through FarmKind](https://farmkind.giving/donate?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking), from $12 a month, or give once.

## Version F: a way to act on it

**🐷 If you care about animals, here is a way to act on it that keeps working all year.**

Caring about animals and reducing their suffering in a measurable way are two different things. The second one needs answers to practical questions: which charity, how much, and whether it works. FarmKind did that homework. It picks six charities only from [Animal Charity Evaluators' recommended list](https://farmkind.giving/about-us/what?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking#selection-process), and their work has a record you can check: the share of U.S. hens kept out of cages went from [about 4% to around 45% in a decade](https://iowaegg.org/egg-research/), after campaigns that won cage-free commitments from [more than 300 major companies](https://www.farmkind.giving/how-to-help-animals?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking), and the same groups now work on better conditions for fish and shrimp.

A monthly gift is the part you can do, and it is one decision you make once. By FarmKind's estimate, [$12 a month spares about one animal a day from suffering](https://www.farmkind.giving/pledge-kindness?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=clearer_thinking), around 390 a year, and one gift to the Impact Fund is split across all six charities. FarmKind is grant-funded and takes no cut; by card, the only deduction is the processing fee, and a bank transfer sends every dollar.

That is what being kind to animals can look like in practice: a few minutes once, and a measurable difference every month after.
