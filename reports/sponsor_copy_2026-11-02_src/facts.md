# Fact sheet behind the copy (sources checked 2026-11-02)

## Giving What We Can (givingwhatwecan.org)
- 10% Pledge: "a public commitment to give at least 10% of income to the organisations you believe can most effectively use it to improve the lives of others"; "Give a minimum of 10% of your income, until you retire". Pledgers report their donations each year.
- Trial Pledge: "Give a minimum of __% of your income for __ years", donor picks the percentage, 6 months to 5 years; nav blurb "Start with 1% for a year". 1,766 trial pledgers shown.
- 11,397 10% Pledgers from 121 countries (goal 12,000); "$603 million donated by our community"; "$396 million donated through 10% and Trial Pledges"; "17+ years of research and recommendations".
- "Earning ~$65,000/year post-tax puts you in the top 1% globally" (home page); pledge page: ~47,000 USD post-tax is the richest 2%.
- "Your $ can go 100x further": "Some charities are significantly more impactful than others"; the 100x is an illustrative comparison on the home page.
- Best charities 2026 page lists nine entries (six global health, two animal welfare, one global catastrophic risks fund): Against Malaria Foundation "$6 buys a net for 2 people for 2 years"; Malaria Consortium "$7 protects a child through the malaria season"; Helen Keller Intl "$2 provides a year's worth of Vitamin A" (a preventable deficiency behind many child deaths); New Incentives "$146 provides a child with all routine vaccinations"; GiveWell funds; EA Funds animal welfare "$10 can protect dozens or hundreds of farmed and aquatic animals"; ACE movement grants; a global catastrophic risks fund. Background: "~600,000" malaria deaths a year; "~14,000 children every day" die from preventable and curable diseases. "We base our recommendations on the research of independent, impact-focused charity evaluators whose research methodology we've rigorously assessed." Funds allocate by evaluator research.
- Max Klijnstra (Chief Growth Officer) booked four weeks on 2026-10-30 with these angles: Nov 19 pledge + drowning child; Dec 8 "some charities do 100x more good, how do you find them"; Dec 22 charity ask for the last week of the year; Dec 29 pledge, "end the year strong and set yourself up to live your morals next year". He asked that the pledge weeks say Igor took the pledge this year and is glad he did. Spencer Greenberg is not a pledger. Igor took the 10% Pledge in spring 2026 after a one-year Trial Pledge.
- 2026-11-02 counters on the site are live (goal 12,000 pledgers); re-check before each send.
- Peter Singer's drowning child thought experiment: "Famine, Affluence, and Morality", 1972.

## FarmKind (farmkind.giving)
- "Together, we can make farming kinder"; "The problem isn't you. It's factory farming."; the calculator is "like carbon offsetting, but for your plate!"; "balance your meals with your ideals"; "Where you donate can make a bigger difference than how much you donate".
- Compassion Calculator: choose your diet (advanced: amounts of each food); combines average-diet data (US, CA, UK, IE, AU, NZ) with charities' estimated cost to help different animals; donation split among recommended charities in proportion to your diet; modelled conservatively.
- Six recommended charities: The Humane League (its main program is corporate campaigns pressing food companies to go cage-free, thehumaneleague.org), Sinergia Animal, Good Food Institute (alternative proteins), Dansk Vegetarisk Forening, Fish Welfare Initiative, Shrimp Welfare Project. The GWWC animal welfare entry describes the field as "corporate advocacy and alternative protein research". Impact Fund = all six ("think of it like an investment portfolio for ending factory farming"). Charities are chosen only from Animal Charity Evaluators' recommended list by expert grantmakers.
- "Just $23/month to effective charities can do as much good as going completely vegan"; "Donating $10 to THL protects 8 hens"; a shelter spends ~$3,000 a year on one dog and that amount "can help more than 6000 animals suffering on factory farms".
- About page: FarmKind "describes itself as a non-profit donation platform"; "We connect compassionate people with impactful charities that are fixing factory farming"; "We've done the homework for you".
- "when you give through FarmKind, we don't take a cut of your donation"; grant-funded; no fees (payment processor fees may apply; optional tip to Every.org can be set to 0%).
- New Incentives (GWWC list, newincentives.org): cash incentives in northern Nigeria "at government clinics after children receive vaccinations"; 2.3 million infants enrolled; the GWWC entry says it "supports routine childhood immunization" and costs "$146 provides a child with all routine vaccinations".
- Donate page (farmkind.giving/donate, "Start helping farm animals today"): offers the Impact Fund and a Climate Fund; FarmKind takes no cut and charges no fees, Every.org charges no fees, card or PayPal processing fees are the only deduction, bank transfers and DAF payments have 0% fees; the form itself is at /quick-donation.
- Context stats on the site: ~112 billion farmed animals alive at any time; 99% of US farm animals live on factory farms; only 3% of animal-related donations go to farmed animals.
- Polina Povarova booked the week of December 1 (newsletter box + 30 to 60 second podcast mid-roll, $18 CPM, ~$3,600) and said "We'll ask directly for donations." She worries that donations from readers already into effective altruism are not counterfactual, so the copy should win readers outside that circle.

## Clearer Thinking house rules
- No em dashes anywhere. Evidence first, no hype, non-partisan, teaches how to think not what to think. Readers: lifelong learners (80%), science enthusiasts, 34% rationalists, ~25% effective altruists, 45% want "a greater positive impact on the world", their blocker is execution (procrastination, follow-through), they distrust hype and want sources.
- Sponsored box format: emoji at the start of a bold headline, three or four short paragraphs with the sponsor's numbers, a closing line that carries the calls to action as links. Host reads: Spencer in first person, honest about his own relationship to the sponsor, URL spoken out at the end.
