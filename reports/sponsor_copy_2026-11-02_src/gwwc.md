## Week of November 19: the pledge, by way of the pond

### Newsletter box

🌊 **You would ruin your shoes to save a drowning child. Would you give $7?**

Peter Singer's thought experiment is more than fifty years old and still uncomfortable. You walk past a shallow pond where a small child is drowning. Wading in would wreck your expensive shoes. Everyone agrees you wade in. Then the question: why does the answer change when the child is far away and the cost is $7, which is what Malaria Consortium needs to protect one child through a malaria season?

Most of us nod along and then give about what we gave last year. That is not a reasoning problem. It is a commitment problem, the same one behind every unused gym membership. Giving What We Can's answer is the 🔸10% Pledge: a public commitment to give at least 10% of your income to the charities that do the most good per dollar. 11,397 people in 121 countries have taken it, and the community has donated more than $600 million.

## Week of December 8: finding the charities that do 100 times more

### Newsletter box

🔍 **Most people choose a charity the way they choose a restaurant. Here is the alternative.**

A friend mentions it, the website looks trustworthy, the cause feels right. That works for dinner. For charities it leaves most of the value on the table, because the gap between a typical charity and the best ones is not 10% or 50%. By the estimates Giving What We Can relies on, the best can do around 100 times more good per dollar.

Finding them is a research job, so Giving What We Can did it. Their 2026 list rests on independent evaluators (GiveWell among them) whose methods they audit before trusting. The results are concrete: $6 buys a malaria net that protects two people for two years. $7 protects a child through a malaria season. $2 supplies a year of vitamin A, which prevents a deficiency that still kills children. If you would rather not pick, their cause-area funds split your gift across the top options and shift it as the evidence changes.

Giving What We Can has spent 17 years on this one question. The list takes ten minutes to read, and it will probably change where your money goes.

## Week of December 29: the resolution that holds

### Newsletter box

🔸 **A New Year's resolution with an 11,000-person enforcement mechanism**

Resolutions fail quietly: nobody notices when you stop. The 🔸10% Pledge is built differently. You commit in public to giving at least 10% of your income to the charities that do the most good per dollar, you join 11,397 people in 121 countries who made the same commitment, and each year you report what you gave. That structure, not willpower, is why the community has donated more than $600 million.

It also answers the objection most of us raise in December: "I will give more when I earn more." If you earn about $65,000 a year after tax, you are already in the richest 1% of people alive. The pledge takes the decision out of each December and makes it a standing one.
