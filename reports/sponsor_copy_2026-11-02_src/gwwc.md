## Week of November 19: the pledge, by way of the pond

### Newsletter box

🌊 **You would ruin your shoes to save a drowning child. $7 protects one from malaria for a season.**

Peter Singer's thought experiment is more than fifty years old and still uncomfortable. You walk past a shallow pond where a small child is drowning. Wading in would wreck your expensive shoes. Everyone agrees you wade in. Then the question: why does the answer change when the child is far away and the cost is $7, which is what Malaria Consortium needs to protect one child through a malaria season?

Most people accept the argument and change nothing, which suggests the gap is in follow-through, not in the logic. Giving What We Can built the 🔸10% Pledge for that gap: a public commitment to give at least 10% of your income to the organisations you believe can most effectively use it to improve the lives of others. 11,397 people in 121 countries have taken it, and the community has donated more than $600 million.

Igor, who runs growth at Clearer Thinking, took it this spring after a one-year Trial Pledge, and is glad he did. ## Week of December 8: finding the charities that do 100 times more

### Newsletter box

🔍 **Most people pick a charity the way they pick a restaurant. The difference in results can be 100 to 1.**

A friend mentions it, the website looks trustworthy, the cause feels right. That works for dinner. For charities it leaves most of the value on the table, because by the comparisons Giving What We Can uses, the best charities can do around 100 times more good per dollar than a typical one.

Finding them is a research job, so Giving What We Can did it. Their 2026 list rests on independent, impact-focused evaluators (GiveWell among them) whose methodology they assess before relying on it. The results are concrete: $6 buys a malaria net that protects two people for two years. $7 protects a child through a malaria season. $2 supplies a year of vitamin A, which prevents a deficiency behind many child deaths. If you would rather not pick, their cause-area funds split your gift across the top options according to the evaluators' research.

Giving What We Can has spent 17 years on this one question. The list takes ten minutes to read, and it will probably change where your money goes.

## Week of December 29: the resolution that holds

### Newsletter box

🔸 **A New Year's resolution with 11,397 witnesses**

Resolutions fail quietly: nobody notices when you stop. The 10% Pledge is built differently. You commit in public to giving at least 10% of your income to the organisations you believe can most effectively use it, you join 11,397 people in 121 countries who made the same commitment, and each year you report what you gave. The community has donated more than $600 million so far, and the yearly report is the part most resolutions lack.

It also answers a familiar December objection: "I will give more when I earn more." If you earn about $65,000 a year after tax, you are already in the richest 1% of people alive. The pledge takes the decision out of each December and makes it a standing one.
