Version C: fifty animals (from the carousel)

Newsletter box

🐔 Can $26 help 50 animals?

By FarmKind's estimate, yes: 29 chickens, 11 pigs, 10 fish, and about a third of a cow. That is what $26 does when it goes to the charities FarmKind recommends, which it picks only from Animal Charity Evaluators' top-rated list. The third of a cow is not a typo. It is what an honest estimate looks like when one gift is spread across campaigns that help different animals at very different costs.

The need is as large as the number is small. 99% of farm animals in the U.S. live on factory farms, most of them in cages and crates for their whole lives, and only 3% of animal-charity donations go to farmed animals. The charities on FarmKind's list work where the leverage is: they get food companies to commit to phasing out cages, press governments to back a kinder food system, and help companies make food without factory farming.

The results are measurable. U.S. egg production has gone from about 4% cage-free a decade ago to around 45% today, a shift The Humane League's corporate campaigns helped drive, and the Good Food Institute helped secure $390 million in European government funding for alternatives to factory farming. FarmKind is grant-funded and takes no cut: by card, 97.5 cents of every dollar reach the charities and the rest is the card fee; a bank transfer sends all of it.

Will you help 50 animals today? Give $26 through FarmKind and it reaches all six charities at once.

Version D: the conflict (from the Compassion Calculator video)

Newsletter box

🐷 You can care about animals and still eat meat. FarmKind did the arithmetic that reconciles the two.

Plenty of people care about animal suffering, keep eating meat, and feel a little conflicted about it. FarmKind's answer is not a diet. It works the way carbon offsetting works for a flight: you keep the trip and pay for something that cancels out the harm. Their Compassion Calculator takes your diet, how much meat, dairy, and eggs you actually eat, and estimates the monthly donation that would do as much good for farm animals as giving them up. For a typical diet, FarmKind's figure is about $23 a month, modeled conservatively by their own account.

The money goes to charities FarmKind picks only from Animal Charity Evaluators' recommended list. By FarmKind's account, they have freed millions of animals from cages and from painful procedures done without anesthetic, and they fund the food technologies that could cut our dependence on factory farming. All six publish what they do, so you can check them yourself.

FarmKind is grant-funded and takes no cut; the only deductions are card or PayPal processing fees, and a bank transfer has none.

Put your diet into the calculator, see your number, and decide. Try the Compassion Calculator.
