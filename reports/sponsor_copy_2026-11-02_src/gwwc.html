Week of November 19: the pledge, by way of the pond

Newsletter box

🪢 You already agree with the drowning child. The pledge is for what happens after you agree.

Peter Singer's pond has been doing its job for fifty years: you would ruin your shoes to pull a child out of the water, so distance should not change what you owe a child you cannot see. Most of us concede the point, then give what we gave last year.

Ulysses knew this about himself. He wanted to hear the Sirens, knew he would not resist, and had himself tied to the mast before the song began. Giving What We Can's 10% Pledge is a mast: a public commitment to give at least 10% of your income to the organisations you believe can use it best, made once, alongside more than 11,000 people in 121 countries who made the same one. Pledgers have given more than $390 million so far.

The ropes are not tight. Igor from our team took the pledge this spring after a year on a Trial Pledge, which is any percentage, even 1%, for as little as six months. The mast comes in sizes.

Take the 10% Pledge, or start with a Trial Pledge.

Week of December 8: the 100 to 1 spread

Newsletter box

🔍 The most expensive decision you'll make this December is where your donation goes, not how much.

Charities don't come with price tags, so most of us choose one the way we'd choose a restaurant: a friend mentioned it, the website looked honest, the cause felt right. Giving What We Can's comparison puts the spread between a typical charity and the best ones at around 100 to 1. No restaurant is a hundred times better than the one next door. Some charities are.

Giving What We Can does not rate charities itself. It picks the evaluators (GiveWell among them), checks their methods, and publishes a short list built on their research: nine charities and funds for 2026. The entries read like a menu with real prices. $6 buys a malaria net that covers two people for two years. $2 is a year of vitamin A for a child. $146 is a child's full set of routine vaccinations.

If choosing is the part you keep postponing, the cause-area funds choose for you, splitting one gift across the top charities in a cause according to the evaluators' research.

See the best charities to donate to in 2026 or give through a fund.

Week of December 22: the procrastinator's guide

Newsletter box

⏳ The procrastinator's guide to giving: three choices, ten minutes, done before midnight.

If you are reading this with a few days left in the year and no charity picked, you are in good company, and Giving What We Can built a shortcut for exactly this position. Three choices.

One: a cause. Global health, animal welfare, or global catastrophic risks. Two: a fund in that cause. Each one takes a single gift and spreads it across the top charities in the field, following the research of independent evaluators that Giving What We Can vets first. Three: an amount. For scale, $146 covers a child's full set of routine vaccinations through New Incentives, which pays families cash incentives at government clinics in northern Nigeria when their children are vaccinated, and around 14,000 children die every day of diseases we know how to prevent or cure.

That is the whole procedure. No spreadsheet, no shortlist, no "I'll look into it in January", which is how last year's gift went to whoever emailed last.

Week of December 29: pre-register your giving

Newsletter box

🔸 Pre-register your giving.

Scientists pre-register a study so they can't quietly change the plan once the results come in. The 10% Pledge does that for generosity. You decide in advance, in public, that at least 10% of your income goes to the organisations you believe can use it best. Then each year you report what you actually gave. There is no quiet February exit, which is how most New Year's resolutions end.

The usual objection is "I'll give more when I earn more". Giving What We Can's calculator has a reply: earn about $65,000 a year after tax and you are already in the richest 1% of people alive. The question was never whether you can afford it.

More than 11,000 people in 121 countries have pre-registered this way, including Igor from our team, who took the pledge this spring after a year on a Trial Pledge and is glad he did. If 10% is too much for 2027, the Trial Pledge takes any percentage, even 1%, for six months to five years. Register the plan before the year starts.

Take the 10% Pledge, or start with a Trial Pledge.
