Career Navigation Survey: what 143 readers told us

GuidedTrack program 38720 · runs from Sep 1 to Sep 7, 2026 · report generated 2026-09-07 10:17am

The survey asked newsletter readers where they are with their career, what is driving a change, what they weigh when choosing a path, what would help most, and what stands in their way. This report covers every non-test run exported from GuidedTrack.

The short version

  • Most respondents are mid-change. 104 of 143 (73%) are actively changing, seriously considering it, or thinking about it. Only 18 say they are happy where they are.
  • They want a diagnosis first, then a plan. A self-assessment that maps strengths and values to careers was the top pick (41%), followed by a step-by-step change program (25%). The live workshop itself came third at 14%.
  • Fit and meaning beat money when weighing a path. 70% chose fit with their strengths and personality and 70% chose meaning, against 62% for salary. Reasons for leaving are flatter: no single driver passes 43%.
  • The biggest obstacle is choosing. 29 of 96 open answers (30%) are some version of "I don't know which path fits me". Money and time, age and credibility, confidence, and the AI-shaped job market each take another 13 to 17%.
  • This is an experienced, highly educated audience. 50% have more than ten years of experience and 43% hold a master's or doctorate. Tech, public sector, research, education, and healthcare lead.
  • Demand for the workshop is real. 65 of the 111 people who finished (59%) left an email to be alerted when it is scheduled, rising to 71% among active changers.

What this means for the workshop

  1. Make the core of the workshop a self-assessment, then a plan. Two thirds of respondents asked for exactly that pairing. A live session that walks people through mapping strengths and values to concrete career options, and leaves them with a step-by-step plan, serves both preferences at once.
  2. Build the agenda from the obstacle themes. A narrowing exercise for the third who cannot choose; a transition budget (money and time) for the 15% blocked by cost; a segment on transferable skills and age for the 15% who fear not being taken seriously; a short evidence-based look at which skills hold up against AI for the 13% worried about the market; and a commitment step for the 17% held back by fear or inertia.
  3. Pitch it to experienced professionals, not graduates. Half the audience has more than ten years of experience and nearly half holds a graduate degree. Examples and case studies should be mid-career pivots, with first-career choosers as a secondary track.
  4. Invite the 65 who left an email first. They are the warmest segment, concentrated among active changers. A short reply that quotes the survey findings back to them would close the loop the survey promised.
  5. Fix the survey's own front door before reusing it. A third of openers left on the intro page, and finishers took a median of 3.6 minutes against a promised two. Put the first question on the landing page, drop the Start button, and say "about four minutes".

Method and caveats

  • Source: the GuidedTrack CSV export for program 38720, pulled 2026-09-07 10:17am. GuidedTrack excludes runs marked as tests. One further run whose free-text answers were "qq" was removed as a manual test. 1 row removed in total.
  • Three runs came from earlier program versions (7 and 9) before the September 4 send; they are included because their questions were unchanged.
  • Bases differ by question because routing skips questions for some stages, and because people dropped out along the way. Every chart states its own n.
  • Multi-select questions report the share of respondents who ticked each option, so percentages sum to more than 100.
  • Respondents are self-selected newsletter readers who clicked a survey link, not a sample of the newsletter, let alone of the general public. Treat the shares as directional.
  • Obstacle themes were coded by hand, one theme per answer; borderline answers exist and a different coder would move a handful between "which path fits me", "confidence", and "practical how-to".
