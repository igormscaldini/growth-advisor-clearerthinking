Do they email less when you stop opening?

Marketing Brew, McKinsey & Company and theSkimm, from the full headers of every email they sent to Igor's Gmail (967 + 2,265 + 3,222 messages) · Generated 2026-11-13 08:09am

Answer: none of the three throttles you. Igor has opened 1 to 13% of these emails in most years, and all three kept their full cadence through no-open stretches of 181 days (Marketing Brew), 439 days (McKinsey) and 1,083 days (theSkimm). What not opening does trigger is extra email: Marketing Brew sent one "confirm your subscription" message, McKinsey sent five "Are we sending you the right email?" messages, and Marketing Brew sends "FW:" resends to people who skipped a promo. On sender domains, theSkimm changed ESP once (2018) and kept its old From subdomain. McKinsey only rotated a DKIM key. Marketing Brew changed its From address and moved promos onto a separate subdomain on another ESP.

a. Volume while not opening

Method: each month's email count divided by that sender's median for the same calendar month across all years. That removes seasonal swings like summer lulls and year-end recaps. Months are grouped by how long it had been since Igor last opened one. 1.00 = a typical month. If they throttled, the ratio would fall as the gap grows.

  • McKinsey: flat, within plus or minus 10% with no trend. During the longest no-open stretch (Jan 23, 2021 to Apr 8, 2022: 439 days) they sent 396 emails at the normal rate. There's one ambiguous episode. The topic alerts fell to 7 to 13 a month in Feb to May 2026, during a no-open stretch, and rose to 20 in June, right after Igor opened one on May 25. But the 2021 stretch, which was longer, caused no drop, and most of the decline is the newsletter lineup changing (Daily Read, Shortlist and Weekend Read were retired). So this is not evidence of a rule.
  • theSkimm: the falling ratio is a schedule artifact, not throttling. The "over a year" months are almost all 2019 to 2022, when the Daily Skimm ran on weekdays only, and in 2022 it went to 7 days a week. Since 2023 Igor has opened about 1% of issues, and the Daily Skimm arrived on 1,389 of 1,411 days. The 22 missing days are holidays (Memorial Day, July 4, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's, MLK Day), Sundays in January 2023 before the Sunday edition existed, and 2 unexplained days (Dec 8, 2025 and Jun 30, 2026).
  • Marketing Brew (from the previous analysis): a scheduled issue arrived on 98 to 100% of send days at every length of no-open stretch.

Pattern across the three: all of them keep the editorial From domain stable for years, and none of them hops domains. When they change, they change the ESP and DKIM underneath and leave the address readers see alone (theSkimm still sends from a morning7. subdomain eight years after leaving the ESP it was built for). Two of the three separate promotional mail onto another address or subdomain.

Caveats. One inbox, so these are the rules that applied to Igor, not each sender's full policy. "Opened" means Gmail's read flag. If this account is read in Apple Mail, those apps load images automatically, so the senders may have recorded opens Igor never made, and none of them would have treated him as inactive. Igor reads almost none of these, so a sender that sunsets only after, say, 2 years of zero opens and zero clicks could still exist. The data rules out any rule shorter than 181 days (Marketing Brew), 439 days (McKinsey) or 1,083 days (theSkimm) while the read flag is the signal.
