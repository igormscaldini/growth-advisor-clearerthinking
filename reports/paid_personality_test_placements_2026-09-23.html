Paid placements for the Ultimate Personality Test

Which "best personality test" lists on high-authority domains can be bought into, what they cost, and what I would do instead. 2026-09-23 10:20am. Authority and traffic figures are Ahrefs (DR = Domain Rating, traffic = estimated monthly organic visits, US-weighted). Prices are quoted from each site or from the named third-party source and were not negotiated.

The short version. None of the lists that actually rank for "best personality tests" sell inclusion (Forbes Health, Verywell Mind, Psychology Today, Zapier, BetterUp, Choosing Therapy). Money buys three things: (1) sponsored articles on DR 70 to 79 lifestyle and self-improvement sites (Good Men Project from $130, YourTango, Thought Catalog, Elephant Journal from $5,000, Brainz), where you supply a "best personality tests" article that includes ours; (2) a DR 91 to 94 byline through a paid council seat (Forbes, Entrepreneur, Newsweek, Fast Company, about $2,500 to $3,300 a year) where Spencer writes the list himself and editors cap self-promotion; (3) grey-market resold placements (Psychreg via PeoplePerHour) that I would not buy.

The catch. Paid links must carry rel="sponsored" or nofollow under Google's link-spam policy, and Google's site reputation abuse policy (live since May 2024, tightened Nov 2024) has already deindexed paid third-party sections on Forbes, USA Today and Newsweek. So the value you are buying is referral traffic, brand mentions and AI-answer citations, not link equity. The test is already at DR 63 and ranks #10 for "personality tests" on its own.

The bigger opportunity. The SERP for "best personality tests" is weak: a DR 44 blog ranks #4, a DR 50 blog ranks #8, a DR 33 site ranks #10. Clearer Thinking (DR 63) publishing its own honest comparison of free personality tests is the cheapest way to own that list. Details in section 5.

4. Checked and ruled out

  • Addicted2Success (DR 70): their contact page states "We do not offer sponsored posts, link placements, paid opportunities or features for companies, brands or entrepreneurs."
  • Thrive Global (DR 84): contributor guidelines forbid posts a third party paid for and any product links.
  • Lifehack (DR 85): guest writing is unpaid and promotional content is banned; link resellers claim to sell Lifehack placements, which I could not verify and would treat as grey market.
  • UpJourney (DR 71): affiliate links and display ads only; its expert roundups are free to join, but organic traffic has collapsed to about 1,000 visits a month.
  • Success.com (DR 80), PickTheBrain (DR 70), mindbodygreen (DR 85): sell display and branded-content campaigns through media kits (CPM-based), not article inclusion. Possible but priced like ad campaigns.
  • Indian news sites that sell "best X" listicles (Times of India DR 92, Hindustan Times DR 89, Outlook India DR 86, Deccan Herald DR 83, Mid-day DR 80): no personality-test listicles found on the two I could crawl, and this is precisely the paid-third-party pattern Google's site reputation abuse policy targets. Skip.
  • Well+Good (DR 83): Ahrefs shows about 45 organic visits a month; the site is effectively dead.

5. What I would do instead, or first

  1. Publish Clearer Thinking's own "best free personality tests, compared" page. The organic top 10 for "best personality tests" contains a DR 44 vendor blog at #4, a DR 50 solo blog at #8 and a DR 33 site at #10 for "best free personality tests"; Clearer Thinking is DR 63 and the test already ranks #10 for "personality tests" and #14 for "types of personality tests". An honest comparison (Big Five, 16 types, Enneagram, DISC, HEXACO, with validity notes and links to the free versions) is the kind of page Spencer can write credibly, and it makes the test the natural first entry.
  2. Pitch the editorial lists with one paragraph. Angle: free, no email required, three frameworks in one report (Big Five, Jungian 16 types, Enneagram), 20,000+ runs, cited and linked by Scientific American (DR 91) and 80,000 Hours (DR 76). Targets: Forbes Health's expert panel, Verywell Mind, Choosing Therapy, Inside Out Mastery, Deeper Signals.
  3. Run one cheap paid test before spending more. Good Men Project at $130 and a direct sponsored-rate ask to Psychreg. Use a tracked link (?src=gmp, ?src=psychreg) and judge on completed runs, not on the link.
  4. If you want a DR 90+ byline, buy one council seat for Spencer, not several. Entrepreneur ($3,000) or Forbes Coaches Council are the closest audience fits.
  5. Skip the grey market (PeoplePerHour and marketplace resellers such as Collaborator, Adsy, WhitePress). Marketplaces add 30% to 70% on top of the publisher's price, and the placements are the ones most likely to be nofollowed or removed.
