5. What to create next

Scored 1 to 10. Ordered by score. Ideas 1, 3 and 8 are copy or re-uploads and take an hour; 2 and 4 are new images (use the ct-ad-designer skill); 7 needs an editor.

9

Say 'Free' and '8 minutes' in the text

Add three headlines ('Free Rationality Test', 'Rationality Test In 8 Minutes', 'Take the Rationality Test Now'), one description ('Rationality Test: discover your reasoning style and your overall rationality score. Free.') and two long headlines ('How Rational Are You, Really? Free 8-minute test: your score + your reasoning style'; 'Which of 16 reasoning styles is yours? Take the free rationality test and find out'). The slots come from the removals above.

For: None of the 15 live headlines says 'free' and none states the length. In the sister UPT campaign the two best headlines are exactly these two ideas: 'Free Personality Test' (4,903 conversions, 8.7% CTR) and 'Personality Test In 15 Minutes' (2,656 conversions, 12% CTR). The removed auto headline 'Take the Rationality Test Now' did $0.65 per email at 25.7% Search CTR before it was dropped. Zero production cost.

Against: Search CTR is already 20 to 30%, so the upside is in conversion per click and in Google choosing the ad more often, not in CTR. 'Free' can pull in lower-intent clickers; watch cost per email, not clicks.

8

Grow the line-art family (no text overlay)

Three new landscape and three new square images in the same light, blue line-art style as the current winner: a head with scales inside, two heads in dialogue, a head with a maze that resolves into a straight line, a 4x4 grid of 16 small heads (the 16 reasoning styles). No overlay text, no button, logo optional. Use the ct-ad-designer skill so the brand description is consistent.

For: The one line-art landscape is the best asset in the campaign ($0.31 per email, 3.86% Display CTR, 3,965 emails) and the three line-art squares are the three best squares. There is exactly one landscape in that style, so Google has nothing to rotate to. The dark 'Test Your Rationality / FREE QUIZ' photos are the opposite case: ten variations, eight of which never got past 100k impressions or 80 emails.

Against: It is one image's track record, so some of its edge may be the specific composition rather than the style. Display traffic at $0.03 a click needs a subscriber-quality check (open rate of the rationality_test_pmax cohort in beehiiv) before scaling it further.

7

Re-test UPT's animal images in this group

Upload the four UPT winners (cat in sunglasses, dog on skateboard, pig in cubicle, chicken playing chess) into the live asset group, landscape + square.

For: Free: the files already exist in the account. In UPT they are the best images by far (dog on skateboard: 208 conversions at 8.2% CTR; cat: 126 conversions on $88). Here they only ever sat in the removed asset group and got 100 to 200 impressions each, so they have never had a fair test against the rationality text.

Against: A pig in a cubicle has nothing to do with rationality; the incongruity that stops the scroll may also lower post-click completion. Run it as a test, and judge on cost per email, not CTR.

7

A 'result preview' image

One landscape and one square showing what the test gives you: a score dial and a reasoning-style card, in the line-art style, with the real name of one of the 16 styles (take it from the test, do not invent one). This also becomes the image Google pairs with the Search ads.

For: The descriptions that promise a concrete deliverable are the ones that convert ('Free results', 'tailored advice'); no image currently shows the deliverable. The March 2026 audience survey asked for proof that tools produce something real. On Search, the image slot is currently a decorative head.

Against: Google can flag results-style imagery as misleading if it looks like a real score. It needs one accurate, non-fabricated style name and score format from the test itself.

6

Headlines that match what people actually search

'Critical Thinking Test' (22 chars) and 'How Rational Am I? Find Out' (27 chars).

For: September search categories were 'iq test', 'critical thinking test', 'psychology test', 'know yourself test', 'how rational am i'. 'Boost Critical Thinking Skills' already converts (30 emails at $1.03) despite being benefit-framed; a plain noun-phrase headline usually beats it on Search.

Against: 'IQ test' seekers convert badly (11 clicks, 1 conversion on 'iq test free') and the test is not an IQ test, so do not chase that query. Gains are limited by the 15-headline cap.

6

Lean into '16 reasoning styles'

Headline 'Which of 16 Reasoning Styles?' (29 chars), plus the long headline in idea 1, plus the 4x4 grid image in idea 2.

For: The test's own description on the CT site leads with the 16 styles. UPT's 'Discover Your Personality From Our 16 Types' did 3,541 conversions at 15.3% CTR, and 'Discover Your Thinking Type' is the best headline here in September. People like being typed.

Against: It pushes the test toward 'just another personality test', which 18% of cold UPT respondents said about CT's personality test. The rationality score is the real differentiator; keep it in the same ad.

5

A 16:9 cut of the UGC videos

Re-cut UGC 1 and UGC 2 to landscape 16:9, 15 to 20 seconds, captions on, ending on the URL.

For: All five current videos are vertical and together got 8,400 impressions and $21 in a year; PMax reaches YouTube here only through the image assets (5.3M impressions). Landscape unlocks in-stream, which vertical cannot serve.

Against: UPT's videos got 65k impressions and 1 conversion. The audience survey says video discourages consumption. Real production cost for a placement type with no evidence it converts for CT.

4

Campaign-level callouts that fit the offer

Replace the account-level callouts that ride along with these Search ads ('Send A Message', 'Read Blog', 'Browse Tools') with campaign-level ones: 'Free', '8 minutes', '16 reasoning styles', 'Personalized report'.

For: The current callouts are generic site navigation and appear in the served text combinations. Specific ones add the proof points the headlines cannot fit.

Against: Callouts are a small CTR lever and they are not really 'creative'; low effort, low impact.

6. Caveats

  • Google removed asset performance labels ("Best / Good / Low") from the API in the current version, so every verdict here comes from metrics, not from Google's labels. The labels in the UI may disagree on the marginal calls; they will not disagree on the removals.
  • Asset-level conversions in Performance Max are attributed to every asset in the served combination, so the email counts across a table add up to more than the campaign total. Rates (CTR, $ per email) are comparable within a field type; counts across field types are not.
  • Cost per email before March 2026 is affected by the mobile Display traffic of that era (12k to 17k mobile sessions a month, 38 to 63 s average, 2.7% to 8.5% of sessions submitting an email). April 2026 on desktop is the cleaner benchmark: 36k sessions, 14.7% submitting.
  • Subscriber quality by source has not been checked. The next step, outside this audit's scope, is the open rate of beehiiv subscribers whose UTM Campaign contains 'rationality_test_pmax' against the 'upt_pmax' cohort (restrict both to 3+ emails received).
