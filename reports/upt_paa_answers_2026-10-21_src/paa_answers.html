Suggested section heading (H2): Questions people ask about personality tests

Is there a truly free personality test?

Yes. The Ultimate Personality Test is free, takes about 12 minutes, and reports your Big Five trait scores, your MBTI-style (Jungian) type and your Enneagram type in one sitting. Free is the norm for two of the three frameworks: there is no official Big Five or Enneagram test you have to pay for. The exception is the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator. The official MBTI is proprietary and paid (listed at $59.95 in August 2025), so every free "MBTI test" online, ours included, is an MBTI-style approximation rather than the official instrument. More on the three frameworks and their types.

Which is the most accurate personality test?

If accuracy means how well a result predicts real life, the Big Five wins. We gave 559 US adults tests built on the three most popular frameworks and used each one to predict 37 life outcomes, from life satisfaction to exercise habits. The Big Five predicted them about twice as accurately as a four-letter MBTI-style type, and the Enneagram came in between. A follow-up with roughly 3,500 test takers and 30 outcomes gave the same ranking. That is why the Ultimate Personality Test reports Big Five scores alongside your Jungian and Enneagram types. Read the full study, or our summary of it in Scientific American.

Is MBTI or Big 5 more accurate?

The Big Five, by a wide margin in our data, for two reasons. MBTI-style tests leave out neuroticism, and dropping that one trait from the Big Five cut its predictive accuracy by 22 percent in our study. They also sort you into one of two letters on each dimension, while our data show most traits form a bell curve with most people near the middle, so a letter throws away information that a score keeps. Combining Big Five and MBTI-style results predicted nothing that the Big Five alone did not. People do like MBTI-style reports more: 19 percent said the Big Five report did not make them feel good about their personality, against 10 percent for the MBTI-style report. The key findings in brief.

Has Myers-Briggs been debunked?

Not in the sense of being worthless, but it does not hold up as well as its popularity suggests. Peer-reviewed critiques point to weak test-retest reliability (many people get a different type when they retake it) and to the forced either/or letters. In our study an MBTI-style type predicted life outcomes about half as well as the Big Five, though far better than astrology, which predicted nothing at all. The four dimensions do track real traits: the extraversion, intuition and feeling scales line up with Big Five extraversion, openness and agreeableness. An MBTI-style type is a simplified and more flattering reading of traits the Big Five measures more precisely. What the research says about MBTI-style tests.

Are personality tests accurate?

Some are, and what matters is the framework, not the branding. Measured by how well results predicted 37 life outcomes for 559 US adults, Big Five scores did best, Enneagram types came next, MBTI-style types last, and astrological signs scored zero. Scores beat types every time: converting any framework from types to continuous scores raised its accuracy, and converting the Big Five into types lowered it. In our data personality also predicted happiness and life satisfaction better than IQ did. A good test tells you where you sit on each trait, not only which box you fall in. Personality versus IQ as a predictor.

What is my personality type?

The only way to know is to take a test, and the answer depends on the framework: a four-letter MBTI-style type, an Enneagram number from 1 to 9, or a Big Five profile of five trait scores. Use a test that shows your score on each trait rather than a label alone, because most people sit near the middle of each trait and a label hides how strong a lean is. The Ultimate Personality Test gives you all three in about 12 minutes, plus one of 16 Clearer Thinking types derived from test takers' answers rather than from theory.

How many personality types are there?

It depends on the model. MBTI-style tests have 16 types, built from four either/or letters. The Enneagram has 9. The Big Five has no types at all: it scores five traits on a continuous scale, so there are as many profiles as there are people. Our own system has 16 types, found by running a cluster analysis on test takers' answers so that people in a group answered alike and differently from the other groups; the names and descriptions were written afterwards to fit each cluster. In our data a 16-cluster type predicted life outcomes better than MBTI-style or Enneagram types, but not as well as Big Five scores. The full list of types in each system.

What is the rarest personality type?

There is no stable answer, because "rarest" depends on which test and which sample you count. Published rarity rankings come from the test publishers' own samples, and in letter-based systems a type's share changes with the sample and with where the test draws the line between two letters. Our data show that most traits form a bell curve, with most people close to the middle, so whether someone counts as a rare type can hinge on one or two answers. A score on each trait tells you more about yourself than a rarity ranking does. How traits are distributed in our study.

Am I an INFJ or INTJ?

The two types differ on one letter, Feeling versus Thinking: whether you make decisions from personal values or from objective analysis. If you keep landing on both, you are probably near the middle of that dimension, which is where most people sit in our data. A test that scores each dimension, instead of only assigning a letter, shows how strong your lean is. In our study the Thinking versus Feeling scale lined up with Big Five agreeableness, so your agreeableness score is a second reading on the same question. The Ultimate Personality Test reports both. What each MBTI-style letter means.

Is there an official MBTI test?

Yes. The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator is a proprietary assessment owned by the Myers & Briggs Foundation, sold through The Myers-Briggs Company (listed at $59.95 in August 2025) and administered by certified practitioners. Free online "MBTI tests", including the Jungian type in our test, are unofficial MBTI-style versions written to measure the same four dimensions. We checked ours against people's own results: among 118 test takers who knew their MBTI type, our scores matched the individual letters 72 percent of the time, about three of four letters per person. More on official and MBTI-style tests.

What is the Big Five model of personality?

The Big Five scores you on five broad traits: openness to experience, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism. It was built by factor analysis of thousands of personality-describing words, which found the five factors that explain most of the variation in how people describe themselves, and it has been replicated across languages and cultures, which is why academic psychologists prefer it. Unlike MBTI-style tests it includes neuroticism, the tendency toward negative emotion, which predicts outcomes such as life satisfaction. The Ultimate Personality Test reports your score on each of the five. The five traits explained.

What are the 9 types of the Enneagram?

The Enneagram describes nine interconnected types, each defined by a basic desire and a basic fear: Type 1 the Perfectionist, 2 the Helper, 3 the Achiever, 4 the Individualist, 5 the Investigator, 6 the Loyalist, 7 the Adventurer, 8 the Protector and 9 the Peacemaker. In our study an Enneagram type predicted life outcomes better than an MBTI-style type but less well than Big Five scores, and scoring people on all nine types instead of assigning one improved it further. The Ultimate Personality Test reports your Enneagram type together with your Big Five scores. The nine types, with each one's desire and fear.
