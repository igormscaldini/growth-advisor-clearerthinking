New subscribers by month, 2026

Without Ads and without the Who Attracts You study · January 1 to November 17, 2026 (UTC) · 380,834 beehiiv records read · generated 2026-11-18 04:08pm

Leaving out everyone who came from an ad or from the Who Attracts You study, Clearer Thinking gained about 79,400 new subscribers from January 1 to November 17, 2026, out of the 366,757 that beehiiv counted. Set the three Leaf batches and the August viral wave aside as well and a full month runs from 4,800 (July) to 9,900 (March, a tool launch), with a median of 6,500. April onward is an exact count from the records. January to March include an estimate, because the tools did not tag ad sign-ups in beehiiv before March 27.

Each row adds up: all new subscribers, minus the study, ads and the December backlog, equals kept. ≈ marks a figure that includes the ad estimate for January to March. November covers the 1st to the 17th. "Still subscribed today" is the record's status on November 18.

August is inflated by the viral wave. From Aug 3 to 14 the X/Twitter wave that launched the study also pushed the other tools (mostly the Ultimate Personality Test and the Unique Traits Test) to 5,624 sign-ups, about 3,400 above their usual 185 a day. On the record these are not study sign-ups: the first tool they finished was a different one and only 66 of them ever took the study, so they are kept. If you see that wave as the study's doing, read August as about 6,400, not 9,777. The September and October waves sent almost nobody to another tool first (about 400 and 200 above baseline; September's is mostly an unrelated spike on the Value of Your Time Calculator).

January holds two one-offs. 2,424 of its ≈10,634 are the Leaf student batch of Jan 16 (kept: neither an ad nor the study, but not organic either). And 2,824 people uploaded on Jan 1 at 01:15 UTC are left out, because an upload made 75 minutes into the year can only hold December 2025 sign-ups.

March's bump is the Unique Traits Test launch. Mar 23 to 26 brought 4,604 new subscribers in four days, 2,150 of them on that tool alone. It is real, non-ad, non-study growth, but it is a launch, not a run rate.

Tagged cpc: the visit that created the subscriber carried utm_medium=cpc (Google or Meta). A reader who signed up without an ad and clicked one later is kept. Partner ad campaigns: sign-ups on MailList Accelerator pages (BlueDot, ACE, FarmKind, HIP, Hive). They carry no UTM tag, but those pages are fed by paid campaigns (98% of their GA4 email submissions in January and February came from google/cpc), so I counted them as Ads. Add this column back if you see them differently. Estimated: before March 27 the tools wrote no UTM tags to beehiiv. GA4 recorded 4,272 email submissions in ad sessions on CT's own tools from January 1 to March 27. In the first weeks with tags (Mar 28 to Apr 30) each such event produced 0.82 new tagged subscribers, so that share is taken out. Later months ran lower (0.60 to 0.69); at those rates January to March would keep up to 914 more in total, so the figures shown are the cautious end.

How each subscriber was classified

  • Source. Every beehiiv subscription created from January 1 to November 17, 2026: 380,834 records, read one by one with their custom fields. Month = the UTC date the record entered beehiiv.
  • Invalid addresses are not subscribers. 14,077 records (3% to 5% a month) failed beehiiv's validation or hard-bounced. beehiiv leaves them out of its own growth numbers and so does this report.
  • From the study = the first tool the person finished was Who Attracts You, or they finished no tool and carry the study's dating-app opt-in. 264,370 people, most of them uploads of earlier participants in August. Someone who joined through another tool and took the study later is kept.
  • From Ads = the three columns in the table above.
  • How much the reading matters. Removing anyone who EVER touched the study or an ad, even long after signing up, would lower the kept figure by only 16 to 181 a month from April on (at most 2.3%).

What the records cannot show

  • January to March: ads are estimated, as described above. The kept figures for those three months could be up to 914 higher in total, not lower.
  • Batch timing. Until March 9 sign-ups were uploaded every three to five days, so a January to March figure can be off by a few days of sign-ups at each month boundary.
  • Ad clickers who sign up on a different tool. The cpc tag is written by the tool the ad lands on. Someone who clicks an ad and then leaves their email on another tool has no tag. GA4 bounds this: ad-session email submissions exceed tagged ad sign-ups by 179 to 1,333 a month from April on, and most of that gap is existing subscribers and repeat submissions, so the real leak is well below it.
  • The study before August. It was already live at a trickle (101 sign-ups in April, 79 in July), and those are removed. If it also ran in January to March its sign-ups would sit unseen inside the uploads; at that pace it is under 100 a month.

Checks run

  • Against beehiiv's own count. The records, with invalid addresses removed, match beehiiv's daily "subscribed" figure on 319 of 321 days; the other 2 differ by one record each.
  • Independent recount. A second script that shares no code with the analysis (pandas and two regular expressions on the raw fields) returns the identical figure in all 11 months.
  • Unit tests. Each rule, hand-worked tables, a synthetic world with no ads and no study (nothing removed) and one with planted numbers (all recovered exactly, including untagged ad sign-ups).
  • Against GA4. Tool sign-ups kept per GA4 email submission outside ad sessions and off the study page run 0.59 to 0.72 from March to November (July and August set aside: GA4 recorded nothing on the personality test for three weeks, and study participants then took other tools). January and February run 0.82 to 0.83: the uploads held more people than GA4-tracked tools explain, so they included sign-up sources GA4 does not see. Those are not ads (GA4 saw only 1,195 and 1,171 ad-session submissions in those two months) and not the study.
  • Dashboard. Its "new subscribers" card counts invalid addresses, so it reads 3% to 5% above beehiiv's own figure. About 2,000 of those addresses, from the September and October waves, now carry beehiiv's import label: day by day they are exactly the records that turned out invalid.
