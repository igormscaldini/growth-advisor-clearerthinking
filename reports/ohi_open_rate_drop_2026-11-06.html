Why One Helpful Idea opens fell in 2026

2026-11-06 02:42pm · 35 One Helpful Idea sends, Jan 6 to Oct 20, 2026 · beehiiv send statistics split by mailbox provider, cross-checked against your own open pixel in GA4

The open rate of One Helpful Idea (OHI) went from 45% in January to 21% in October. Most of that is not readers losing interest. It is three separate things stacked on top of each other:

  • Gmail stopped registering opens, about 11 points. Between Aug 5 and Aug 25, on the same engaged audience, Gmail's open rate fell from 37.0% to 20.6%. Readers on Yahoo, Hotmail, Outlook and AOL opened the very same emails at 58.7% and then 65.5%. Gmail is 82% of the audience, so it drags the whole number, and it has not recovered: 16% in October.
  • A slow drift before that, about 8 points. From January to early August, on the same size of audience, the rate eased from 45% to 37% (Gmail 43.5% to 35.4%, the other providers 63.5% to 58.9%).
  • A wider audience, about 4.6 points. In September the "engaged" audience OHI goes to grew from about 241k to about 368k people. The added readers open less, which lowers the rate without anyone reading less.

The timing points at the sending domain's standing with Gmail, not at the content. On Jul 1 the main newsletter moved onto OHI's sending domain (ohi.clearerthinking.net). Mail from that domain went from 0.9M emails a month to 2.4M, then 3.4M in September, and it started carrying sends to the whole list and resends to people who never open. Gmail's treatment of OHI changed six weeks later.

  • September: 15 sends and 3.4M emails, of which 599k in three sends that opened under 15% (the dating app update, the "everyone else" half of a tool launch at 8.3%, and a re-launch to non-openers). OHI's engaged audience is widened in the same weeks.

The old sending domain (info@clearerthinking.net) went through the same thing earlier. 4. Are Gmail readers gone, or just not counted?

Partly not counted. Opens overstate the damage. Gmail's click rate, measured against the other providers' click rate on the same send, was 0.41 before the break, 0.45 from Aug 18 to Sep 28 and 0.32 in October. So through September the Gmail readers who click were still receiving and reading OHI, while recorded opens had already halved. In October Gmail clicks are down by about a fifth as well (19 per 10,000 delivered, from 25).

That pattern is what you get when Gmail moves a sender's mail out of the inbox (to Spam or Promotions) or stops loading its images, starting with the least engaged recipients. All three are Gmail reacting to a domain whose mail many recipients ignore. Which of the three it is cannot be read from beehiiv: Google Postmaster Tools and an inbox placement test can show it.

6. What does not add up yet

  • The segments have been edited and I cannot see the history. The Oct 20 send targeted "OHI Audience - Engaged Subs" and reached 371,636 people, but that segment today is defined as an open rate of 30% or more and holds about 161k. "OHI Audience - All Subscribers" reached 358,641 on Oct 13 and holds about 660k today. I worked from recipient counts, not segment names.
  • Two halves of the same email get very different results. On Oct 8 the half sent first opened at 19.3% and the half sent 71 minutes later at 30.5%. On Nov 5 the first half is at 10.7% and the second at 23.6%; the gap is all Gmail (6.1% against 23.3%) while the other providers lean the opposite way (19.3% against 14.5%). Nov 5 has under a day of data. If the halves are random, Gmail is making different bulk decisions about identical emails, which is filtering and not reader behaviour. It also means the rating-box order test those variants were built for is confounded.
  • OHI does somewhat worse at Gmail than the main newsletter from the same domain. Gmail opened the main newsletter at 26.8% on Aug 20 and 25.6% on Sep 18; OHI got 20.6% on Aug 25 and 18.9% on Sep 21. One known difference: since Aug 5 every OHI email carries your second tracking pixel on a vercel.app address and the main newsletter does not. That is a hypothesis, not a finding (see the fourth idea below).
  • An early step in March. Between Feb 3 and Mar 10, after three whole-list sends in a row, Gmail went from 41.6% to 35.6% on the engaged audience while the others stayed at 63.7% and 61.4%. Same mechanism, smaller scale.
  • beehiiv's spam-report counts say nothing about Gmail. Gmail does not pass individual complaints back to senders, so the low counts on these sends (9 to 26 per send) come from the other providers.
  • No OHI has gone out since Oct 20 (as of Nov 6), so there is no reading yet on the current, stricter segment.

7. What I would do, in order

9/10Look at Google Postmaster Tools for ohi.clearerthinking.net

Google's free dashboard of how Gmail itself sees a sending domain: the share of recipients who press "Report spam", authentication, delivery errors and whether the domain meets Gmail's bulk-sender rules (spam rate under 0.1%, never 0.3%). If the domain is not set up there yet, it takes one DNS record.

For: It is the only place Gmail's spam complaints are visible (beehiiv's spam counts leave Gmail out), and ten minutes there confirms or kills the diagnosis in this report.

Against: It tells you how bad things are, not how to fix them, and it may hold no history if the domain was never verified.

9/10Send OHI to proven readers only for six weeks

Use a strict engaged audience (the segment as it is defined today, open rate of 30% or more, about 161k people, is strict enough) for every OHI send, and watch whether Gmail's open rate climbs back toward the 35% of early August.

For: Since July, mailing more people has not produced more opens: the Aug 10 send to 464k got 13,727 fewer opens than the Aug 5 send to 234k. So there is little to lose, and steady engagement from a smaller audience is how a sender earns its way back at Gmail.

Against: The reported subscriber reach of OHI drops, which matters for sponsor numbers, and Gmail readers whose opens are currently not being recorded could be cut by an open-based filter.

8/10Keep resends to non-openers and whole-list blasts off this domain

The Sep 14 re-launch (6% opened), the Oct 29 resend (2.6% opened, 1.0% at Gmail) and whole-list sends teach Gmail that mail from this domain is mostly ignored. Stop them, or send them rarely and from a different subdomain so OHI and the main newsletter are not graded with them.

For: It removes the most likely cause, and it costs only the few hundred clicks each of those sends brings (434 verified clicks on Oct 29).

Against: Launch emails lose their second wave, and a separate subdomain is one more thing to maintain.

6/10Test OHI with and without the second tracking pixel

Since Aug 5 every OHI email carries your own open-tracking pixel, hosted on a shared vercel.app address, next to beehiiv's. The main newsletter does not carry it. Send one OHI in two random halves, one with the pixel and one without, and compare Gmail's open rate. In plain terms: spam filters also judge the web addresses inside an email, and free shared hosting addresses have a poor name because scammers use them.

For: It is free, takes one send, and the pixel is redundant for counting (it follows beehiiv's numbers at a constant ratio).

Against: The evidence is thin: April's main newsletters carried the same pixel with no visible effect, and the first two OHI sends with it (Aug 5, Aug 10) were normal.

6/10Run an inbox placement test

Services such as GlockApps send your email to a panel of test mailboxes and report where it landed: inbox, Promotions or Spam, per provider.

For: It answers directly the one thing this data cannot: whether Gmail is hiding OHI in Spam, moving it to Promotions or just not loading images.

Against: Test mailboxes have no reading history, so they show the worst case, not what an engaged reader gets. It costs a little money.

5/10Re-test the old sending domain on a small engaged sample

info@clearerthinking.net has barely been used since July. Repeat the Aug 13 two-domain test on about 20k engaged readers per half.

For: If the rested domain has recovered at Gmail, that is proof that rest plus engaged-only sending works, and it gives you a fallback.

Against: A dormant domain that suddenly sends volume is itself suspicious to Gmail, hence the small sample. It also does not fix anything by itself.

2/10Move OHI to a fresh sending domain

Set up a new subdomain and send OHI from it.

For: A new domain starts with no bad history, and the July move did lift opens for a few weeks.

Against: This is what was done in July. The old domain's Gmail ratio had fallen from 0.98 to 0.51 in six weeks at the start of the year; the new one lasted about six weeks before it followed. The standing follows the sending habits, not the name, and a brand-new domain has to be warmed up slowly.

Method and data

Rates are unique opens divided by delivered. Totals per send come from the beehiiv API; the per-provider rows come from beehiiv's deliverability breakdown and cover the six largest providers. "Other providers" is Yahoo, Hotmail, Outlook and AOL pooled (about 13k to 23k recipients per engaged send); iCloud is excluded because Apple's privacy feature makes its open rate meaningless. A send counts as "whole list" at 395,000 recipients or more; the other providers' open rate confirms the two groups are cleanly apart (25% to 31% against 46% to 67%). The "October if Gmail had kept its usual gap" figure multiplies each October send's other-provider open rate by the median pre-break ratio (0.62) and leaves everything else as observed; this assumes a wider audience would dilute Gmail and the other providers in the same proportion. The split of the 24-point fall into 8, 4.6 and 11 points follows from those definitions. Source, data and tests: reports/ohi_open_rate_drop_2026-11-06_src/ and tests/test_ohi_open_rate_drop.py.
