How Marketing Brew runs its list

Reverse-engineered from the 963 Marketing Brew emails in Igor's Gmail, Dec 8, 2022 to Nov 9, 2026 (full headers via the Gmail API) · Generated 2026-11-10 04:21pm

In one paragraph. Marketing Brew (Morning Brew Inc.) sends one editorial email at 1:00 pm ET, Monday to Thursday, from Sailthru on four dedicated IPs under morningbrew.com. All event and sponsored promotion goes out from a separate subdomain, community.morningbrew.com, through a different ESP (SparkPost since May 2024). That promotional mail is about 2 emails a month, and non-openers get a rewritten "FW:" resend 2 to 13 days later. They emailed one re-permission check, early in the subscription. After that, Igor went 181 days without opening a single issue and they kept sending every issue, with no re-confirm email, so they show no strict open-based sunset. In Gmail the dailies land in the Updates tab, not Promotions.

  • Send time is fixed: since 2024, 95% of issues are stamped 13:00 to 13:02 ET. The issue reaches Gmail a median of under 1 minute after the stamp, and at worst about 5 minutes later. That means the list goes out in a fast blast, not a slow drip spread over hours.
  • Cutting Fridays (about a 20% cut in volume) is the biggest list-health lever visible here. They chose fewer sends over a stricter list.
  • Promotional stream: 26 emails in 2023, 26 in 2024, 31 in 2025 and 18 in 2026, so about 2 a month on top of the dailies. They cluster around their own events (The Brief, Marketing Brew Summit, virtual sessions) and sponsored webinars ("Together with Webflow").

  • Reputation isolation: promotional mail never shares a domain with the editorial issue, and since 2024 it doesn't share an ESP or IPs either. If an event blast draws spam complaints, the damage stays on community. and not on the newsletter.
  • Per-brand sender (Nov 11, 2025): the daily moved off the shared crew@ address (used by Morning Brew and Revenue Brew too) to marketingbrew@. A month later, replies went to a dedicated marketingbrewreplies@. Gmail tracks reputation by domain more than by address, so this is mostly about brand recognition and keeping replies separate.
  • Compliance: RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (List-Unsubscribe-Post) appears on every issue from May 29, 2024, when Google and Yahoo started enforcing their bulk-sender rules. It sits alongside a mailto unsubscribe and a tracked web unsubscribe link.
  • Inbox placement: the dailies landed in Updates (or Primary/Personal), never Promotions, until 3 issues in 2026. Promotional emails moved from Promotions (2023) to Updates from 2024 on.

Resends to non-openers ("FW:")14 promotional emails got a "FW:" follow-up 0 to 13 days later (median about 4). In 13 of them Igor hadn't read the original. The copy is rewritten for urgency ("Early birds caught the worm!…"), not just re-sent. Dailies are never resent.

The likeliest reading: they run an onboarding inactivity check on new subscribers in their first weeks, and after that they keep long-time inactives on the list. Their ad business sells reach, so they cut frequency (no Friday) rather than cutting subscribers.

Caveats. (1) This is one subscriber, so it shows the rules that applied to Igor, not the whole list. (2) Gmail's read flag is not the same as Sailthru's open. If Igor reads Gmail in Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection, Sailthru logged automatic opens on every issue and Igor never looked "inactive" to them. In that case, "no sunset" could be wrong. 4. What Clearer Thinking can take from this

  • Separate promotional mail onto its own subdomain, and ideally its own sending setup. This is the most directly useful finding. The OHI diagnosis showed the OHI domain's Gmail reputation broke after the main newsletter's wide sends moved onto it. Marketing Brew does the opposite: the daily owns morningbrew.com, and everything promotional goes through community.
  • One fixed send slot. Same time, same days, for years. Gmail's filters learn the pattern.
  • Cut frequency before cutting people. Dropping Friday was their lever. Since CT has no sunset either (678k active vs a 700k plan ceiling), the lever differs: their business sells reach, but CT pays per subscriber and has a Gmail reputation problem. So CT has stronger reasons to sunset than they do.
  • An onboarding inactivity check (around 3 weeks of no opens leads to "confirm you still want this") is cheap and catches typo'd and bot sign-ups early. That matters given CT's viral waves through the API.
  • Rewritten resends only for promotional mail, not for the editorial issue. That fits the earlier advice against routine resends of the newsletter.
