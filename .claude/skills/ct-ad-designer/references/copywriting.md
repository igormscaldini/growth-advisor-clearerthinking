# Ad copywriting — research-backed, time-enduring principles

Use this whenever you write or critique ad copy for CT (headlines, subheads, CTAs, primary text, descriptions). The guidance here is drawn **only from durable, evidence-based sources** — not influencer "hacks." Lean on these; ignore trend-of-the-week advice.

## Canon (the only sources to trust)
- **Claude Hopkins, *Scientific Advertising* (1923)** — advertising as testable salesmanship; specifics, offers, measurable claims.
- **John Caples, *Tested Advertising Methods*** — headline-first; principles validated by split-run/direct-response testing.
- **David Ogilvy, *Ogilvy on Advertising* / *Confessions***— headline carries most of the weight; clarity over cleverness; benefits; research-led.
- **Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*** — match the message to the reader's **awareness** and **market sophistication**.
- **Robert Cialdini, *Influence* / *Pre-Suasion*** — seven persuasion principles, each backed by controlled experiments.
- **Platform creative research** — Meta/Google's own large-scale ad studies (mobile-first, hook fast, one clear message, clear CTA).
- **CT Communication Guidelines** (`Branding assets/Communication … PDF`) — the brand-voice constraints below override anything that conflicts.

When unsure whether a tactic is legitimate, ask: *is it in the canon above, or supported by controlled research?* If not, don't use it.

## The hierarchy (where effort goes)
1. **Headline first.** Caples & Ogilvy: the headline does ~80% of the work; on average ~5x as many people read the headline as the body. Write 10–20 headlines, keep the best.
2. **One idea per ad** (single-minded proposition). If it says two things, it says nothing. Ogilvy/Hopkins.
3. **Then the proof/benefit (subhead/body), then the CTA.**

## Headline principles (Caples / Ogilvy / Hopkins)
- **Lead with self-interest / benefit** — the reader's gain, not the product. "What's in it for me?"
- **Be specific, not vague.** Concrete numbers and facts beat generalities and outperform them in tests ("106 self-help books, 12 levers" > "lots of advice, simplified").
- **The four proven appeals:** *self-interest, news/new, curiosity, quick & easy*. The strongest combine self-interest **+** curiosity. Curiosity alone is weakest (and risks clickbait).
- **Name the audience / the problem** so the right person self-selects.
- **Clarity beats cleverness.** Puns and wordplay usually lower response (Ogilvy). A clear promise wins.
- **Useful framing — the 4 U's:** make the headline **Useful, Unique, Ultra-specific**, and (only when genuinely true) **Urgent**. Don't manufacture urgency.
- **Don't make the headline a riddle** the image/body can't pay off.

## Match the reader (Schwartz)
- **Awareness:** Is the reader unaware of the problem, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or most-aware? Start the copy where they are. Cold audiences need the problem/benefit named; warm audiences (retargeting) can go straight to the offer.
- **Sophistication:** the more competing claims the reader has heard, the more you must differentiate with mechanism/specificity rather than louder claims.

## Benefits, "you," and concreteness
- **Benefits over features.** Translate every feature into a reader outcome (feature: "12 strategies" → benefit: "a complete toolkit for whatever life throws at you"). (CT comms: always phrase benefits in the user's own terms.)
- **Talk to "you."** Second person, active voice. (CT comms: "You can…" not "one can…".)
- **Concrete > abstract.** Specific words create pictures; vague words evaporate.
- **Short, plain words.** Hopkins/Ogilvy: simple language, short sentences. Write at a level a smart non-expert reads effortlessly.

## Persuasion principles (Cialdini — research-backed; use honestly)
- **Social proof** — numbers of users, real results/ratings ("Join 100,000+ readers"). Only if true.
- **Authority** — credible credentials/sources (CT's evidence base; "two PhDs"). CT's natural strength.
- **Liking / Unity** — speak as a peer who shares the reader's goal; warm, relatable.
- **Commitment & consistency** — small first step ("take the free 5-minute test").
- **Reciprocity** — lead with genuine free value (CT's free tools are this).
- **Scarcity** — only when real (a true deadline, e.g. pre-order/launch date). Never fake it.

## CTA (calls to action)
- **One** primary action. Don't split attention.
- **Start with a verb**, name the value: "Take the free test", "Get matched with a coach", "Pre-order the book".
- **Reduce friction & risk** — signal it's free / quick / no-commitment when true ("free", "5 minutes").

## Proven structures (use as scaffolding, not formulas)
- **PAS** — Problem → Agitate → Solve. Strong for problem-aware readers.
- **BAB** — Before → After → Bridge. Show the transformation, then the path.
- **AIDA** — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Classic long-form spine.
- **4 U's** — the headline checklist above.

## Platform notes (research-backed, durable)
- **Mobile-first; hook in the first line.** Front-load the value/curiosity; most users never expand "…see more."
- **One message per creative**; let the headline + image carry it.
- **Meta primary text:** lead with the benefit/hook in line 1; keep it tight; one clear CTA.
- **Google RSA / search:** provide multiple distinct headlines and descriptions; include the core benefit and (where natural) the query intent; stay specific and honest.
- **Test variety:** ship several distinct angles (different benefits/awareness levels), not minor rewordings — performance is found by testing (Hopkins' founding idea).

## CT brand-voice guardrails (override anything above if in tension)
From CT's Communication Guidelines:
- **No clickbait, no hype, no fake urgency.** No "You won't believe…", no manufactured scarcity, no ALL-CAPS shouting (a single styled accent word is fine).
- **Never talk down** or imply the reader is a poor thinker (beyond universal human biases). Speak as a peer.
- **Explain the benefit honestly** — value over persuasion; don't overpromise or imply guaranteed outcomes.
- **Benefit-focused, in the reader's own terms; "you"; American English.**
- Refer to us as **Clearer Thinking** / **ClearerThinking.org** (never "ClearerThinking"); tools are **"interactive tools"**.
- Keep it nuanced and quietly optimistic.

## Workflow when generating copy
1. Pin the **one idea** and the reader's **awareness level**.
2. Draft **10–20 headlines** across the proven appeals (self-interest, curiosity, news, easy) + a couple using PAS/BAB.
3. Cut to the 3–5 strongest, distinct angles. For each, mark the **blue-accent phrase** (for the house layout) and pair a **subhead** (the honest benefit/proof) and a **CTA**.
4. Pressure-test against the CT voice guardrails; kill anything clickbait or vague.
5. Pull real proof points/specifics from the page (Phase 2) — never invent stats or testimonials.
6. Present options and note which awareness level / structure each one targets, so the user can test.
