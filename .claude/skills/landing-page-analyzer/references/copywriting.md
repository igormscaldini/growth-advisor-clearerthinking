# Landing-Page Copywriting Reference

For analyzing a landing page's **copy** and answering: *"What could I change to convert more people?"* Sources span the advertising canon (Hopkins, Caples, Ogilvy, Schwartz), peer-reviewed psychology (Cialdini, Kahneman & Tversky, cognitive-fluency research), and tested CRO practice (NN/g, CXL, Copyhackers, Unbounce). Evidence is flagged: *[Research]* = peer-reviewed/experimental, *[Tested]* = documented A/B case (often single-source), *[Canon]* = enduring practitioner principle, *[Advice]* = heuristic.

> Shared canon note: this overlaps the ad-copy canon in `ct-ad-designer/references/copywriting.md`. That file governs **CT brand voice** — when suggesting copy rewrites for a CT page, the CT voice guardrails there (no clickbait, no hype, no fake urgency, never talk down, benefit-honest, "you", American English, "Clearer Thinking"/"interactive tools") **override** anything here that conflicts.

A lens before tactics: the highest-leverage move on most pages is **clarity**, not cleverness. "When a human being is reading your value proposition, clarity trumps persuasion any day." — Peep Laja, CXL.

---

## 1. The headline — highest-leverage element
- **Carries ~80% of the value.** "Five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy." — Ogilvy *[Canon]*. If the headline fails, nothing below it is read.
- **Appeal to self-interest or news; be specific.** Caples (split-run tested): strongest headlines appeal to **self-interest** or convey **news**; **specifics beat generalities**; "long headlines that say something" beat "short headlines that say nothing"; curiosity-only tests least reliably. *[Tested]*
- **Specifics out-pull superlatives.** Hopkins: "Platitudes and generalities roll off the human understanding like water from a duck." *[Canon]*
- **4 U's** (Masterson): Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific — Useful first. *[Advice]*
- **"So what?" test:** state the headline, ask "So what?" If the honest answer means nothing to the reader, rewrite. Name the outcome, not the product/company.
- **Use proven formulas + the customer's words, don't invent** (Wiebe/Copyhackers): e.g. *"The Only Way to [desire] Without [pain]"*, *"[Do hard thing] in [time] or [promise]"*. *[Advice]*
- **Message match:** the headline must echo the ad/email/link that drove the click — same promise, keyword, tone. Mismatch spikes bounce (Unbounce: ~98% of paid ads underperform largely on message match + attention ratio). *[Tested/Advice]*

## 2. Awareness & sophistication (Schwartz) — start where the reader is
**5 Stages of Awareness** — match the *opening* to the reader:
| Stage | State | Copy starts with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | doesn't know they have the problem | story/hook/startling fact; surface the problem, don't pitch |
| Problem-Aware | feels pain, doesn't know solutions | lead with the problem; agitate; reveal a solution category exists |
| Solution-Aware | knows solution types, not you | lead with result + your **mechanism**; differentiate |
| Product-Aware | knows you, not convinced | lead with proof, specifics, offer, risk reversal |
| Most-Aware | ready, needs the deal | lead with offer, price, urgency, CTA |

**Rule:** cold/paid traffic skews Unaware→Problem-Aware (lead with the problem); warm traffic (your email list, retargeting) skews Product-Aware→Most-Aware (lead with offer + proof). *[Canon]*

**5 Stages of Market Sophistication:** as a market hears more claims, louder claims stop working → escalate: simple claim → quantify it → introduce a **unique mechanism** → refine the mechanism/out-specify → shift to identity/brand/"why". In crowded categories (where most self-help/productivity tools sit), **explain the mechanism and differentiate**, don't just claim louder. *[Canon]*

## 3. Benefits vs features — translate every feature to an outcome
- Customers buy **benefits** (what it does for them), not features (what it has). *[Canon]*
- **"So what?" / "...which means that"**: append "...which means that" to any feature to force benefit language ("syncs across devices → which means you never lose your place").
- Give **both** the rational benefit and the emotional/identity payoff; lead with emotion (§11).
- *Caution:* blog "30–40% lift" figures attached to benefit-copy are practitioner claims, not controlled studies — use the technique, treat percentages as illustrative.

---

## 4. Voice of Customer / message mining (Wiebe) — use the customer's words
- **"Don't write copy — find it."** The best copy is mined from customers, not invented. Their phrasing makes readers feel seen and **lowers risk** vs invented marketing-speak. *[Advice]*
- **Mine:** reviews, support tickets, survey open-ends, sales-call transcripts, Reddit/Quora, competitor reviews. **For CT specifically:** the **March 2026 audience survey (n=539)** and tool feedback are ready-made VoC sources.
- **Method:** collect → analyze line by line → highlight repeated phrases → **use exact wording, don't paraphrase** (esp. in pain/objection sections).

---

## 5. Clarity & readability — how people actually read
- Users read ~**20–28%** of words on a page. *[Research]* — NN/g, "How Little Do Users Read?"
- **F-shaped scanning**; first lines and first words of each line dominate. *[Research]* — NN/g
- **Front-load:** users effectively see ~the first **2 words** of a link/heading. A front-loaded link was understood by 85% vs 15% for one opening with "Introducing…". Never open with "Welcome to / We are / Introducing." *[Research]* — NN/g
- **Inverted pyramid:** payoff first, detail after. *[Research]*
- **~8th-grade reading level**; short common words; short sentences (Flesch-Kincaid). *[Research]* — NN/g, plainlanguage.gov
- **Cognitive fluency — easy-to-read is *more persuasive*:** easy-to-read statements are judged **more true** (Reber & Schwarz 1999); needless complexity **lowers perceived intelligence** of the author (Oppenheimer 2006); hard-to-read instructions feel harder and sap motivation (Song & Schwarz 2008). *[Research]*
- **Scannability gains:** concise +58%, scannable layout +47%, objective (non-promotional) language +27%, all combined **+124%** usability. *[Research]* — Morkes & Nielsen 1997
- **5-second test:** can a stranger say what it is and what to do? *[Advice]*

## 6. "You"-focused, second person, active voice
- Write **to "you," not about "we."** Count you/your vs we/our — reader-facing language should dominate.
- **Active voice, present tense, concrete verbs** (passive/abstract is slower to process and weaker). The page should feel *about the reader*, not a brochure.

---

## 7. Structure & flow — the slippery slide
- "The sole purpose of the first sentence is to get the reader to read the second." — Sugarman. Every element earns the next; remove stopping points before the CTA. *[Advice]*
- **Typical high-converting order:** Hook (headline+subhead, message-matched) → Problem → Solution/mechanism → Benefits → Proof → Objection handling → Offer/risk reversal → CTA (repeated as the page lengthens).
- **Frameworks (scaffolds, not validated causal models):** **PAS** (Problem·Agitate·Solution — default for cold traffic), **BAB** (Before·After·Bridge — transformation offers), **AIDA** (Attention·Interest·Desire·Action). *[Advice]*
- **Subheads must tell the whole story alone** — because most visitors only scan, benefit-led subheads read top-to-bottom should convey the full gist. Bold the load-bearing phrases; one idea per paragraph; bullets for lists.

---

## 8. Proof & specificity — concrete beats superlative
- Specifics out-pull generalities ("lowers bounce 31%" > "dramatically improves results"). Replace every vague superlative with a number, name, or fact.
- **Named, specific testimonials** (full name, photo, role, concrete result) beat anonymous praise. Quantified social proof (counts, ratings + review volume, named logos, case studies). Every strong claim needs a checkable detail — unsupported hype *lowers* trust.

---

## 9. CTA copy — verb-first, value-led, friction-reducing
- **First-person wins:** "Start your free trial" → "Start **my** free trial" = **+90% clicks** (Aagaard/Unbounce, single famous test). *[Tested]*
- **Verb-first, value-led, never "Submit."** Call-to-Value (top/mid funnel) completes "I want to ___" ("Show me how," "Get my free assessment"); Call-to-Action (high commitment) = "Buy now." Begin with value, minimize effort/friction words. *[Advice]*
- **Click-trigger microcopy near the button** ("No credit card required," "Takes 2 minutes," "Cancel anytime") removes last-second resistance.
- **Descriptive button text > generic** (Baymard): "Get my free eBook" > "Download."

---

## 10. Objection handling in copy
- Answer each hesitation ("too expensive," "won't work for me," "too hard," "will I get spammed?") **near where it arises**, not buried in an FAQ.
- **FAQ** in the customer's voice answering the real fear behind each question.
- **Risk reversal / guarantees** (Hopkins tested them; Abraham named the concept): specific, prominent, near the CTA ("30-day, no-questions-asked refund"). Stronger guarantees often *lift* sales while *reducing* refunds (reduced perceived risk + endowment effect). *[Canon/Advice]*

---

## 11. Emotion + logic — move with feeling, justify with reason
- **People decide emotionally, then justify rationally** (Damasio's somatic markers; emotion is *necessary* for choice). Lead with emotional/identity benefit; supply logical proof (specs, data, guarantees) so buyers can justify. *[Research]* (treat the "95% subconscious" figure as imprecise)
- **Loss aversion** (Kahneman & Tversky 1979): frame the **cost of not acting**, not just the upside. *[Research]*
- **Storytelling lowers resistance** (Green & Brock narrative transportation: absorption reduces counterarguing). Customer-success stories and before/after scenarios beat bare claims. *[Research]*
- **Cialdini's 7 levers** *[Research]*: Reciprocity (give first — free tool/assessment), Commitment/Consistency (micro-first-step), Social Proof, Authority (credentials, cited research, professional design), Liking (relatable, warm), Scarcity (genuine only), Unity (shared identity / "for people like us").

---

## 12. Microcopy — small text that removes friction
- **Labels** specific ("Work email," not "Email"). **Privacy reassurance** by the email field lifts completions. **Button subtext / click triggers** ("No card required," "2 minutes"). **Error messages** specific and recovery-oriented ("Enter a valid email like name@site.com"), never "Invalid input." (Treat specific microcopy lift % as practitioner case studies.)

---

## 13. What to avoid
Jargon/"erudite vernacular" (lowers perceived intelligence & trust) · we-focused brochure copy · vague superlatives with no proof · feature dumps · weak/passive/clever headlines that fail "So what?" · clickbait/message mismatch · manufactured urgency · burying the value prop · too many links/CTAs (attention ratio).

---

## 14. Copy-audit checklist (ordered by likely impact)

**A. Headline & above-the-fold (highest)**
1. Headline names a **specific benefit/outcome** + passes "So what?"
2. **Matches the traffic source** (message match)?
3. Passes the **5-second test**?
4. Specific (numbers/names) not vague/superlative?

**B. Awareness fit**
5. Opening **meets the reader's awareness stage** (cold → problem-first; warm → offer/proof-first)?
6. In a crowded category, explains a **mechanism/differentiation**, not just a louder claim?

**C. Clarity & scannability**
7. ~Grade-8 reading level, short words/sentences, active voice?
8. **Benefit-led subheads, read alone, tell the whole story?**
9. Important info **front-loaded** (first 2 words carry meaning)?
10. One idea per paragraph, bullets, key phrases bolded, no fluff intro?

**D. Reader focus & benefits**
11. **"You"-focused** (you/your >> we/our)?
12. Every feature **translated to a benefit** ("...which means that")?
13. **Leads with emotion/identity**, backed by logical proof?

**E. Proof & credibility**
14. **Named, specific, quantified** testimonials/social proof?
15. Every strong claim **backed by a checkable specific**?
16. Which Cialdini levers present/missing?

**F. Objections & risk**
17. Top **3–5 objections answered** inline/FAQ, in the customer's words?
18. **Risk reversal/guarantee**, specific, near the CTA?

**G. CTA & microcopy**
19. Button **verb-first, value-led, first-person** ("Get my…"), never "Submit"?
20. **Click trigger** near the button?
21. Form: minimal fields, specific labels, privacy reassurance, helpful errors?

**H. VoC & focus**
22. Built from **mined customer language**, not invented?
23. Focused on **one action** (attention ratio near 1:1)?

### Evidence legend
*[Research]* — peer-reviewed/experimental (Cialdini; Kahneman & Tversky 1979; Reber & Schwarz 1999 / Oppenheimer 2006 / Song & Schwarz 2008 cognitive fluency; Green & Brock; Damasio; NN/g eyetracking/readability). *[Tested]* — documented A/B case, often single-source (Aagaard +90%; Unbounce "98% of ads"; Caples split-run). *[Canon]* — Hopkins, Caples, Ogilvy, Schwartz. *[Advice]* — PAS/BAB/AIDA, slippery slide, attention ratio, click triggers, microcopy % (illustrative, not laws).
