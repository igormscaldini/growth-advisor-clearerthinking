# Landing Page & CRO Reference

A research-backed reference for analyzing any landing page (LP) and answering: *"What could I change to convert more people?"*

Prioritizes large-scale UX research (Baymard, Nielsen Norman Group), research-driven CRO practitioners (CXL/Speero, WiderFunnel, Unbounce), academic persuasion/credibility work (Cialdini, Stanford/BJ Fogg, Iyengar), and aggregated benchmark data — over influencer "hacks." Where a popular myth contradicts the evidence, it's flagged explicitly.

> **How to use:** Sections 1–14 are the knowledge base. Section 15 is the audit checklist. When auditing, work the checklist and **prioritize findings by likely conversion impact** — focus/message match and value-prop clarity usually move the needle most; button color usually does not.

---

## 0. First principles (read before anything else)

- **"Best practices" are hypotheses, not laws.** Almost every tactic here is context-dependent. Real wins come from understanding *the visitor*, not copying a competitor's button color. Peep Laja (CXL): gains "don't come from psychological trickery but from analyzing what your customers really need, the language that resonates with them, and how they want to buy it." (https://cxl.com/blog/which-color-converts-the-best/)
- **People scan, they don't read.** Two decades of NN/g eye-tracking confirm it. (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)
- **First impressions are near-instant.** Impression forms in ~50ms; users routinely leave within 10–20s if value isn't clear. (NN/g)
- **Benchmark reality check:** median LP conversion ~**6.6%** (range ~3.8% SaaS → ~12.3% events), per Unbounce's 464M-visit / 41,000-page analysis. "Good" is ~5–10%; top 10% convert at 11%+. (https://unbounce.com/landing-pages/whats-a-good-conversion-rate/)

## 1. Conversion goal & message match

### One page, one goal, one action
- A campaign LP should have **a single conversion goal** and a **1:1 attention ratio** (things you *can* do : things you *should* do). Every off-goal link is a leak. (Unbounce/Oli Gardner — https://unbounce.com/conversion-glossary/definition/attention-ratio/)
- Unbounce's **20,000+ LP** analysis: more links correlate with lower conversion. Most actionable structural finding for LPs. (https://unbounce.com/conversion-centered-design/)
- **Implication:** on a dedicated campaign LP, **remove the site nav and footer links**, or cut them drastically.

### Message match / "scent"
- **Message match** = consistency between the ad/email/source and the page (same headline language, offer, visual style). **Ad scent** = the trail of consistency from click to page. (https://www.klientboost.com/cro/message-match/)
- Mechanism is **cognitive fluency**: a page that echoes the ad is processed effortlessly and judged more positively. Mismatch silently kills conversions. A Mox case study reported up to **+212%** from message matching (treat number as illustrative; direction is robust).
- **Audit rule:** the LP headline should contain, near-verbatim, the promise/keyword from the ad/email that drove the click.

## 2. Above-the-fold & the 5-second test

- **The fold matters but isn't everything.** NN/g: ~80% of viewing time is above the fold, ~20% below — **but users do scroll** if what's up top is promising. The fold is an attention gradient, not a wall. (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention-original-research/)
- **Myth flagged:** "everything important must be above the fold / long pages don't work" is false. Long pages convert when each screen pulls the reader down. Above-the-fold's job is to **earn the scroll**.
- **5-second test:** show the page for 5s, then ask *What is this? What can I do here? Is it for me?* If they can't answer, the value prop isn't clear.
- **Hero must contain:** (1) clear value-prop headline (what + for whom + why better), (2) supporting subhead (the "how"/proof), (3) relevant hero visual, (4) primary CTA visible without scrolling, (5) optionally one trust cue (a key number, logo bar, or proof point).

---

## 3. Value proposition

The #1 thing to get right; influences conversion more than any cosmetic change.

- **Clarity beats cleverness.** Ambiguous "creative" headlines underperform plain, specific ones. (CXL — https://cxl.com/blog/how-to-build-a-high-converting-landing-page/)
- Must instantly answer: **What** is it? **For whom**? **Why better/different** (unique mechanism or main benefit)?
- **Structure:** Headline = main benefit/outcome in the customer's language. Subhead = specificity (mechanism / "how" / concrete proof). Bullets = 3–5 scannable, benefit-led statements.
- **Specificity converts.** "Set up in 4 minutes," "used by 12,000 teams" beat "the best solution." Specificity also raises credibility (§7).

## 4. Visual hierarchy & layout

- **F-pattern** emerges on **text-heavy pages with weak hierarchy** — horizontal sweep top, shorter sweep lower, vertical scan down the left. Right-side content gets skipped. **Nuance/myth-buster:** the F is a *symptom of poor formatting*, not a goal. Good formatting (headings, bold, bullets, grouping) disrupts it and lets you steer attention. Front-load key info; start headings with information-bearing words. (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)
- **Z-pattern** suits sparse, low-copy LPs: top-left → top-right → diagonal → bottom-left → bottom-right. Put logo top-left, secondary cue top-right, CTA at the Z's terminal point. (Unbounce CCD)
- Other patterns: **layer-cake** (scanning headings only → make subheads self-sufficient), **spotted**, **commitment**.
- **Faces & gaze:** humans look at faces/eyes and **follow gaze direction**. Breeze "baby" study: viewers followed the baby's gaze to the headline. **Rule:** orient any hero face's gaze toward your headline/CTA, not at the viewer or off-page. Real smiling people often beat generic product shots (one case: +102.5%; directional). (https://www.neurosciencemarketing.com/blog/articles/baby-heat-maps.htm)
- **Whitespace** around a key element raises its salience — don't crowd the CTA. **Reserve the boldest/most-contrasting color for the CTA** (isolation/Von Restorff effect). Use **directional cues** (arrows, lines, gaze, layout flow) toward the CTA.

## 5. Cognitive load & friction reduction

- **Hick's Law:** decision time rises with number/complexity of choices. More options = more effort = slower/abandoned decisions.
- **Jam study (Iyengar & Lepper, 2000):** 6 vs 24 jams — 24 drew more lookers but only **3% bought** vs **30%** at 6 (~10× difference). **Myth-honesty:** choice overload **doesn't always replicate** (Scheibehenne 2010 meta-analysis ≈ zero mean effect; Chernev 2015 — effect is *moderated* by decision difficulty, preference uncertainty, assortment complexity, effort). Reducing choices is a sound *default*, not a law. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11111947/)
- **Friction reduction:** remove nav; one primary CTA repeated; minimize form fields (§9); plain language, one idea per section; fast page (§10).

## 6. CTA design

- **Placement & repetition:** primary CTA above the fold and **repeated** down a long page (after value prop, after social proof, at the end) so visitors act at the moment of conviction. Surround with whitespace; place at the page's visual terminal point. (CXL — https://cxl.com/blog/call-to-action/)
- **Button color — the real research (myth-buster):** there is **no universally best color**. What matters is **contrast** — the button must stand out from its surroundings. "Red beats green" case studies are true *for that palette*, not transferable laws. (CXL — https://cxl.com/blog/which-color-converts-the-best/)
- **Button copy matters more than color.** First-person, benefit-led wins: "Start your free trial" → "Start **my** free trial" lifted CTR **~90%** (ContentVerve via Unbounce). Replace "Submit/Sign up/Click here" with specific value that completes "I want to ___" ("Get my free audit"). CXL: ~1 in 7 CTA tests reach significance, but winners average ~**49%** lift, and *wording* moves results more than color.
- **Size:** big enough to be obviously clickable (Fitts's Law + mobile tap targets). Don't disguise the main action as a low-contrast "ghost" button.

## 7. Trust & credibility

**Stanford Web Credibility (BJ Fogg, 4,500+ participants) — 10 guidelines:** make accuracy easy to verify; show a real org (address, photos); highlight expertise; show the real people/bios; make contact easy; design professionally; make it easy to use; update content; restrain promotional content; avoid errors (typos/broken links hurt disproportionately). (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Web_Credibility_Project)
- **Key finding:** users judge credibility largely on **surface/visual design** — ~46% cited visual design appeal (highest for finance/search/travel). A polished design is itself a trust signal.
- **Risk reduction:** guarantees, free trials, "no credit card required," privacy/security assurances, visible refund/return policy — all lower perceived risk and counter "what if this is a mistake?" (§12).
- **Specificity of proof:** "Rated 4.8/5 by 2,431 customers" beats "loved by everyone."

---

## 8. Social proof

(Cialdini: under uncertainty, people copy others.)
- **Ranked by credibility:** named testimonials **with photo + full name + title/company** are the strongest text format (63% find identifiable testimonials more credible than anonymous) → **video testimonials** → **aggregate numbers** ("12,000+ customers") → **star ratings/review counts** → **customer/press logos** (keep grayscale so they don't fight the CTA) → **anonymous quotes** (weakest; can *reduce* trust by looking fabricated).
- **Placement:** near the decision point (next to CTA/pricing) and after the value prop. Reported lifts ~34–37% (directional, variable).

---

## 9. Forms

Every field is friction; field count is one of the most reliable levers.
- **Baymard:** average checkout has ~11–12 fields but most need ≤8; guest checkout works with 6–8. ~**26% abandon checkout because it was too long/complex.** (https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-optimization-from-16-fields-to-8)
- **Lead-gen LPs:** Unbounce CCD — **3–5 fields max.**
- **Myth nuance ("shorter is always better"):** usually true, but fewer fields can raise lead *quantity* while lowering *quality*; a qualifying field sometimes improves ROI. Optimize for the business outcome and test.
- **Usability:** persistent labels above the field (not placeholder-only); inline, specific, real-time errors (never wipe the form); mark only required fields; single-column; multi-step + progress bar can beat one long form (test); correct input types/autofill/mobile keyboards.

## 10. Page speed & mobile

- **Speed → conversion (Google/SOASTA):** bounce probability +32% (1s→3s), +90% (1s→5s), **+123%** (1s→10s) on mobile. Conversions drop ~**4.42% per added second** (0–5s). A 0.1s improvement lifted conversions ~8.4–10.1% (Deloitte/Google). **53% of mobile visitors abandon** pages slower than 3s. Target **<2–4s**. (https://business.google.com/think/marketing-strategies/mobile-page-speed-new-industry-benchmarks/)
- **Mobile-first:** design/test on mobile first (most paid traffic is mobile). Single column, large tap targets, minimal copy, compressed images, no intrusive interstitials. F-pattern persists on mobile, so front-loading matters more.

---

## 11. Urgency & scarcity

(Cialdini: scarcity raises perceived value.)
- **Legitimate works:** genuine deadlines, real low-stock, limited cohorts/seats.
- **Fake urgency is a dark pattern — and a legal/reputational risk.** FTC's 2022 dark-patterns report calls out fake countdown timers and false scarcity (timers that reset, "only 2 left" that never changes, "expiring" offers that don't). (https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800+Dark+Patterns+Report+9.14.2022+-+FINAL.pdf)
- **Rule:** a timer/scarcity claim must be *true* — the offer actually changes when it expires. Fake urgency erodes trust (the main conversion driver, §7).

---

## 12. Objection handling

- Unspoken objections ("Is it worth it? Will it work for me? What if I'm wrong? Is this legit?") block conversion. Surface and answer them on the page.
- **FAQ** for the top real objections (price, commitment, setup effort, compatibility, cancellation). **Risk reversal:** money-back guarantee, free trial, "no card," easy cancellation, transparent pricing (hidden fees are a top Baymard abandonment cause). **Match each objection to proof** (testimonial/stat/guarantee) right where it arises. **"Why us"/comparison** handles "why not a competitor / why not do nothing."

---

## 13. Testing mindset

- **Best practices are starting hypotheses, not guaranteed wins.** Copying a competitor copies their context, not their results.
- **A/B fundamentals:** pre-calculate sample size from a chosen MDE, baseline, significance/power *before* launching; don't peek-and-stop; run to significance (p<0.05) **and** full business cycles (≥1–2 weeks). Many tests never reach significance (~1 in 7 CTA tests) — prioritize **big swings** (value prop, offer, structure) over micro-tweaks. A good hypothesis names the problem, the mechanism, and a measurable outcome.
- **Prioritization frameworks:** **PIE** (Potential, Importance, Ease), **ICE** (Impact, Confidence, Ease), **PXL** (CXL — objective binary criteria to reduce gut-feel). Pair upside scores with a risk/measurability check.

## 14. Common conversion killers

1. No single clear goal / attention ratio worse than 1:1 (full nav on a campaign LP). 2. Message mismatch (broken scent). 3. Vague/clever-but-unclear value prop; fails 5-second test. 4. Buried CTA / not above fold / not repeated. 5. Low-contrast "ghost" CTA. 6. Generic CTA copy ("Submit"). 7. Too many form fields. 8. Slow page (>3s), esp. mobile. 9. Poor mobile UX. 10. Weak/anonymous social proof. 11. Wall-of-text, no hierarchy. 12. Unaddressed objections / no risk reversal / hidden pricing. 13. Low surface credibility (dated design, typos, no "who we are"). 14. Hero image working against you (stock photo, or face gazing away from CTA). 15. Fake urgency / dark patterns. 16. Distractions near the CTA (competing colors, autoplay, pop-ups, full-saturation logos). 17. Optimizing on opinion, not data.

## 15. Practical audit checklist (ordered by likely impact — fix top tiers first)

### Tier 1 — Goal, message, value prop (highest impact)
- [ ] Exactly **one** conversion goal?
- [ ] **Attention ratio ~1:1** (no site nav / competing links/offers)?
- [ ] **Headline matches the ad/source** (same promise/keyword)?
- [ ] Passes the **5-second test** (What? For whom? Why better?)?
- [ ] Value prop **clear and specific** (not vague or clever-but-confusing)?
- [ ] **Primary CTA above the fold** and **repeated**?
- [ ] Above-the-fold content **earns the scroll**?

### Tier 2 — Friction, CTA, proof (high impact)
- [ ] CTA copy **first-person/benefit-led** (not "Submit")?
- [ ] CTA **stands out by contrast**, has whitespace?
- [ ] Form **as short as the goal allows** (lead-gen 3–5; checkout ≤8)?
- [ ] Labels persistent, errors inline/specific, single-column?
- [ ] **Strong specific social proof** (named + photo, real numbers) **near the decision point**?
- [ ] **Top objections handled** (FAQ, guarantee, "no card," transparent pricing)?
- [ ] Page **fast (<3s)** and **mobile-first**?

### Tier 3 — Hierarchy, trust surface, visuals
- [ ] Text **scannable** (headings, bold, bullets) not a wall?
- [ ] Sensible **F/Z flow** ending at the CTA?
- [ ] Hero image aids comprehension; if a face, **gaze points at headline/CTA**?
- [ ] **Directional cues / whitespace** lead the eye to the CTA?
- [ ] Passes **surface-credibility** checks (professional design, no typos/broken links, real org/contact)?
- [ ] Partner/press logos **desaturated**?

### Tier 4 — Integrity & process
- [ ] Any urgency/scarcity **genuine** (no fake timers/false stock)?
- [ ] A **testing plan** (prioritized hypotheses, pre-set sample size/MDE, run to significance + full cycle)?
- [ ] Measuring the **real business outcome** (qualified conversions/revenue), not just clicks/form fills?
