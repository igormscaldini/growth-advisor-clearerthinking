# Deliverability and sending infrastructure of 1M+ subscriber newsletters (as of 2026-11-06)

Reading notes for the report writer:
- Research date 2026-11-06. "First-hand" = the operator, mailbox provider or vendor said it itself. "Second-hand" = reported by someone else. "Search snippet" = seen only in a search-result summary, page not opened, treat as unverified.
- "DNS lookup" = public DNS records I queried directly on 2026-11-06 (reproducible through the dns.google links). DNS shows what a domain publishes and which vendors it authorizes. It does NOT prove which ESP sent today's newsletter, and SPF records often keep legacy vendors.
- Sources older than 2023 are flagged "possibly outdated". Vendor case studies are marketing documents: numbers are the vendor's.
- Correction to the brief: I found no evidence that Morning Brew built its own ESP. Tyler Denk describes a custom CMS, ad platform, referral program and data pipeline, not a sending platform, and Morning Brew's SPF record authorizes Sailthru and Mailgun (details under Domains).

## 1. Domains: root versus subdomains, stream separation, and rotating versus repairing a damaged domain

### Takeaway
Large senders use both patterns: some send from the root brand domain (Morning Brew, Axios, The Hustle appear set up that way in DNS), others from a dedicated subdomain (1440's `email.join1440.com`, theSkimm's `morning7.theskimm.com`, the NYT's `e.newyorktimes.com` for a Responsys stream). Mailbox providers and independent experts say a new domain or subdomain is not a reset (it must be warmed for 4 to 6 weeks and filters link it to the parent), yet one documented fix (Morning Brew, 2018) did include a new sending domain alongside list cuts and a dedicated IP.

### Cited Findings
- Google: "Messages of the same category should have the same From: email address", and gives examples of separate addresses per category on one domain. The page does not mention subdomains as a separation method. First-hand, page undated, read 2026-11-06. Source: [Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)
- Google counts subdomain mail toward the parent: "Messages sent from the same primary domain count toward the 5,000 limit" and "Senders who meet the above criteria at least once are permanently considered bulk senders." A new subdomain therefore does not escape bulk-sender status. First-hand, undated, read 2026-11-06. Source: [Google Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en)
- Google's Postmaster Tools help page says the Domain Reputation dashboard only shows messages sent from the exact domain used for DKIM and SPF authentication (paraphrase of the fetched page). First-hand, undated, read 2026-11-06. Source: [Postmaster Tools dashboards](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14668346?hl=en)
- Yahoo: "Don't send bulk/marketing email from the same IPs you use to send user mail, transactional mail, alerts, etc." and "Each IP and DKIM domain has a reputation, which can impact the delivery of your email." First-hand, undated, read 2026-11-06. Source: [Yahoo Sender Hub best practices](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)
- Apple iCloud Mail: "Use consistent sending IP addresses and domains for your bulk email" and keep marketing and transactional streams segmented. First-hand, published 2025-02-25. Source: [Apple postmaster information](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102322)
- Al Iverson (Spam Resource), 2026-03-21: three reasons to use a subdomain are clarity and auditing, technical necessity, and reputation segmentation; a bulk subdomain "can help insulate your critical corporate/executive communications"; but "spam filters are smart enough to link a subdomain to the main organizational domain"; "Stay away from cousin domains or lookalikes"; use short neutral labels (m., e., msg.), not "marketing" or "blast"; "You cannot go from zero to 100,000 emails overnight on a new subdomain"; sending from the top-level domain is not inherently wrong. Expert, not a 1M+ operator. Source: [Choosing the Right Subdomain](https://www.spamresource.com/2026/03/choosing-right-subdomain_0608263959.html)

### Inferences
- The reader's own split test (31% Gmail opens from the newer subdomain, 5% from the root address, same email) fits Google's statement that Postmaster reputation is shown per exact authenticating domain: Gmail was scoring the two identities differently at that moment. Experts' warning is about durability: the subdomain is linked to the organizational domain, so it keeps its advantage only if the mail sent on it stays wanted.
- No large operator in this research publicly describes rotating domains as a routine tactic. The only documented domain change (Morning Brew 2018) happened together with a 100,000-subscriber purge and a dedicated IP, so its separate effect cannot be isolated.
- Publishers with several newsletters or vendors tend to end up with several mail hosts (1440, theSkimm, NYT, WaPo), which looks like stream separation by subdomain, but DNS alone cannot show which stream uses which host.

## 2. IPs: dedicated versus shared, IPs per million, and what beehiiv offers

### Takeaway
At 1M+ scale the documented pattern is dedicated IPs (Morning Brew 2018, The Hustle on SendGrid, 1440 certifying additional IPs); vendor rules of thumb range from 1 to 1.5 million emails per IP (Iterable) to 3 to 4 million per IP per day (Twilio SendGrid). On beehiiv, dedicated IPs exist only on Enterprise with a custom domain and are required only above 700,000 emails per day per publication, so a ~500k list sending once a day sits below the mandatory threshold.

### Cited Findings

First-hand, updated 2026-03-05. Source: [beehiiv, When a dedicated IP address is needed](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/13092212986647)
- beehiiv Enterprise page: "Dedicated IPs are available for Enterprise customers sending on a custom domain"; "Enterprise migrations come with hands-on IP and domain warming from our deliverability team"; "direct access to deliverability specialists throughout"; quarterly strategy sessions "including deliverability reviews". The fetch showed plan labels Free, Lite, Pro, Enterprise rather than the Launch, Scale, Max names in the brief, so verify names on the live page. - By the SendGrid and Iterable ratios, a single daily send to ~500k needs one IP, two at most for stream separation. IP count is not the reader's constraint; a dedicated IP would mainly remove dependence on a shared pool's reputation and would itself need warming.

## 3. Authentication and compliance: SPF, DKIM, DMARC level, BIMI, one-click unsubscribe, and the Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft rules

### Takeaway
The binding rules are: SPF and DKIM both passing, DMARC published (p=none is enough) with From-domain alignment, RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe on marketing mail, and a complaint rate kept under 0.1% and never at 0.3% (Gmail) or under 0.3% (Yahoo), with Gmail "ramping up" enforcement from November 2025 and Microsoft applying equivalent authentication rules to 5,000+/day senders since 2025-05-05. In practice most large newsletters go well beyond the minimum: of 20 sending organizations checked in DNS, 13 publish p=reject, 5 p=quarantine, and only 2 (Milk Road root, James Clear) p=none; BIMI with a certificate is rare (Washington Post, The Rundown AI, Industry Dive's newsletter domain, HubSpot).

### Cited Findings
- Gmail, all senders: SPF or DKIM, valid PTR, TLS, "Keep spam rates reported in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%". Bulk senders ("more than 5,000 messages per day to Gmail accounts"): SPF and DKIM, DMARC whose policy "can be set to none", "the domain in the sender's From: header must be aligned with either the SPF domain or the DKIM domain", and "Marketing messages and subscribed messages must support one-click unsubscribe, and include a clearly visible unsubscribe link in the message body." Monitoring target: "below 0.10% and avoid ever reaching a spam rate of 0.30% or higher." First-hand, undated, read 2026-11-06. Source: [Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)
- Gmail FAQ: "Spam rate is calculated daily"; "Beginning June 2024, bulk senders with a user-reported spam rate greater than 0.3% will be ineligible for mitigation" and regain eligibility when "their spam rates remain below 0.3% for 7 consecutive days"; one-click unsubscribe must use RFC 8058 headers (mailto alone does not qualify) and "we recommend that you fulfill unsubscribe requests within 48 hours"; "Starting November 2025, Gmail is ramping up its enforcement on non-compliant traffic", with non-compliant mail seeing "disruptions, including temporary and permanent rejections" (4.7.x and 5.7.x codes). Nearly all of them also send DMARC aggregate reports to a monitoring vendor (Valimail, dmarcian, Validity Everest, EasyDMARC, Cloudflare, Inbox Monster, Postmark).
- BIMI is not standard practice at this tier: only 3 of the 17 newsletter publishers checked have a certificate-backed record, and several have a logo-only record that Gmail would not display.

### Gaps

- Not found: a Google statement quantifying the November 2025 enforcement ramp (rejection shares or dates per requirement).
- Not found: a current first-hand Microsoft statement of what happens today to non-compliant bulk mail beyond the April 2025 post. M3AAWG best-practice documents were not reviewed.

## 6. Send mechanics: engagement ordering, throttling, mailbox-provider segmentation, MPP and bot clicks

### Takeaway

## 7. Recovery playbooks: what happened, what was cut, for how long, and whether a new subdomain is legitimate

### Takeaway
The consistent expert playbook for a bad Gmail domain reputation is: confirm mail is really going to spam, fix authentication, send Gmail recipients only recent openers and clickers (90-day look-back per Atkins, six months as a starting point per Iverson) at normal cadence for at least a month, expect 3 to 4 weeks historically and 5 to 7 weeks since 2024, then re-add older subscribers in batches (about 10% a week) while permanently dropping the long-dead. Experts treat a new domain as "(almost) never" the solution and a new subdomain as something that must be warmed and stays linked to the parent, so it is a legitimate tool for separating streams but a short-lived fix if the same audience and practices follow it.

### Cited Findings
- Laura Atkins, Gmail repair steps (2021-01-27, possibly outdated): (1) "Verify that your mail is actually going to bulk"; (2) cut Gmail sends to people who "opened or clicked in the recent past", no more than 90 days back, for "a minimum of a month"; (3) reputation graphs lag 3 to 4 weeks and inboxing improves first; (4) if nothing improves, find mail you do not know about that uses your domain; (5) then apply the same hygiene to Google Workspace hosted domains; (6) re-add removed recipients gradually: "Do not drop them back into the feed all at once, start a warmup process to get you back up to full sends." Source: [Step by step guide to fixing Gmail delivery](https://www.wordtothewise.com/2021/01/step-by-step-guide-to-fixing-gmail-delivery/)
- Al Iverson (2024-09-24): "What was previously a 3-4 week process of sending to restore your domain reputation through boosted engagement is taking longer for some folks"; "what was 3-4 weeks before, might take 5-7 weeks now"; steps: authenticate with your own domain, set up Google Postmaster Tools, suppress subscribers not engaged in "the past X months" (his starting point is six months), keep normal cadence to the tighter list, wait; it does not work on old or non-opt-in data; Microsoft repair has taken "sometimes twice as long, sometimes longer"; prevention is a lifecycle policy with one to three winback attempts. Source: [Tuesday Tip: Gmail remediation is slower](https://www.spamresource.com/2024/09/tiny-tip-tuesday-gmail-remediation-is.html)
- Laura Atkins on re-expansion (2019-02-26, possibly outdated): removing unengaged addresses is part of the repair; "24 months is pretty safe", "12 or 18 months" is stricter, "I wouldn't advise being less aggressive"; add back recent engagers in batches, for example "10% a week"; slow down or stop if Gmail reputation falls or complaints rise; "Unless a recipient actually marked you as spam, you will be able to reach their inbox." Source: [Re-adding subscribers after reputation repair](https://www.wordtothewise.com/2019/02/re-adding-subscribers-after-reputation-repair/)
- Google mitigation rule: above a 0.3% user-reported spam rate a bulk sender is "ineligible for mitigation" until the rate stays below 0.3% for 7 consecutive days. Source: [Google Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en)
- New domain or subdomain as a fix: Atkins 2019 "Changing domains is (almost) never the solutions to domain reputation problems"; Atkins 2017 a new subdomain on a warm IP still needs warm-up; Iverson 2026 filters "link a subdomain to the main organizational domain"; Google counts subdomain volume toward the primary domain's permanent bulk status. Sources: [Word to the Wise 2019](https://wordtothewise.com/2019/02/my-domain-reputation-is-bad-should-i-get-a-new-domain), [Word to the Wise 2017](https://www.wordtothewise.com/2017/09/warmup-advice-gmail/), [Spam Resource 2026](https://www.spamresource.com/2026/03/choosing-right-subdomain_0608263959.html), [Google FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en)
- Vendor view (Suped, published 2025-05-26, updated 2026-10-14; generic, not from a 1M+ operator; the fetched page was titled "Why is my gmail postmaster reputation stuck on 'bad' and how can I fix it?"): subdomains do not reset Gmail reputation; moving platforms does not help because the "bad audience follows"; a 7 or 14 day pause does not reset a domain; recovery steps are pause Gmail marketing sends that land in spam, remove unconsented addresses, verify authentication, then restart with a small recently engaged cohort and ramp. Source: [Suped](https://www.suped.com/knowledge/email-deliverability/sender-reputation/how-to-fix-a-bad-gmail-email-reputation-for-a-subdomain-and-is-the-bulk-sender-contact-form-effe)
- Case, Morning Brew (2018, 250,000+ list then; possibly outdated): "In a year, we've gone from 60% inbox deliverability to 99% deliverability"; open rate from 22% to 50% ("jumped from 20% to 47% within 8 months"); Gmail moved its mail "from the promotional category to the personal inbox"; levers: re-engagement campaigns, about 100,000 unengaged subscribers churned, new sending domain, dedicated IP, add-to-contacts ask in the welcome series, ESP change from Mailchimp to Campaign Monitor. First-hand quotes in a vendor case study. Source: [Campaign Monitor](https://campaignmonitor.com/blog/customers/how-morning-brew-improved-email-open-rates-by-125)
- Case, 1440 (2024, 3.6M): inbox placement swinging by up to ten points was stabilized at ">99%" after an ESP migration, Everest monitoring, pre-send list validation and Sender Certification; no cuts or durations are reported. - beehiiv's guide gives no recovery timeline and neither recommends nor discourages changing the sending domain; it notes only that new domains need gradual volume increases, and sets re-engagement at about 90 days of inactivity with suppression of non-responders after 7 to 14 days. Source: [beehiiv diagnostic guide](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/38340775067415)

### Inferences
- For the reader's case, the evidence supports a two-track plan rather than a choice: keep the daily value on the better-performing subdomain (ramping, engaged-first), and repair the root domain with engaged-only Gmail sends for 5 to 7 weeks, because the subdomain's standing is tied to the organizational domain over time.
- Before repairing, apply Atkins' step 4 to the root domain: its DMARC has no reporting address and its SPF still authorizes Mailchimp and Mailgun (section 3), so other mail using `clearerthinking.net` could be feeding the bad reputation. This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
- If the old domain carried the full, older list while the new subdomain was warmed on engaged readers, moving the whole list to the subdomain without trimming would reproduce the old domain's engagement profile there. That is the mechanism behind experts calling a domain switch short-lived.

### Gaps
- Not found: a documented, dated Gmail spam-folder or Promotions incident and recovery at a named 1M+ newsletter after 2018 (what was cut, for how long, results). Operators at this size rarely publish them.
- Not found: any expert endorsing a move to a new subdomain as a durable fix, or any controlled data on how long a fresh subdomain keeps its advantage.
- Not found: evidence on the usefulness of Gmail's sender contact or escalation form at this scale.

## 8. Inbox-placement tactics used by big newsletters, and the evidence for them

### Takeaway
Reply asks, add-to-contacts and move-to-Primary instructions are widely used and recommended (Morning Brew's welcome series, beehiiv, Inbox Collective, Newsletter Operator), and mailbox-provider guidance supports a consistent From identity, but I found no controlled evidence that any of these tactics moves inbox placement; one deliverability vendor argues that chasing the Primary tab is wasted effort and can raise complaints.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew (2018, possibly outdated): its Welcome Series asked new subscribers to add Morning Brew to their contacts, listed among the actions behind the 60% to 99% improvement; the separate effect is not measured. Source: [Campaign Monitor](https://campaignmonitor.com/blog/customers/how-morning-brew-improved-email-open-rates-by-125)
- beehiiv (Tyler Denk, 2026-07-13): "Optimize for clicks and replies when possible"; moving mail to the primary inbox is listed as a positive engagement signal; ask subscribers to add you to contacts; for corporate readers ask IT to safelist `*beehiiv.com`, `*beehiiv.net` and your own domain. No data offered. Source: [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/newsletters101-understanding-email-deliverability)
- Matt McGarry (2025-05-24; advisory): welcome email asks for a reply because "It tells Google (and other email providers) you actually want to get this"; the "Greatest Hits" re-engagement email's P.S. asks readers to click and move the email to the primary inbox; "Clicks tell Gmail (and other email providers) that your email is valuable." No measured results. Source: [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate)
- Yanna-Torry Aspraki (Inbox Collective, 2024-01-03): educate subscribers to whitelist the address and move emails to the primary tab; encourage replies through questions and feedback requests; repeat deliverability reminders periodically. No data offered. Source: [Inbox Collective, 10 steps](https://inboxcollective.com/10-steps-to-maintain-great-deliverability-in-2024/)
- Counter-position (Brad Van der Woerd, Inbox Monster, 2025-09-29; vendor): "The Promotions tab is not a penalty box"; "Stop wasting energy trying to escape Promotions"; "Landing in Primary can increase spam complaints and unsubscribes." Only cited figure: ZeroBounce 2025, "54% of users check it either daily or sometimes." Source: [Inbox Monster](https://inboxmonster.com/blog/gmail-promotions-tab-inbox-placement)
- Consistent From identity: Apple, "Use a consistent From: name and address to clearly identify your brand"; Google, "Messages of the same category should have the same From: email address." Sources: [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102322), [Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)
- Links: beehiiv lists link reputation as a cause of sudden drops: check full redirect chains for sponsor and affiliate links and avoid URL shorteners (2026-10-09). Source: [beehiiv diagnostic guide](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/38340775067415)
- Workaround during a provider incident: Mailchimp's interim advice in June 2023 was for publishers to ask subscribers to move campaigns from spam to the inbox. Source: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/gmail-glitch-sending-newsletters-to-spam-mailchimp-confirms/488801/)
- Vendor statements that replies and address-book adds are positive signals (Litmus, Validity) appeared only as search snippets and cite no measurements. Source: [Litmus](https://www.litmus.com/blog/why-theyre-just-not-that-into-your-emails-and-how-to-fix-it)

### Inferences
- These tactics are cheap and consistent with how providers describe engagement signals, so large newsletters keep them in onboarding, but the documented recoveries (section 7) were driven by audience cuts and infrastructure changes, not by reply or Primary-tab asks.
- With Gmail ranking the Promotions tab by engagement since late 2025, sustained opens and clicks from the people mailed matter more than which tab the mail lands in.

### Gaps
- Not found: any test, from an operator or a vendor, isolating the effect of reply asks, add-to-contacts or drag-to-Primary on Gmail placement.
- Not found: 1M+ operator statements on plain design, image or link weight, or whitelisting instructions on confirmation pages, with evidence. Advice exists only in generic form.

## 9. Where practitioners disagree

### Takeaway
The real disagreements are four: new domain or subdomain as a fix, shared versus dedicated IPs (and how many), whether Primary-tab tactics are worth doing, and how far back the engagement window should reach; on all four the mailbox providers' own text is silent or minimal, so the positions below come from experts, vendors and operators.

### Cited Findings
- New domain or subdomain. Against: "Changing domains is (almost) never the solutions to domain reputation problems" (Atkins, 2019); filters link a subdomain to the organizational domain (Iverson, 2026). For, in practice: Morning Brew's 2018 turnaround included "moving to a new sending domain"; subdomains are endorsed for reputation segmentation (Iverson, 2026) and beehiiv supports per-publication subdomains. Sources: [Word to the Wise](https://wordtothewise.com/2019/02/my-domain-reputation-is-bad-should-i-get-a-new-domain), [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2026/03/choosing-right-subdomain_0608263959.html), [Campaign Monitor](https://campaignmonitor.com/blog/customers/how-morning-brew-improved-email-open-rates-by-125), [beehiiv Enterprise](https://www.beehiiv.com/enterprise)
- Shared versus dedicated IP. Dedicated: The Hustle left a shared pool that "didn't reflect" its engagement; Morning Brew moved to a dedicated IP; 1440 certifies its own IPs. Shared is fine below very high volume: beehiiv requires a dedicated IP only above 700K emails a day per publication. IPs per volume: 3 to 4 million a day per IP (Twilio SendGrid) versus 1 to 1.5 million per IP (Iterable). Sources: [Twilio, The Hustle](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/the-hustle), [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/13092212986647), [Twilio SendGrid docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/sendgrid/ui/account-and-settings/dedicated-ip-addresses), [Iterable](https://iterable.com/blog/lasting-deliverability-strategies/)
- Primary tab. Pursue it: McGarry, Aspraki and beehiiv recommend move-to-Primary and reply asks. Do not: Inbox Monster says "Stop wasting energy trying to escape Promotions." Sources: [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate), [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/10-steps-to-maintain-great-deliverability-in-2024/), [Inbox Monster](https://inboxmonster.com/blog/gmail-promotions-tab-inbox-placement)
- Engagement window. 14 days of inactivity then removal (1440, vendor case study); six weeks then a CEO email (Morning Brew, 2018); 30 to 90 days opened or 60 to 180 days clicked (McGarry's ramp); no more than 90 days during a Gmail repair (Atkins, 2021); six months as a starting suppression window (Iverson, 2024); about 90 days before a re-engagement flow (beehiiv, 2026); 12 to 24 months as the outer limit for ever re-adding (Atkins, 2019). Sources: [1440 case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/campaign-monitor/case-study/how-1440-media-averages-a-55-open-rate), [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers), [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate), [Word to the Wise 2021](https://www.wordtothewise.com/2021/01/step-by-step-guide-to-fixing-gmail-delivery/), [Spam Resource 2024](https://www.spamresource.com/2024/09/tiny-tip-tuesday-gmail-remediation-is.html), [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/38340775067415), [Word to the Wise 2019](https://www.wordtothewise.com/2019/02/re-adding-subscribers-after-reputation-repair/)
- Repair duration at Gmail. "A minimum of a month" with graphs lagging 3 to 4 weeks (Atkins, 2021) versus 5 to 7 weeks on recent anecdotal data (Iverson, 2024). Sources: [Word to the Wise](https://www.wordtothewise.com/2021/01/step-by-step-guide-to-fixing-gmail-delivery/), [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2024/09/tiny-tip-tuesday-gmail-remediation-is.html)
- Value of seed tests. Iverson lists them first among reputation checks but calls them "occasionally imperfect thanks to things like individualistic filtering"; 1440 relies on a paid suite for per-provider placement. Sources: [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2025/06/top-5-ways-to-check-your-email.html), [Validity, 1440 case study PDF](https://validity.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/240801-CaseStudy-1440-FINAL.pdf)
- DMARC level. Providers require only p=none; Aspraki says p=none "offers no protection" and recommends quarantine or reject; most large newsletters publish quarantine or reject, while Milk Road's root and James Clear publish none (DNS, 2026-11-06). Sources: [Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en), [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-diagnose-an-unexpected-drop-in-open-rates-and-engagement/), [dns.google milkroad](https://dns.google/resolve?name=_dmarc.milkroad.com&type=TXT), [dns.google jamesclear](https://dns.google/resolve?name=_dmarc.jamesclear.com&type=TXT)

### Inferences
- The disagreement on domains is narrower than it looks: nobody credible says a fresh domain repairs reputation by itself, and nobody says subdomains are illegitimate. The dispute is whether the gain lasts, and every source that addresses it ties durability to who is mailed, not to the name.
- Engagement-window disagreements track send frequency and risk: daily news senders with revenue tied to opens (1440, Morning Brew) cut far earlier than experts' repair windows, which are written for senders trying to keep as much list as possible.

### Gaps
- Not found: any head-to-head data (same sender, same period) comparing shared and dedicated IPs, or root-domain and subdomain sending, at 1M+ scale.
- Not found: positions from Sailthru, Mailgun/Sinch, SparkPost/Bird, Postmark, Customer.io or Amazon SES deliverability teams specific to large newsletters; only general documentation surfaced. M3AAWG documents, Validity benchmark reports, Chad S. White, Litmus and Email Geeks threads were not reviewed first-hand.
