# Send operations and process at 1M+ subscriber newsletters (as of Nov 2026)

Research notes compiled 2026-11-06. Reading guide: every finding carries a tag for the kind of evidence. "First-hand" = the operator or their staff said it (interview, own page). "Sales page" = the operator's own sponsor/advertise page (self-reported numbers, undated unless stated, read 2026-11-06). "Second-hand" = someone else reporting the operator's practice. "Vendor" = ESP or tool case study. "Snippet" = seen only in a search-result summary, page not opened, treat as unverified. "Generic" = not from a 1M+ operator. Anything from before 2022 is flagged "old" because it predates or straddles Apple Mail Privacy Protection (Sept 2021) and the 2024 Gmail/Yahoo sender rules. Overall verdict on the evidence base: the public record on what 1M+ newsletters do around the send is thin and skews old. The best-documented items are sunset rules (2018 to 2020), Morning Brew's subject-line test (2020), sponsor-slot rules (current sales pages) and platform testing mechanics (2025 to 2026). Published pre-send checklists, send-time test results, promo frequency caps and team org charts for the named newsletters were mostly not found, and those gaps are listed rather than filled.

## 1. Cadence and consistency: fixed day and time, emails per week, extra sends, resends to non-openers

### Takeaway
The weekly essay-style newsletters all hold one fixed day and build it into the product promise (Clear on Thursday, Ferriss on Friday, Parrish on Sunday, Manson on Monday), with one issue a week (Sahil Bloom and The Marginalian run two). No named 1M+ newsletter was found documenting routine resends to non-openers: beehiiv's CEO (ex-Morning Brew) calls resends "a terrible idea" and says none of the largest newsletters he worked with considered them, while consultant Dan Oshinsky allows them only sparingly for high-value announcements.

### Cited Findings
- James Clear, 3-2-1: "Sent once a week on Thursdays". The page gives two audience figures ("3,500,000 readers" and "sent to more than 3,000,000 people") and "47-49% open rates". Sales page, undated, read 2026-11-06. Source: [jamesclear.com sponsor page](https://jamesclear.com/sponsor) (fetched via [jamesclear.com/?p=41229](https://jamesclear.com/?p=41229))
- James Clear: a third-party email archive shows one 3-2-1 issue timestamped "Thu, 9:36 PM UTC", sent from james@jamesclear.com. Snippet, single observation, not a stated policy. Source: [Sequenzy email archive](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/james-clear/emails/3-2-1-how-to-feel-good-again-choosing-your-information-diet-and-the-secret-to)
- Farnam Street, Brain Food: "Sent weekly on Sunday", "Over 950,000 subscribers", "Open rates over 45-48%", "One sponsor per newsletter", "Booking Q3 2026 now". Sales page, metadata last modified 2026-06-04. Source: [fs.blog/sponsor](https://fs.blog/sponsor)
- Mark Manson, Your Next Breakthrough: weekly, every Monday, "1 million readers and growing", "55% average open rate"; each issue = one thing to think about, one question, one action, plus reader "breakthrough" stories. Sales page, images dated Aug 2024. Source: [markmanson.net/sponsor](https://markmanson.net/sponsor)
- Tim Ferriss, 5-Bullet Friday: "Every Friday, I send out an exclusive email with the five coolest things I've found (or explored) that week." An older version of the sign-up page adds "No spam, ever. Emails are never shared." First-hand sign-up pages, copyright 2025 and 2018. Sources: [go.tim.blog/5-bullet-friday-1](https://go.tim.blog/5-bullet-friday-1/), [go.tim.blog/5-bullet-friday](https://go.tim.blog/5-bullet-friday/)
- Sahil Bloom, Curiosity Chronicle: "Join 800,000+ others who receive the 2x weekly newsletter"; one of the recurring formats is "The Friday Five" roundup. First-hand page, undated, read 2026-11-06 (the same page also carries an older "225,000+" block). Source: [sahilbloom.com/newsletter](https://www.sahilbloom.com/newsletter)
- The Marginalian: two separately subscribable newsletters, "the standard Sunday digest of new essays" and a midweek email sent "Every Wednesday" that resurfaces one archive piece. First-hand page, undated, read 2026-11-06. Source: [themarginalian.org/newsletter](https://www.themarginalian.org/newsletter/)
- Lenny's Newsletter: weekly; at the 2020 paid launch Lenny described "a weekly newsletter" and sending "a post every week". First-hand, Aug 1, 2020 (old). Source: [Substack interview](https://on.substack.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-earned-65000)
- The Rundown AI: main AI newsletter (2.1M subscribers) sends Monday to Friday; Tech newsletter (610k) Tuesdays and Fridays; Robotics (475k) Mondays and Thursdays. The only dedicated sponsor email ("Full Email Takeover") is "sent only on Sundays", "reserved for select partners", so it never displaces a regular edition. Sales page, read 2026-11-06. - Morning Brew: daily including Saturdays ("go out pretty early (even on Saturdays)"). First-hand (Neal Freyman), June 18, 2019 (old). Source: [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-newsletter-writer-with-morning-brews-neal-freyman)
- theSkimm: added a Saturday edition of the Daily Skimm from April 2, 2022, sent to "more than 7 million subscribers", making it six days a week. Snippet (Digiday and company press release, not opened). Sources: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/why-theskimm-is-extending-its-daily-newsletter-to-the-weekend), [theSkimm press release](https://www.theskimm.com/press/theskimm-unveils-the-daily-skimm-weekend-extending-its-newsletter-beyond)
- Axios: fixed day-part slots per product: Markets (morning), Macro (around lunchtime), Closer (afternoon, after East Coast markets close), Finish Line (evening, "two-minute read or less"), Markets Saturday edition. First-hand (editor-in-chief Sara Kehaulani Goo), June 13, 2022. Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/a-qa-with-axios-sara-kehaulani-goo-on-bundling-newsletters-as-an-audience-engagement-strategy)
- NYT: adds temporary "pop-up" briefings around news events on top of the standing portfolio (the Coronavirus Briefing was built in a few weeks and became a top-five newsletter). First-hand (Adam Pasick, editorial director of newsletters), Feb 10, 2021 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/the-new-york-times-aims-to-convert-newsletter-readers-into-paid-subscribers/)
- Resends, the "refuse" position: Tyler Denk (beehiiv co-founder and CEO, formerly Morning Brew) calls resending to non-openers "truthfully a terrible idea that should be avoided". His reasoning: mailbox providers track engagement over time; a resend to non-openers gets a very low open rate and drags the aggregate down (his example: 10,000 list, 40% first send, 5% on the 6,000-person resend, aggregate 27%), and duplicates invite unsubscribes and spam complaints. He says he has worked with "some of the largest and most successful newsletters in the world" and none considered this strategy. First-hand, post updated July 12, 2026 (the original argument dates from 2022, since Oshinsky cited it in Dec 2022). Source: [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/why-you-shouldn-t-resend-to-subscribers-who-didn-t-open)
- Resends, the "sparingly" position: Dan Oshinsky (Inbox Collective; former director of newsletters at The New Yorker and BuzzFeed): "It's OK to resend a newsletter, but do it sparingly, and only for high-value emails." He would resend for a major announcement needing the widest reach; for a routine newsletter chasing a few hundred or thousand extra opens, "that's probably not the best idea." First-hand consultant view, Dec 1, 2022. Source: [Not a Newsletter, December 2022](https://inboxcollective.com/not-a-newsletter-december-2022/)

### Inferences
- For a weekly essay newsletter the norm among the largest peers is one fixed weekday, one issue (two at most), with the day in the product name or tagline. A two-edition week (main essay + short idea) matches Sahil Bloom and The Marginalian, not an outlier.
- The one documented rule for extra sponsor sends (The Rundown) isolates them on a day with no regular edition and limits them to select partners. That is the closest thing found to a published "extra send" rule.
- Because nobody at 1M+ scale documents routine resends and the one platform-side operator who has seen many large lists argues against them, a resend should be treated as an exception for a launch-level announcement, not a weekly habit.

### Gaps
- Exact send hour and how strictly it is held: not found for Farnam Street, Mark Manson, Tim Ferriss, Sahil Bloom, Lenny. James Clear has only the single third-party timestamp above.
- Total emails per week per subscriber across all products at Morning Brew, The Hustle, NYT, Washington Post, Bloomberg: not found.
- Written rules for launch or promotional extra sends (book launches by Clear, Bloom, Manson; course launches): not found.
- No 1M+ newsletter was found that says it does resend to non-openers, so the "who does" half of the question is unanswered rather than answered "nobody".

## 2. Send time: how it was chosen, time-zone sending, send-time optimization, published tests

### Takeaway
Published evidence on send-time selection at 1M+ newsletters is close to nonexistent. What exists is operational: Morning Brew tested at 5 a.m. and sent the winner an hour later, NYT reported that the "vast majority" of opens land within three hours of the send, and Axios assigns each product a day-part slot.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew: subject-line test goes out at 5 a.m., full send one hour later, which fixes the main send in the early morning (Eastern implied, not stated). Second-hand (AWeber article relaying a tweet by Morning Brew writer Toby Howell, tweet ID dates it to Aug 4, 2020; the AWeber original now returns 404 and was read through a mirror dated April 20, 2021). Old. Sources: [mirror of AWeber post](https://goforwardinternet.blogspot.com/2021/04/how-to-split-test-subject-lines-like.html), [original tweet (not opened)](https://twitter.com/tobydoyhowell/status/1290789622964867072)
- Morning Brew: content is queued at 8:00 or 9:00 p.m. the evening before, and news is monitored overnight. First-hand (Neal Freyman), June 18, 2019 (old). Source: [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-newsletter-writer-with-morning-brews-neal-freyman)
- NYT, The Morning: the "vast majority" of opens happen within the first three hours after sending. First-hand (Adam Pasick, Jordan Cohen), Feb 10, 2021 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/the-new-york-times-aims-to-convert-newsletter-readers-into-paid-subscribers/)
- Axios: send slot is a product-design decision tied to the reader's day (morning, lunch, after market close, evening). First-hand, June 13, 2022. Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/a-qa-with-axios-sara-kehaulani-goo-on-bundling-newsletters-as-an-audience-engagement-strategy)
- beehiiv: a scheduled send uses the time zone of the publisher's browser. Snippet from beehiiv's own blog, not opened; no beehiiv documentation of per-subscriber send-time optimization or recipient-local-time delivery was found. Source: [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/best-times-to-send-a-newsletter.md)

### Inferences
- With most opens inside three hours of the send (NYT), the send time mainly decides which time zones catch the email at a natural reading moment; for a US-heavy list an evening Eastern send lands mid-afternoon on the West Coast.
- Since none of the weekly essay newsletters publishes a send-time test, there is no peer benchmark to copy; a send-time test on one's own list is the only way to get an answer.

### Gaps
- A search summary attributed to Axios the claim that open rates rose "eight percentage points" after adopting personalized send times. The apparent source (a Marigold/Sailthru client page, https://go.meetmarigold.com/clientsuccess/axios) returned 404 on 2026-11-06, so this is unverified and is not counted as a finding.
- No published send-time A/B result from any named 1M+ newsletter was found. No evidence found of time-zone sending at Morning Brew, 1440, The Hustle or the weekly essay newsletters.

## 3. Segmentation of sends: whole list vs engaged tiers, topic and geo, new subscribers, promo pressure caps

### Takeaway
The documented segmentation at 1M+ scale is engagement-based pruning, not content personalization: unengaged subscribers are challenged and removed on short clocks (14 days for new 1440 subscribers, 60 days at Morning Brew, 1 to 2 weeks after sign-up at Axios, "cleaned weekly" at The Rundown), so that "the whole list" is by construction an active list. Promotional pressure is capped through fixed ad slots per issue (1 at Brain Food, 2 at The Rundown, 3 at TLDR) rather than through published frequency caps.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew sunset rule (2019): no open in 60 days triggers a re-engagement email; no response within 48 hours means removal. New subscribers who do not open the first email within three weeks enter a similar 48-hour process with different messaging. 1.6M active subscribers at the time versus 3M+ lifetime sign-ups. First-hand (Tyler Denk, then senior product lead), Oct 15, 2019 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers/)
- Morning Brew earlier rule (2018): no open in six weeks triggers a message from the CEO restating the newsletter's value; Facebook retargeting of lapsed readers was "minimally effective". First-hand (Austin Rief), Nov 5, 2018 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers)
- Morning Brew removed 100,000 inactive users in a re-engagement campaign to lift aggregate open rates and deliverability; the page claims a 125% open-rate improvement and 99% deliverability with no baseline or period. Vendor case study, undated. Source: [Campaign Monitor customer page](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/customers/morning-brew/)
- 1440 new-subscriber rule: new subscribers get 14 days or 10 messages to open an email, after which they are automatically removed; a drip campaign targets subscribers who have temporarily lost interest. First-hand via vendor blog (co-founders Drew Steigerwald and Tim Huelskamp), Oct 1, 2020 (old). Source: [Campaign Monitor blog](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)
- Axios: re-engagement starts as early as one week after sign-up with a multiple-choice question on why the reader has not opened; subscribers with no engagement two weeks after sign-up are asked if they want to stay and removed if they do not answer; average open rate 43% before and after. Second-hand reporting, Oct 15, 2019 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers/)
- Axios treats engaged and unengaged readers as "two distinct cohorts that you act upon in different ways" and does not send identical campaigns to zero-percent and hundred-percent openers. First-hand (Rameez Tase, VP of growth), June 10, 2019 (old). Source: [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2019/06/be-smart-how-axios-drives-engagement-with-its-email-newsletters-through-user-level-data/)
- The Rundown AI: "Lists are cleaned weekly." Sales page, read 2026-11-06. Source: [therundown.ai/advertise-with-us](https://www.therundown.ai/advertise-with-us)
- SmartBrief (275 newsletters): automated drip starts at 30 days without an open, and re-engagement effort is concentrated on the top 50 revenue newsletters. First-hand (Joe Webster, VP marketing and audience development), Oct 15, 2019 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers/)
- Reactivation defaults from Dan Oshinsky: start with readers who have not opened in 90+ days (about 60 days for daily senders, about 30 days for sign-ups from paid ads or giveaways); at least three emails over about two weeks, five to seven days apart, from a different sender address; remove non-responders; expect to win back 5-15% (2-5% on a first campaign). Consultant guidance, April 5, 2023. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)
- Topic segmentation by portfolio: TLDR runs separate lists per topic (Tech 1.6M, AI about 1.1M, plus Data, IT, Hardware, Fintech, Dev, InfoSec, Product, Founders, DevOps, Marketing, Design, Crypto), and The Rundown runs AI, Tech and Robotics as separate newsletters. Sales pages, read 2026-11-06. Sources: [advertise.tldr.tech](https://advertise.tldr.tech/), [therundown.ai/advertise-with-us](https://www.therundown.ai/advertise-with-us)
- Ad-slot caps per issue: Brain Food "One sponsor per newsletter"; The Rundown one Main Ad plus one Secondary Ad; TLDR "Only 3 advertisers max in every newsletter". Sales pages, read 2026-11-06. Sources: [fs.blog/sponsor](https://fs.blog/sponsor), [therundown.ai/advertise-with-us](https://www.therundown.ai/advertise-with-us), [advertise.tldr.tech](https://advertise.tldr.tech/)
- Dynamic content: Morning Brew used dynamic content blocks to personalize sharing (referral) links per subscriber. Vendor case study, undated. Source: [Campaign Monitor customer page](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/customers/morning-brew/)

### Inferences
- The large dailies shortened the clock most for brand-new subscribers (2 to 3 weeks) and gave established readers longer (6 weeks to 60 days). For a weekly newsletter the equivalent windows have to be measured in issues, not days: 10 unopened messages (1440's rule) is about two weeks of a daily but ten weeks of a weekly.
- All of these rules were written when opens were trustworthy. Since Apple MPP an "opened in the last N days" rule keeps Apple Mail users forever, so the same policies now need a click or reply signal for that share of the list (Oshinsky's 2023 article recommends in-newsletter click prompts for ambiguous Apple readers, same source as above).

### Gaps
- Not found for any named newsletter: sending regular editions only to an engaged tier (as opposed to pruning), geo editions, excluding recent purchasers or paying members from promotional sends, numeric frequency caps on promotional or dedicated emails per subscriber per month.
- Current (2024 to 2026) sunset rules for Morning Brew, 1440, The Hustle and theSkimm: not found; everything above for them is 2018 to 2020.
- Sunset or cleaning rules for James Clear, Farnam Street, Mark Manson, Sahil Bloom, Tim Ferriss, Lenny: not found.

## 4. Testing: subject lines, preheaders, From name, content, holdouts, winning metric since Apple MPP

### Takeaway
The one specific large-newsletter test protocol on record is Morning Brew's: four subject lines, about 3% of the list each, sent at 5 a.m., winner to the remaining 88% an hour later (second-hand, 2020). The Hustle's process could not be verified from any live primary source. Platform defaults in 2025 to 2026 still pick winners on opens (beehiiv) or on an unspecified "best-performing" measure (Substack).

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew: every morning four subject lines, taken from catchy phrases in that day's issue, each go to about 3% of the list at 5 a.m.; the winner goes to the remaining 88% one hour later. List was 2.5M+ at the time, so each cell was roughly 75,000 people. The article describes the winner as the line with more opens; Morning Brew's own stated metric is not quoted. Second-hand (AWeber relaying a tweet by Morning Brew writer Toby Howell; the mirror text misspells him "Roby Howell", the linked handle is @tobydoyhowell; tweet ID dates to Aug 4, 2020). Old, pre-MPP. Sources: [mirror of AWeber post, April 20, 2021](https://goforwardinternet.blogspot.com/2021/04/how-to-split-test-subject-lines-like.html), [original tweet (not opened)](https://twitter.com/tobydoyhowell/status/1290789622964867072)
- beehiiv A/B test mechanics (the reader's platform; used by several 1M+ newsletters): subject lines only (preview text "remains consistent across all subject line variations"); up to four subject lines; test audience set as a percentage, default 10%; duration default 60 minutes, selectable from 5 to 240 minutes; "Once you've set the duration and begun the A/B test, it cannot be ended early"; beehiiv looks for a statistically significant winner and otherwise sends the subject line with the most opens; a tie goes to the original. Platform doc, updated Oct 1, 2026. Source: [beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/9479415454615-creating-and-using-a-b-tests-in-your-beehiiv-posts)
- Substack "title test" (announced Aug 6, 2025): test multiple titles and/or subtitles on a chosen share of subscribers, equal-sized cohorts, default duration 1 hour, winner sent automatically to the rest or picked manually; eligibility 200+ subscribers; "In nearly 60% of tests, a title other than the original came out on top." The announcement does not say whether opens or clicks decide. Platform announcement. Source: [Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/new-on-substack-ab-testing-for-headlines)
- The Hustle: a HubSpot blog post titled "How The Hustle got 43,876 more clicks" existed but now redirects to a dead page (404 on 2026-11-06). A third-party summary says the test optimized "one specific element", was judged on clicks, and that The Hustle tested subject lines, preview text, send times, content formats and CTAs one variable at a time; it gives no variants, sample sizes or windows. Second-hand, undated, unverifiable against the original. Source: [Digital Thrive summary](https://www.digitalthriveai.com/en-ca/resources/email-marketing/how-the-hustle-got-43876-more-clicks/)
- From-name testing: Oshinsky recommends A/B testing the sent-from name ("Until you run some A/B tests on this, you won't know which sent-from name" gets opens). Consultant guidance, Jan 2, 2023. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/30-ideas-to-improve-your-newsletter-this-year/)
- Product-format testing: 1440 tested a Weekend Edition with a thumbs up/down poll that routed thumbs-up voters to a survey on what they liked and thumbs-down voters to a suggestions prompt. Second-hand, Oct 2, 2024. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-use-in-newsletter-polls-to-engage-with-and-learn-from-your-audience/)
- Farnam Street tested pop-up wording on its site and suspected pop-up sign-ups contributed to the open rate slipping from 45% to about 40%. First-hand (Shane Parrish annual letter), Dec 20, 2019 (old). Source: [fs.blog 2019 annual letter](https://fs.blog/2019/12/2019-annual-letter/)
- NYT, The Morning: subject lines are typically no more than four words after the newsletter title. Snippet of a vendor's outside analysis, not an NYT statement. Source: [Campaign Monitor blog](https://campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/what-makes-nyts-the-morning-newsletter-so-successful)
- Winning metric after MPP: Tyler Denk listed the metrics left to newsletters as "Clickthrough, open and unsubscribe". First-hand, Sept 17, 2021. Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/dont-freak-out-yet-publishers-brace-for-ios-changes-to-their-newsletter-businesses/)

### Inferences
- Morning Brew's design (12% of the list in test, 1-hour window) and beehiiv's default (10%, 60 minutes, up to four variants) are nearly the same protocol, so the beehiiv defaults are a reasonable stand-in for "how the big ones do it". On a 500k list, 10% split four ways is 12,500 per cell.
- Opens remain the default winner metric even in 2026 platform docs, which means MPP-inflated Apple opens add noise equally to all cells but do not bias the comparison; the larger risk is the short window, since a 60-minute test only measures early openers.
- beehiiv's rule that a started test cannot be ended early means the test duration is also a forced scheduling buffer: the full send goes out at schedule time plus the test window.

### Gaps
- The Hustle's subject-line process (variants, sample, window, metric): not found in any live primary source. A Mailmodo bulletin is reported (snippet only) to quote Sam Parr saying subject-line testing is partly about which lines reach the inbox, not only which get clicked; not verified.
- Preheader tests, content or layout tests, holdout groups, and any explicit "we pick on clicks since MPP" statement from a named 1M+ newsletter: not found.
- Whether Morning Brew still runs the 2020 protocol: not found.

## 5. Pre-send QA: checklists, test sends, rendering, clipping, approvals, corrections, buffers

### Takeaway
No 1M+ newsletter has published its pre-send checklist. What is documented: Morning Brew finishes writing by 4:30 p.m., edits into the evening and queues the issue at 8 to 9 p.m. for an early-morning send (a buffer of roughly 9 hours), 1440 gets a daily automated report on its sending backend, and the NYT runs newsletter launches from "a checklist and a playbook". Correction norms come from Dan Oshinsky: fix small errors quietly or to the affected readers only, apologize fully and personally for big ones.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew daily timeline: start about 1:00 p.m. for the next day; writing finished by 4:30 p.m.; "a decent amount of editing, and more editing"; staff leave around 7:00 to 7:30 p.m. (about six hours end to end); issue queued at 8:00 or 9:00 p.m.; news monitored overnight; three people on the daily newsletter at the time. First-hand (Neal Freyman), June 18, 2019 (old). Source: [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-newsletter-writer-with-morning-brews-neal-freyman)
- Morning Brew has a dedicated copy editor role for "proofreading and fact checking" the newsletters against the style guide, working with the writers and the managing editor. Snippet of a job posting, not opened, undated. Source: [Jobspresso listing](https://jobspresso.co/job/copy-editor-6/)
- 1440: built an internal interface to monitor its email program with the vendor's deliverability team; "Each morning, the team receives an email with an overview of their sending backend" so technical issues are caught early. Steigerwald calls deliverability "the lifeblood of our business". First-hand via vendor blog, Oct 1, 2020 (old). Source: [Campaign Monitor blog](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)
- NYT: for launching pop-up newsletters, "a checklist and a playbook means we don't have to reinvent the wheel every time" (Adam Pasick); the Times had upgraded its CMS and newsletter tools. First-hand, Feb 10, 2021 (old). The checklist itself is not published. Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/the-new-york-times-aims-to-convert-newsletter-readers-into-paid-subscribers/)
- Correction policy, Oshinsky's rules: minor mistakes (typo, broken link, wrong time) get a short, quick correction in the newsletter's own voice, with a plain subject such as "Corrected link" (The Colorado Sun) or "Correction"; not every error needs a broadcast; major mistakes get a full apology sent from a named person, apologizing in the first sentence, explaining what went wrong and how it will be made right, with contact details. Consultant guidance, Sept 21, 2022. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-send-an-apology-email/)
- Correction examples in the same article: at The New Yorker, after an "it's/its" typo in a holiday send drew dozens of complaints, Oshinsky did not email the full list ("felt odd to call attention to a typo") and apologized only to readers who wrote in; Tangle (Isaac Saul) accidentally sent a draft and followed with a brief apology plus the final version; Hatch sent a humorous apology the next day ("Whoops, we need a nap"). First-hand for The New Yorker case, Sept 21, 2022. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-send-an-apology-email/)
- Mis-send precedents at large publishers: NYT accidentally emailed over 8 million people in Dec 2011 and followed up saying the message was "sent by us in error"; The Guardian apologized after a newsletter went to non-subscribers, citing an email-system error. Snippets, not opened; the NYT case is very old. Sources: [TheJournal.ie](https://thejournal.ie/new-york-times-accidentally-sends-email-to-over-8-milllion-readers-315593-Dec2011), [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/375623/None)
- Generic, not from a 1M+ operator: Litmus's pre-send checklist ("29 Things to Check Before You Send") includes confirming merge tags and fallbacks and keeping the HTML under Gmail's 102 KB clipping limit; a clipped email hides the footer and unsubscribe link. Source: [Litmus](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-email-checklist-24-things-to-check-before-you-send/)

### Inferences
- The documented Morning Brew pattern is "content locked the evening before, send automated the next morning", which gives a multi-hour window to catch errors and is what makes a pre-send A/B test possible. A weekly essay newsletter can afford a longer lock (for example a full day).
- The targeted-apology rule (reply only to those who noticed a minor error) matters more at scale: a correction email to 500k people is itself an extra send with its own unsubscribes.

### Gaps
- A published pre-send checklist from any named 1M+ newsletter: not found.
- Use of Litmus or Email on Acid, dark-mode checks, link-checking tools, clipping checks, or named sign-off steps at Morning Brew, The Hustle, 1440, theSkimm, James Clear, Farnam Street, Sahil Bloom, Lenny: not found.
- A Tiny (TinyMCE) vendor case study says Morning Brew built its editor on TinyMCE to save formatting hours; only the title was seen, details not verified ([casestudies.com listing](https://www.casestudies.com/company/tiny/case-study/how-morning-brew-saved-its-writers-dozens-of-wasted-hours-per-week-with-tiny)).

## 6. Preference centers and multi-newsletter portfolios: opt-down, pause, unsubscribe pages, auto-enrollment

### Takeaway
Among the essay-style newsletters the documented patterns are a per-product unsubscribe (James Clear lets readers leave 3-2-1 only or all email) and a one-click 30-day pause (Farnam Street, 2019). Among publishers, Axios bundled several newsletters into a single sign-up and merged lists without a large drop-off. ## 7. Metrics and reporting: KPIs since Apple MPP, dashboards, published benchmarks

### Takeaway
On the record, the large newsletters still manage to and sell on opens: Morning Brew's north star was total unique opens, Farnam Street tracks "effective readers" (subscribers times open rate), 1440 prices ads on opens, and in 2023 publishers told Digiday their advertisers rely on open rates and subscriber cohorts rather than click-through rates. Self-reported open rates on 2024 to 2026 sales pages cluster at 45-55%; published click rates are rare (The Rundown: 2.5%).

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew: north-star metric is total unique opens, "the only metric that really matters"; "we don't care about the vanity metric of total subscribers"; "We really care about the total opens." First-hand (Tyler Denk), Oct 2019 (old, pre-MPP). Sources: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers/), [Talking Biz News summary, Oct 17, 2019](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/how-morning-brew-focuses-on-total-opens-not-total-subscribers/)
- Farnam Street: "effective readers" = subscribers multiplied by average open rate (2018: 185,000 x 0.45 = 83,250; 2019: 250,000 x 0.40 = 100,000), so effective readers grew about 20% while headline subscribers grew about 35%. First-hand, Dec 20, 2019 (old). Source: [fs.blog 2019 annual letter](https://fs.blog/2019/12/2019-annual-letter/)
- 1440: "We sell ads based on how many subscribers open the email"; a 4-5% open-rate gain meant a 4-5% revenue gain; "a consistent 55%+ average open rate". First-hand via vendor blog, Oct 1, 2020 (old). Source: [Campaign Monitor blog](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)
- 1440: about $0.05 of revenue per daily email open; about 3M subscribers; 14 people; over $1M revenue per employee; 75% margin before growth spend ("EBITDAG"). Second-hand interview write-up with Huelskamp quotes, Oct 10, 2023. Source: [The Rebooting](https://www.therebooting.com/p/newsletter-moneyball)
- 1440: about 3.3M subscribers; internal bar of 40%+ open rate, with the article reporting 60%+ from week one onward; advice to reach 45-50% open rate before buying ads; acquisition judged on CPA with CPM, CTR and CVR as drivers; subscribers acquired from other newsletters open at about 2x the rate of paid-social subscribers at about 2x the CPA; mix about 30% organic, 70% paid. First-hand (Tim Huelskamp), Feb 4, 2024. Source: [Newsletter Circle](https://newslettercircle.beehiiv.com/p/1440-newsletter-circle-interview)
- Axios: core acquisition metric is cost per engaged email acquired (CPEEA): "Getting an email address is the lead, converting that email to an engaged subscriber is the goal" (punctuation adapted); LTV adopted as a core strategic measure; time spent deliberately de-emphasized; 45% average open rate and a 10% survey response rate across 19 newsletters. Stack: Sailthru API for sending and data, BigQuery and Tableau for user-level views. First-hand (Rameez Tase), June 10, 2019 (old). Source: [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2019/06/be-smart-how-axios-drives-engagement-with-its-email-newsletters-through-user-level-data/)
- Axios later benchmarks: 43% average open rate (Oct 2019); about 2.5M email subscribers with an average open rate of "about 41%" (June 2022). Sources: [Digiday 2019](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers/), [Digiday 2022](https://digiday.com/media/a-qa-with-axios-sara-kehaulani-goo-on-bundling-newsletters-as-an-audience-engagement-strategy)
- NYT: reports unique opens and total opens (The Morning passed 1 billion unique opens between May 2020 and Jan 2021; 3.6 billion newsletter opens in 2020, up 150% on 2019). First-hand, Feb 10, 2021 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/the-new-york-times-aims-to-convert-newsletter-readers-into-paid-subscribers/)
- Advertisers and opens after MPP: executives at Front Office Sports, The Gist and Industry Dive said their advertisers generally do not rely on click-through rates and instead rely on open rates and newsletter subscriber cohorts; all three track clicks with UTMs (Industry Dive's CTO Eli Dickinson: "confident we'll be able to track clicks the same way we always do"). First-hand quotes, July 28, 2023. Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/some-publishers-dont-think-apples-ios17-update-will-impact-their-link-tracking-in-newsletters/)
- Prediction that did not clearly come true: Tyler Denk expected newsletters to move pricing to "a flat rate of CPM based on list size or set up a CPC model". First-hand, Sept 17, 2021. Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/dont-freak-out-yet-publishers-brace-for-ios-changes-to-their-newsletter-businesses/)
- Self-reported open and click benchmarks on current sales pages (read 2026-11-06): James Clear 47-49% open ([source](https://jamesclear.com/sponsor)); Brain Food 45-48% open ([source](https://fs.blog/sponsor)); Mark Manson 55% open ([source](https://markmanson.net/sponsor)); The Rundown AI 54.8% open and 2.5% click on the main newsletter, 57.8% and 1.4% on Tech, 55.2% and 1.6% on Robotics ([source](https://www.therundown.ai/advertise-with-us)); TLDR Tech 47% open, 41% average across its newsletters with a range of 32% to 47% ([source](https://advertise.tldr.tech/)).
- Survey response benchmark: 2-5% is "pretty good" for most newsletter surveys. Consultant guidance, Oct 2, 2024. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-use-in-newsletter-polls-to-engage-with-and-learn-from-your-audience/)

### Inferences
- The 45-55% open rates on sales pages are post-MPP figures that include Apple machine opens, so they are not comparable with the 40-45% figures from 2019. None of the pages says whether machine opens are filtered.
- The only click-rate benchmarks at 1M+ scale found are The Rundown's (1.4% to 2.5% of recipients). Its ad click ranges (2,000 to 4,000 clicks on 900k to 1M impressions) imply a main-ad click rate of roughly 0.2% to 0.4% of impressions, which is a usable yardstick for sponsor reporting.
- Morning Brew's and Farnam Street's metrics are the same idea (subscribers x open rate = readers reached); both reward list quality over list size.

### Gaps
- Dashboards, weekly review rituals, 30-day-active definitions, click-to-open targets, reply counts, NPS, read-time or cohort churn KPIs at any named 1M+ newsletter: not found.
- Open and click rates for Sahil Bloom, Tim Ferriss, Lenny, Morning Brew (current), The Hustle, theSkimm: not found from a primary source (a third-party post claims Sahil Bloom said "over 50%" at about 500k subscribers, unsourced).

## 8. Reader feedback loops: end-of-email polls, reply prompts, surveys, editorial use

### Takeaway
Documented loops at scale: 1440 uses thumbs up/down polls with branched follow-up surveys to evaluate new formats, The Rundown runs a star-rating poll and advertises the tally, and Mark Manson builds reader-submitted stories into every issue and surveys his list for demographics. How ratings feed editorial decisions is rarely spelled out.

### Cited Findings
- 1440: thumbs up/down on its Weekend Edition test; thumbs-up leads to a survey on what readers liked, thumbs-down asks for suggestions. Second-hand, Oct 2, 2024. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-use-in-newsletter-polls-to-engage-with-and-learn-from-your-audience/)
- 1440: the team gathers "hours of customer feedback" and asks readers how to improve (2020); continuously asks for feedback and iterates (2024). First-hand, Oct 1, 2020 and Feb 4, 2024. Sources: [Campaign Monitor blog](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/), [Newsletter Circle](https://newslettercircle.beehiiv.com/p/1440-newsletter-circle-interview)
- The Rundown AI: reader poll with "50,000+ 5-star ratings", used as social proof on the advertising page. Sales page, read 2026-11-06. Source: [therundown.ai/advertise-with-us](https://www.therundown.ai/advertise-with-us)
- Mark Manson: every issue includes reader "breakthrough" stories (over 15,000 received); reader demographics come from an internal survey run in June 2024. Sales page. Source: [markmanson.net/sponsor](https://markmanson.net/sponsor)
- Axios: 10% survey response rate. First-hand, June 10, 2019 (old). Source: [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2019/06/be-smart-how-axios-drives-engagement-with-its-email-newsletters-through-user-level-data/)
- Group Nine Media: after MPP, "heavily focusing on in-email engagement", running surveys inside its emails and considering running them more often (Noah Keil, EVP growth). First-hand, Sept 17, 2021. Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/dont-freak-out-yet-publishers-brace-for-ios-changes-to-their-newsletter-businesses/)
- Poll design guidance from Oshinsky: the common closer is "What did you think of today's newsletter?" with thumbs or a 1-to-5 star scale; one in-newsletter poll means one question, so pick it deliberately; a yes/no poll can also tag readers for segmentation. Consultant guidance, Oct 2, 2024. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-use-in-newsletter-polls-to-engage-with-and-learn-from-your-audience/)
- Reader-survey use: ask readers to describe in one sentence why they like the newsletter and reuse the language in marketing (example: The London Free Press). Consultant guidance, Jan 2, 2023; example is not a 1M+ newsletter. Source: [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/30-ideas-to-improve-your-newsletter-this-year/)

### Inferences
- After MPP, a poll click is one of the few clean per-subscriber engagement signals, which is why Group Nine framed surveys as a measurement tool, not only a feedback tool. A rating poll therefore doubles as input for sunset rules.
- 1440's branching (different follow-up for positive and negative votes) is the most specific documented design and is cheap to copy.

### Gaps
- How Morning Brew, The Hustle, TLDR, theSkimm, James Clear, Farnam Street, Sahil Bloom or Lenny use poll ratings or annual surveys editorially: not found.
- Reply-prompt practices and reply volumes at 1M+ newsletters: not found.

## 11. Monetizing the list without burning it: paid-tier and membership promotion to free subscribers

## 12. Where operators disagree

### Takeaway
The clearest disagreements are on resending to non-openers (never vs sparingly), whether re-engagement campaigns are worth running at all, how fast to cut unengaged subscribers, how many ads an issue should carry, and whether opens remain the currency.

### Cited Findings
- Resend to non-openers. Against: Tyler Denk, "truthfully a terrible idea that should be avoided" (updated July 12, 2026) ([beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/why-you-shouldn-t-resend-to-subscribers-who-didn-t-open)). Conditional: Dan Oshinsky, "It's OK to resend a newsletter, but do it sparingly, and only for high-value emails" (Dec 1, 2022) ([Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/not-a-newsletter-december-2022/)).
- Is re-engagement worth it. Yes: SmartBrief's Joe Webster, "The majority of our revenue comes from ads in newsletters, so reengagement is worth it for us"; Oshinsky, re-engaging is "essentially free" versus $0.25 to $3 to acquire a subscriber. No: The Wall Street Journal's Annemarie Dooling found fewer than 1% of subscribers put through a campaign re-engaged ("less than 1,000 for a huge list") and judged it not worth the effort; an unnamed executive said disengaged subscribers rarely become the most engaged users. All Oct 15, 2019 (old). Source: [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers/)
- How fast to cut. 1440: 14 days or 10 messages for new subscribers (2020) ([Campaign Monitor blog](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)). Axios: challenge at one week, remove at two weeks after sign-up (2019); Morning Brew: 60 days for established readers, three weeks for new ones (2019) ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers/)). Oshinsky's default: 90+ days, 60 for dailies (2023) ([Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)).
- Ad load. One sponsor per issue: Farnam Street ([fs.blog/sponsor](https://fs.blog/sponsor)). Two: The Rundown ([source](https://www.therundown.ai/advertise-with-us)). Up to three: TLDR ([source](https://advertise.tldr.tech/)). Matt McGarry (Newsletter Operator) is reported in a podcast snippet as calling one ad per newsletter "a really bad business model"; snippet only, not verified, source page not identified, so treat as a lead rather than a finding.
- Opens as the currency. For: Morning Brew's "only metric that really matters" (2019) ([Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/how-morning-brew-focuses-on-total-opens-not-total-subscribers/)); 1440 sells on opens (2020) ([Campaign Monitor blog](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)); advertisers still rely on open rates and cohorts (July 2023) ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/some-publishers-dont-think-apples-ios17-update-will-impact-their-link-tracking-in-newsletters/)). Against: Denk predicted a shift to flat CPM on list size or CPC (Sept 2021) ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/dont-freak-out-yet-publishers-brace-for-ios-changes-to-their-newsletter-businesses/)).
- Pause instead of unsubscribe. For: Farnam Street's one-click 30-day break (2019) ([fs.blog 2019 annual letter](https://fs.blog/2019/12/2019-annual-letter/)). Against (generic, not a 1M+ operator): Parcel found 98% of people offered a pause did not use it and removed the feature (snippet) ([Parcel blog](https://parcel.io/blog/pause-emails)).
- Subject-line test sample size. Morning Brew: 12% of the list in four 3% cells (2020) ([mirror of AWeber post](https://goforwardinternet.blogspot.com/2021/04/how-to-split-test-subject-lines-like.html)); beehiiv default: 10% ([beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/9479415454615-creating-and-using-a-b-tests-in-your-beehiiv-posts)); Substack is reported by third parties (unverified) to recommend about 20% for larger lists ([Substack announcement, which does not state the figure](https://on.substack.com/p/new-on-substack-ab-testing-for-headlines)).

### Inferences
- The resend disagreement is narrower than it looks: both positions reject routine resends of a regular issue; they differ only on a rare, high-stakes announcement.
- The sunset disagreement tracks business model: ad-priced-on-opens dailies (1440, Morning Brew) cut fastest because every unopened send dilutes the number they sell; a consultant serving mixed clients sets a slower default.
- The WSJ and SmartBrief results are not contradictory: WSJ ran win-backs on lists "several years old and never cleaned", while SmartBrief and Oshinsky target the 30-day mark. Timing, not the tactic, explains the gap.

### Gaps
- No 2024 to 2026 first-hand statement was found from a 1M+ operator on any of these disputes except Denk's resend post; most positions are from 2018 to 2023 and may have shifted.
- Newsletters in scope for which no send-operations practice at all was found in this pass: Milk Road, The Daily Upside, The Neuron, Robinhood Snacks/Sherwood, Bloomberg, Workweek, Wait But Why. Industry Dive, theSkimm, Washington Post and Tim Ferriss yielded only the single items cited above.
