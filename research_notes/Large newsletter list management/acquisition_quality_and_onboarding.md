# Acquisition quality control and onboarding at 1M+ subscriber newsletters (researched 2026-11-06)

How to read these notes. Every finding carries a source link, the source date and a reliability tag: [first-hand] = the operator's own statement or the vendor's own documentation; [second-hand] = a journalist or third party reporting it; [vendor] = vendor case study or sponsored content; [snippet] = seen only in a search-result summary, the page itself was not opened, so treat as unverified; [generic] = generic source, not from a 1M+ operator. Anything dated before 2022 is flagged "older, possibly outdated". Open-based rules from 2018 to 2020 predate Apple Mail Privacy Protection (Sept 2021), which inflates opens, so the same rule today is looser than it was when stated.

## 1. Sign-up validation: single vs double opt-in, real-time protections, bots and list-bombing

### Takeaway
There is no consensus. The growth-agency school (Matt McGarry) rejects double opt-in because 20% to 50% of sign-ups never confirm, and replaces it with email validation plus removal of non-openers after 30 to 60 days; Morning Brew added double opt-in in 2019 specifically to verify referral sign-ups, HubSpot Media (The Hustle) currently sends a "Confirm my Subscription" email, and Kit defaults to double opt-in. No named 1M+ newsletter was found stating which real-time verification vendor it uses; the documented protections come from the vendors and consultants that serve them (beehiiv's automatic address verification, SparkLoop's anti-fraud checks, Oshinsky's ZeroBounce / reCAPTCHA / honeypot advice).

### Cited Findings

Double opt-in versus single opt-in

- Morning Brew: began requiring double opt-in in 2019 "to ensure only legitimate referrals get counted", and saw "a conversion rate of over 85%" afterwards. Source: [Referral Rock, published 2023-02-08, updated 2023-08-01](https://referralrock.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program/) [second-hand, no primary source given].
- Morning Brew: after adding double opt-in to verify referred subscribers, the company said referred readers were higher quality and clicked more. Source: [Digiday, Kayleigh Barber, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/) [second-hand reporting of the company's statement; older, possibly outdated].
- Morning Brew: a different third-party account says sign-up conversions dropped 15% after the change; another says Tyler Denk described the change as appeasing "the email gods" and that "Providing free rewards and incentives always invites nefarious actors". Sources: [ReferralCandy](https://www.referralcandy.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program) [snippet, second-hand]; [AppSumo blog](https://blog.appsumo.com/?p=3431) [snippet; the page refused connection when fetched]. The 15% drop and the "over 85%" figure are not reconciled by either source.
- Morning Brew, general sign-ups: in Aug 2023 the confirmation page read "You're in!" and told readers to "expect us in your inbox bright and early", with a welcome email following (no confirm-your-email step described). Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-08-25](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-newsletter-welcome-email-guide) [second-hand observation of the live flow].
- The Hustle / HubSpot Media: an archived Day-0 email from HubSpot Media headed "One more click and you're in" thanks the reader for signing up to "The Hustle, Marketing Against the Grain, and The Science of Scaling", says "we just need one quick click to confirm you're in" and carries a "Confirm my Subscription" button. Source: [Sequenzy email archive, undated](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/hubspot/emails/one-more-click-and-you-re-in) [second-hand archive of a first-hand email; date unknown]. In Aug 2023 the same flow was observed with a "you're in" confirmation page and a welcome email, with no confirm step described. Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-08-25](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-newsletter-welcome-email-guide).
- Robinhood Snacks: confirmation page says "You're subscribed!", no welcome email at all, first email is the next day's regular edition. Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-08-25](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-newsletter-welcome-email-guide) [second-hand observation].
- The New York Times: signing up for The Morning requires creating an account "with an email address or through single sign-on (SSO)" (Google, Facebook or Apple). Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-03-24](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/sign-up-flows-across-five-companies) [second-hand observation].
- Matt McGarry (Newsletter Operator, GrowLetter agency): "By using a double opt-in you'll lose 20%-40% of subscribers"; do not use it; send to everyone and "remove non-openers after 30-60 days". Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-02-18](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/easiest-way-to-increase-open-rate) [first-hand]. Repeated in [Newsletter Operator, 2023-10-14](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/subscriber-flow).
- Matt McGarry, for paid cohorts: "Don't use double opt-in (20-50% won't confirm)." Source: [Newsletter Operator, Paid Acquisition Crash Course, 2023-04-08](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/paid-acquisition-crash-course) [first-hand]. Note the range differs from his 20%-40% elsewhere.
- Dan Oshinsky (Inbox Collective; formerly BuzzFeed and The New Yorker): no single answer. At best-in-class organizations about 80% to 90% of sign-ups confirm; at average organizations closer to 50%. Double opt-in is mandatory with some ESPs and in some countries (he cites Germany). He suggests double opt-in for sweepstakes and giveaway entrants even when the rest of the site is single opt-in, because those people often only want the prize (he describes a Zapier rule that sends the confirmation only to those entries in Mailchimp). If double opt-in is used for everyone, add "sniper links" that open the confirmation email directly; Growth Design reported a 7% lift in confirmations. Source: [Inbox Collective, 2023-06-14](https://inboxcollective.com/should-i-use-single-opt-in-or-double-opt-in-for-my-newsletter/) [first-hand from the consultant; the page itself warns it is more than two years old].
- beehiiv's stated position: "Neither option is right or wrong (except when double opt-in is legally required)"; enable it if "security is your highest priority" or you market to a country that requires it. It cites Arnold's Pump Club as using a reply-to-engage ask instead of double opt-in. Source: [beehiiv blog, Robert Hanson and Lenore Sterner, 2024-06-25](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/single-opt-in-vs-double-opt-in) [first-hand vendor].
- beehiiv product behavior: with double opt-in on, the subscriber is "Pending" and receives nothing, including the welcome email, until they click; "Smart Nudge" sends one reminder about 48 hours later; "It is normal for some subscribers to remain in Pending status indefinitely"; the setting can also be applied per embedded form, and per subscriber through the API parameter `double_opt_override` (`on`, `off`, `not_set`); beehiiv calls double opt-in plus Smart Nudge "a best practice for email deliverability, and not just a security measure". Source: [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-09](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/13081072798743-how-to-enable-double-opt-in-and-smart-nudge) [first-hand vendor].
- Kit (ConvertKit; platform of James Clear and others): double opt-in is the default and recommended "on all of your forms and landing pages"; single opt-in is appropriate "Very rarely, if ever" (examples given: a live event, a form shared with a select group); it "protects against fake Subscribers, spammers, and bots designed to attack your deliverability". Source: [Kit help center, 2026-06-25](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2971364-the-all-important-double-opt-in) [first-hand vendor].

Real-time protections

- Dan Oshinsky: double opt-in alone does not stop bots, because confirmation emails still go to fake addresses. Use one of three safeguards on the form: (1) third-party verification, naming ZeroBounce (also corrects typos and blocks spam traps) and noting that beehiiv verifies addresses automatically; (2) reCAPTCHA, which he uses on his own site; (3) a honeypot hidden field, with any address that fills it suppressed or deleted. He cites a BuzzFeed spambot incident as the motivating case. Source: [Inbox Collective, 2023-06-14](https://inboxcollective.com/should-i-use-single-opt-in-or-double-opt-in-for-my-newsletter/) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry: email validation tools can block bots from joining the list (stated as the alternative to double opt-in; no tool named). Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-02-18](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/easiest-way-to-increase-open-rate) [first-hand].
- SparkLoop (partner network used by large newsletters): every referred subscriber must pass anti-fraud checks "including not already being on your list, burner domains, and 100+ other variables", be in an eligible country, stay active (no bounce, no unsubscribe) through the screening period and meet a minimum number of opens or clicks. Source: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-05-26](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/6816463-managing-subscribers-referred-via-your-partner-program) [first-hand vendor].
- beehiiv paid recommendations (formerly Boosts): "Subscriber verification uses a range of signals, including email engagement, geolocation, and behavioral data"; "specific methods aren't disclosed". Source: [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-20](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/14194737991319-faqs-about-grow-and-monetize-boosts) [first-hand vendor].
- beehiiv imports: "beehiiv automatically filters out duplicate, invalid, and inactive emails to keep your list clean"; filtered addresses show as Rejected in the import history. Source: [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-01](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/12231316510871) [first-hand vendor].
- Meta lead-form ads: Manny Reyes (Growth Levers; says he drove 5M+ newsletter subscribers with Meta ads for Morning Brew, Workweek, The Daily Upside and James Clear) says to stop using lead forms, because Meta autofills the email the user "created their Facebook or Instagram account with", often an old address, so "You now have a subscriber who may never see your newsletter"; send ad traffic to a landing page where people type their address ("That friction is a good thing"). "Every newsletter operator I have convinced to make this switch has seen better engagement from those readers." No numbers given. Source: [Growth Levers, 2026-06-06](https://growth-levers.beehiiv.com/p/use-lead-forms-if-you-hate-money) [first-hand from an operator-side media buyer].
- Matt McGarry on X ads: landing-page conversion is very low, "likely due to bots". Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-09-23](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/the-best-paid-acquisition-channels) [first-hand].
- City Cast: sign-up asks users to "confirm humanity" via reCAPTCHA. Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-03-24](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/sign-up-flows-across-five-companies) [second-hand; City Cast is a local network, not confirmed at 1M+].
- [generic, not from a 1M+ operator] A developer reports a bot added about 5,000 addresses to his newsletter despite double opt-in, concludes "Double opt-in and bot protection solve two different problems", and notes a Turnstile widget in the browser is not protection on its own because a bot can call the sign-up endpoint directly; his fix was server-side verification plus rate limiting. Source: [flaviocopes.com, date not captured](https://flaviocopes.com/newsletter-spam-attack.md) [snippet].

### Inferences
- The operators who refuse double opt-in do not skip the filtering; they move it after the first sends (remove never-openers at 2 to 8 weeks). Double opt-in and early pruning are substitutes for the same job. The pruning route keeps the 20% to 50% who would not confirm but might still read; the double opt-in route keeps them from ever touching sender reputation.
- The documented pattern is selective double opt-in: single opt-in for people who typed their address into a newsletter form, confirmation only for incentive-driven or low-intent entry points (Morning Brew referrals, Oshinsky's giveaway entrants). A quiz or tool that captures an email "to receive results" is closer to the second group than the first.
- The Hustle seems to have moved from a plain "you're in" flow (observed Aug 2023) to a confirm-subscription email (undated archive that mentions the newer HubSpot newsletter "The Science of Scaling"). The direction of the change is an inference from two observations, not a stated policy.
- beehiiv's per-form and per-API-call double opt-in switches make a selective policy implementable on beehiiv without turning it on publication-wide.

### Gaps
- No explicit single/double opt-in statement found for 1440, theSkimm, Milk Road, The Rundown AI, The Neuron, TLDR, The Daily Upside, Sherwood, Axios, The Washington Post, Industry Dive, Workweek, James Clear, Sahil Bloom or Lenny's Newsletter: "not found".
- No named 1M+ newsletter found stating that it uses Kickbox, ZeroBounce, NeverBounce, BriteVerify, Cloudflare Turnstile, disposable-domain blocking or typo correction. Only the consultant and vendor statements above.
- No account found of a 1M+ newsletter handling a list-bombing attack. No beehiiv-native CAPTCHA or bot-protection setting was found in beehiiv's support docs (the double opt-in article mentions "anti-bot protection" only in a troubleshooting note).
- A search summary said beehiiv Boosts force a confirmation step even when double opt-in is off; the current (Oct 2026) beehiiv articles opened do not say this. Unverified, possibly outdated.
- A third-party snippet said Milk Road referrals only counted once the referred person signed up "and confirm[ed] their email"; the source page was not identified.

## 2. Welcome and onboarding: what the welcome email does, sequence length, published numbers

### Takeaway
The welcome emails of Morning Brew and The Hustle do the same six things (thanks, short intro, send time, add to contacts, drag to Primary, reply), and the agency standard that large newsletters are coached on is McGarry's "WE-PAC" (Welcome, Expectations, Primary inbox, Ask for a one-word reply, Click a link). Published numbers are thin: McGarry reports welcome emails opening 5 to 10 points above the list average (about 70% open, about 10% click in his 2026 guide); no 1M+ newsletter publishes reply rates or the long-term effect of onboarding.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew welcome email (observed Aug 2023): thank-you, brief intro, delivery timing, add the sender to contacts, move to Primary in Gmail, reply with questions; plus specific Apple Mail guidance and a link to a safelisting article for other clients. Confirmation page: "You're in!" and a prompt to check spam/junk and "move us" to the primary inbox. Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-08-25](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-newsletter-welcome-email-guide) [second-hand observation].
- The Hustle (HubSpot) welcome email (observed Aug 2023): the same six items; the confirmation page asks readers to add the sender to contacts to "ensure you get our emails". Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-08-25](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-newsletter-welcome-email-guide) [second-hand observation].
- Welcome subject lines: The Hustle "Look what you did, you little jerk…"; Morning Brew "☕️ Caution: Morning Brew coming in hot"; Milk Road "bada bing bada boom. You're on my private web3 list."; Marketing Dive (Industry Dive) "Thanks for signing up! A few things to know…". Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-10-14](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/subscriber-flow) [second-hand collection by McGarry].
- Morning Brew referral sign-ups: the post-sign-up email congratulated the new subscriber and showed reward progress to prompt further referrals. Source: [The Growth Playbook, Ryan Kaufman, 2021-03-30](https://thegrowthplaybook.substack.com/p/how-morning-brew-grew-their-business) [second-hand; older, possibly outdated].
- Robinhood Snacks: no welcome email. Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-08-25](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-newsletter-welcome-email-guide).
- theSkimm: the referral ask arrives as an email about five weeks after sign-up, asking the reader to share with 10 friends through a unique URL. Source: [Digiday, 2015-07-20](https://digiday.com/media/theskimm-relies-readers-audience-development/) [second-hand; older, possibly outdated].
- Axios: onboarding journeys built in Sailthru ask new sign-ups which topics they care about; re-engagement starts as early as one week after sign-up with a multiple-choice question on why the person has not opened. Sources: [Digiday sponsored content by Marigold, 2025-05-21](https://digiday.com/sponsored/how-publishers-like-texas-monthly-and-axios-turn-engagement-data-into-revenue) [vendor]; [Digiday, Kayleigh Barber, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [second-hand; older].
- The New York Times: the onboarding for newly registered users pushes four things, per the analyst's reading of the screens: engagement in multiple verticals (news, cooking, sport, audio, games, reviews), signing up to one or more newsletters, games, and the app. Source: [The Audiencers, Madeleine White, 2025-06-21](https://theaudiencers.com/65-the-nyts-new-onboarding-journey-for-registered-users/) [second-hand; author's inference from screenshots, no numbers].
- Matt McGarry, the "subscriber flow" (landing page, thank-you page, welcome email): the thank-you page has three jobs (set expectations for what and when, get the welcome email opened, make people aware of products) and one call to action, two at most (open the welcome email, an offer, past issues, or a survey). Thank-you pages he lists as examples include Morning Brew, The Hustle, The Daily Upside, 1440, James Clear and Tim Ferriss. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-10-14](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/subscriber-flow) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry, welcome email rules: five elements (welcome in 1 to 2 sentences; expectations: what, when, how it helps; ask to move to Primary; ask for a one-word reply such as "yes" and explain why; at least one link, later "at least 2-3 links" with a button). Do not ask an open-ended question because it lowers reply rates. Avoid "welcome" and "thank you" in the subject; keep testing "until your open rate is at least over 50%". Sources: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-02-18](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/easiest-way-to-increase-open-rate); [Newsletter Operator, 2025-05-24](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry, numbers: welcome emails often open 5% to 10% higher than the list's overall rate, from his work with 20+ newsletters (examples 70% vs 65%, 30% vs 25%). His 2026 guide gives about a 70% open rate and about a 10% click rate for the welcome email. Sources: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-02-18](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/easiest-way-to-increase-open-rate); [Newsletter Operator, 2026-09-11](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/welcome-sequence) [first-hand; his own estimates, no sample sizes].
- Matt McGarry, hard-gate option: the thank-you page can promise a free gift inside the welcome email, or state that "they must open your welcome email and follow the instructions inside or they won't receive your newsletter". Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2025-05-24](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry, sequence length: welcome sequence usually 3 to 7 emails; daily newsletters 2 to 3 (example timing 0 min, +2 days, +5 days); weekly newsletters 5 to 10 (0, +1, +2, +3 days, then every 4 days); "try not to send other emails on the same day as your newsletter send"; "Your welcome emails should offer 99% free value and 1% sales." Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2024-04-13](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/email-sequences) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry, 7-email sequence (2026): Day 0 welcome (100 to 300 words); Day 0 plus 6 hours a reminder of the lead magnet or content promised in email 1 (under 100 words, subject such as "Did you get it?"); Day 1 story or mission; Day 2 proof; Day 4 best content with a soft pitch; Day 7 direct pitch; Day 28 a 9-word "are you still looking for..." email with no links. Sequences "often run 14-30 days". Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2026-09-11](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/welcome-sequence) [first-hand].
- SparkLoop: subscribers who arrive through its Signup Flow (co-registration style widget) "haven't seen the publication's website", so they need a separate, context-heavy welcome sequence with an obvious "unsubscribe me" button; for high-volume partners the `RH_PARTNER` field allows a welcome email personalized to the referring newsletter. Sources: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide); [SparkLoop help center, 2026-05-26](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/6816463-managing-subscribers-referred-via-your-partner-program) [first-hand vendor].
- beehiiv: Arnold's Pump Club uses a reply-to-engage ask in place of double opt-in. Source: [beehiiv blog, 2024-06-25](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/single-opt-in-vs-double-opt-in) [vendor].
- [generic, not from a 1M+ operator] Campaign Monitor: welcome email read rates about 42% higher than the average email, as cited in [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-08-25](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-newsletter-welcome-email-guide). Return Path, as cited by Validity: subscribers who read three messages in a welcome series went on to read 69% of later messages versus 18% for those who got one. Source: [Validity blog](https://www.validity.com/blog/best-practices-for-a-successful-onboarding-series/) [snippet; date not captured; correlation, not a controlled test].

### Inferences
- The welcome email at this scale is a deliverability instrument first (reply, Primary, click) and an introduction second. None of the 1M+ examples found uses it to survey the reader; the survey, where it exists, sits on the thank-you page or in the sign-up widget (section 3).
- The only documented onboarding designed for people who did not see the newsletter's own landing page is SparkLoop's: extra context about what this is and why they are getting it, plus a prominent way out. That is the closest published template for quiz or tool sign-ups.
- McGarry's "+6 hours: did you get it?" email is in effect a bridge email for lead-magnet sign-ups: it re-delivers the thing they asked for and uses it to earn a reply or click.

### Gaps
- No published reply rate for any 1M+ newsletter's welcome email. A podcast snippet mentions a 15% reply rate from a yes/no welcome question at a small newsletter; not from a 1M+ operator and not verified.
- No controlled result found on the effect of an onboarding sequence on 30/60/90-day engagement at a 1M+ newsletter.
- Welcome or onboarding contents "not found" for 1440, theSkimm (beyond the 2015 referral email), Milk Road (beyond the subject line), The Rundown AI, The Neuron, TLDR, The Daily Upside, The Washington Post, Workweek, James Clear, Sahil Bloom and Lenny's Newsletter. A Growth in Reverse page on James Clear returned 404.

## 3. Onboarding surveys and zero-party data

### Takeaway
Documented cases are few: Axios asks topic interests during onboarding and used the answers to launch targeted local newsletters, and smaller business newsletters (The Peak) ask job level and function right after the email field. The tooling large newsletters use now supports it natively (SparkLoop Welcome Surveys with preset job-role and company-size questions, beehiiv survey forms writing to custom fields), with the vendors recommending 3 to 5 questions. No 1M+ newsletter publishes a completion rate.

### Cited Findings
- Axios: onboarding journeys in Sailthru collected zero-party data (which topics the reader cares about) at sign-up; the answers let Axios launch highly targeted local newsletters, credited with more than 300,000 new subscribers; personalized send times raised open rates 8%; churn flows made it easier for unengaged readers to leave. Source: [Digiday sponsored content by Marigold, 2025-05-21](https://digiday.com/sponsored/how-publishers-like-texas-monthly-and-axios-turn-engagement-data-into-revenue) [vendor; no baseline or method given].
- The Peak: after the email is entered, a quiz asks "job level and job function" (unclear whether optional). The DONUT: a "10 second quiz to personalize your experience" comes before sign-up and decides which products are offered. Source: [The Newsletter Newsletter, 2023-03-24](https://thenewsletternewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/sign-up-flows-across-five-companies) [second-hand observation; neither confirmed at 1M+].
- SparkLoop Welcome Surveys: "a quick survey step shown inside your Signup Flow widget, right after a new subscriber signs up"; up to 15 questions and promotions, but "we strongly recommend keeping it to 5 or fewer" and "3 to 5 questions is the sweet spot"; preset "system questions" (name, age, job role, company size, gender) get "better completion rates than with a custom equivalent"; answers sync to custom fields in Kit, beehiiv, Mailchimp, Campaign Monitor and ActiveCampaign or export as CSV. Source: [SparkLoop help center, updated about Oct 2026, exact date not shown](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/14665838-welcome-surveys-in-signup-flow) [first-hand vendor; no completion numbers].
- beehiiv survey forms can be embedded at sign-up or triggered after a subscriber joins; responses are stored as custom fields on the subscriber profile for segmentation. Source: [beehiiv features page](https://www.beehiiv.com/features/survey-forms) [snippet, vendor].
- Matt McGarry: a survey is one of four possible calls to action on the thank-you page (with a link to the welcome email, an offer, or past issues), one CTA preferred; survey completion is a recommended trigger for a 1 to 3 email automated sequence; a reply question he suggests for email 2: "If you could only get one thing from signing up for this newsletter, what would it be?" Sources: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-10-14](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/subscriber-flow); [Newsletter Operator, 2024-04-13](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/email-sequences); [Newsletter Operator, 2026-09-11](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/welcome-sequence) [first-hand].
- The Hustle: Sam Parr said "We have gold, silver, and bronze users", tracked by internal systems (an engagement tiering, not a survey). Source: [Practical Ecommerce, 2020-07-31](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/1-million-email-subscribers-is-fricking-hard-says-thehustle-co-founder) [first-hand interview; older, possibly outdated].
- The Rundown AI advertises audience composition to sponsors (a BuySellAds listing says 45% C-suite/executives and 80%+ US), but how that data is collected is not stated. Source: [BuySellAds listing](https://discover.buysellads.com/the-rundown-ai) [snippet].

### Inferences
- At 1M+ scale the survey answers documented in use feed product decisions and segmentation (Axios local newsletters) and sponsor sales data (job role, company size presets). No source documents answers being used to personalize the welcome track.
- Placement matters more than the tool: the documented positions are inside the sign-up widget or on the thank-you page, immediately after the email is captured, never in a later email.

### Gaps
- Completion rates: none published by a 1M+ operator. One unverified internal-looking dashboard for a publication called Status News showed 58% Typeform completion in week 1 with a 36% drop-off on question 2; provenance unknown, not from a 1M+ newsletter, do not cite.
- Whether Morning Brew, The Rundown AI, The Neuron, Workweek or Industry Dive ask job or interest questions at sign-up, and how they use them: "not found".

## 4. Source-level quality control: tagging every subscriber and judging each source on downstream engagement

### Takeaway
The documented method is: tag source, ad set and ad through UTMs, wait until a cohort has 200 to 1,000+ subscribers and 30+ days on the list, then compare open rate and click rate by source and pause what lags; pay partners only for subscribers who survive an engagement screen. Published numeric rules exist on the vendor side (SparkLoop screening of 7 to 21 days with open/click conditions; beehiiv auto-pause when a partner's verified subscribers fall below an operator-set open rate, example 20%, after at least 100 subscribers) and as McGarry's channel benchmarks; 1440 publishes the clearest operator-side unit economics (about $3 CAC, $0.05 per open, about 4 months payback).

### Cited Findings

Operator practice

- Matt McGarry, tagging: UTM Source "facebookads", UTM Medium = dynamic ad set name, UTM Campaign = dynamic ad name (snake case so the dynamic values populate), then review open rate by source, medium and campaign through segments in beehiiv. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-04-08](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/paid-acquisition-crash-course) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry, cut rules: once you have enough data ("200-1,000+ subscribers and 30+ days on list"), pause ads or ad sets with low open rates or CTRs; before that, pause on cost (ads above target CPA when others are at or below it; 0 conversions after 7 to 14 days; 1,000 to 2,000+ impressions with 0 conversions; ad sets with 10k+ impressions and 0 conversions). Benchmarks: CPA $1.00 to $3.00, landing-page CVR above 35%, CPC under $1.20, CTR above 1.50%, CPM under $20, frequency under 1.30, payback 1 to 4 months; expect ad subscribers to open about 5% less than the overall list. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-04-08](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/paid-acquisition-crash-course) [first-hand; no numeric open-rate floor is given].
- Matt McGarry, channel quality table (his estimates): Meta $1 to $3 CPA, 40% to 55% open, 5% to 15% CTR; X $1 to $4, 45% to 60%, 5% to 15%; TikTok $2 to $3, 40% to 50%, 5% to 10%; beehiiv Boosts $1 to $2, 35% to 50%, 3% to 6% ("pay only for active subscribers", low volume); newsletter ads $4 to $7, 50% to 65%, 10% to 25% ("Extremely high subscriber quality"); SparkLoop Partner Program $1 to $2, 30% to 40%, 1% to 2% (low quality, many sign-ups come through co-registration and some are accidental); LiveIntent $3 to $6, 45% to 60%, 5% to 15% ($10k minimum); Google and Bing search $5 to $7, 50% to 60%, 5% to 15% (high intent, low volume); co-registration $0.20 to $0.35, 25% to 40% open, under 1% CTR ("Very low subscriber quality", high unsubscribe and spam rates). Channel users he names: Meta (Morning Brew, The Hustle, 1440, TLDR, Contrarian Thinking); Google/Bing (Morning Brew, The Hustle, theSkimm); LiveIntent (1440, The GIST, The Hustle). Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-09-23](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/the-best-paid-acquisition-channels) [first-hand estimates].
- Scale of paid acquisition at the named newsletters, per McGarry: Milk Road 85%+ of 250k subscribers from paid, over 150k from Facebook ads; The Hustle over 1M of 2M+ from Facebook ads; 1440 and TLDR majority from social ads; Morning Brew, Industry Dive and Workweek also majority paid. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-04-08](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/paid-acquisition-crash-course) [second-hand for those companies].
- 1440 (Tim Huelskamp): about 70% paid, 30% organic/word of mouth; 200K+ new sign-ups a month; subscribers bought through other newsletters open at roughly 2x the rate of paid-social subscribers at roughly 2x the CPA and with limited scale; Facebook/Instagram and Google give near-limitless scale, lower CPA and lower quality; "if we're seeing a high-quality subscriber at a low CPA, that's all that matters for our business model"; about 1,500 ad creatives tested in a quarter; founders should not "even think about paid ads until you have a 45-50% open rate". Source: [Newsletter Circle interview, Ciler Demiralp, 2024-02-23](https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/1440-by-tim-huelskamp-and-andrew) [first-hand interview].
- 1440 unit economics: about 25 emails a month, the average subscriber opens 15; $0.05 revenue per open, about $0.75 per subscriber per month; lead acquisition about $3; payback about 4 months; retention above 50%; target LTV:CAC 3:1. Source: [Letterhead blog, Cameron Smith, 2026-01-28](https://blog.tryletterhead.com/blog/how-1440-scaled-monetized-4.5-million-subscribers) [second-hand, citing a Huelskamp video interview; the article's own example ($3 returning $12) is 4:1, which does not match the stated 3:1].
- 1440: "We sell ads based on how many subscribers open the email" (Huelskamp), which is why the quality metric is opens. Source: [Campaign Monitor blog, 2020-10-01](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/) [first-hand quote in a vendor post; older].
- 1440 via LiveIntent: subscribers sourced from LiveIntent were "2.5x more likely to open the newsletter" than those from social channels; 13% increase in incremental monthly subscribers. Source: [LiveIntent case study, undated, about 2024](https://www.liveintent.com/case-studies/1440-grows-monthly-subscriber-list/) [vendor].
- Milk Road: "Paid channels were evaluated using engagement and unsubscribe rates." Source: [beehiiv case study, 2025-04-07](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/milk-road) [vendor; no thresholds].
- Morning Brew: average CPA across channels $2 to $5 (2019); Tyler Denk called unique opens "the only metric that really matters". Sources: [Digiday, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/); [Talking Biz News summary of Digiday, 2019-10-17](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/how-morning-brew-focuses-on-total-opens-not-total-subscribers/) [second-hand; older].
- The Hustle (Adam Ryan, president): referred subscribers open at roughly double the average rate and have lifetime value "multiple times higher than the average subscriber". Sam Parr: about 50% of 300,000 net new subscribers in June 2020 came from paid channels (Facebook, podcasts, YouTube); "If an email address is worth $15, for example, I'll spend a lot of money to obtain them at $2" (a hypothetical). Sources: [Digiday, 2020-07-10](https://digiday.com/media/its-an-undervalued-growth-channel-publishers-eager-for-subs-increasingly-see-high-value-in-newsletter-referral-programs/); [Practical Ecommerce, 2020-07-31](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/1-million-email-subscribers-is-fricking-hard-says-thehustle-co-founder) [first-hand interviews; older].
- TLDR (Dan Ni): acquiring a subscriber through ads costs between $5 and $10. Source: [Inc. profile, reposted](https://inc-1-smart-business-story.beehiiv.com/p/the-man-silicon-valley-reads-every-morning) [snippet; date not captured].
- SmartBrief (Joe Webster, VP marketing), on whether to keep people who joined for an incentive: "If they came in because of an offer and they haven't engaged with you since then, then probably not." Source: [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [first-hand quote; SmartBrief runs 275 newsletters; older].

Vendor rules that enforce pay-for-engagement

- SparkLoop, definition: CPA should mean "the cost of adding a subscriber to your list who actively engages with your content"; "Cost of Engaged Subscribers = Total Acquisition Costs / Number of Engaged Subscribers". Example: a client's $1 Meta CPA needed five subscribers to find one engaged subscriber, so $5 per engaged subscriber, against $3 per engaged subscriber through the partner program. Source: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-05-27](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/8958147-setting-your-partner-program-cpa) [first-hand vendor; single-client example, self-interested].
- SparkLoop, screening: the operator picks a screening period of 1 to 30 days and the criteria (opens, clicks, still subscribed). Example criteria: click once in the first two weeks and remain subscribed; open two of four emails in the first month and remain subscribed. Set the bar by benchmarking how your existing subscribers from other channels behave in their first 2 to 4 weeks. Source: [SparkLoop help center, Louis Nicholls, 2024-02-13](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/8927934-engagement-screening-best-practices) [first-hand vendor].
- SparkLoop, recommended window: "7 to 21 days", with 14 days "usually optimal" ([2026-05-26](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/6816463-managing-subscribers-referred-via-your-partner-program)); the quality guide a week later says 7 to 14 days and "we recommend 10 days" ([2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide)). The two SparkLoop articles disagree. [first-hand vendor].
- SparkLoop, enforcement detail: a referral who reaches the end of the screening period without unsubscribing counts toward payout "regardless of what's written in your custom partner program conditions", so the operator must run an ESP automation that unsubscribes `pending` referrals with no opens or clicks before the period ends; once confirmed, quality cannot be disputed ("we do not make any exceptions on this"). ESP fields written per subscriber: `RH_ISREF` (`pending`, `yes`, `rejected`), `RH_SOURCE` (`sparkloop-upscribe` for widget sign-ups that never saw the site, `sparkloop-magic`), `RH_PARTNER`. Sources: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide); [SparkLoop help center, 2026-05-26](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/6816463-managing-subscribers-referred-via-your-partner-program) [first-hand vendor].
- SparkLoop pricing copy: on the Optimized Partner Program "you only pay for subscribers who are still subscribed after 14 days". Source: [SparkLoop help center pricing guide](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/9184574-the-ultimate-guide-to-sparkloop-features-pricing) [snippet].
- SparkLoop, handling a weak partner: report suspected fraud (confirmed fraud means all that partner's referrals are rejected, "even confirmed ones", and a permanent ban); for low-value but non-fraudulent referrals lower that partner's budget or CPA rather than banning; restricting which partners may recommend you is treated as a red flag by the network. Source: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide) [first-hand vendor].
- beehiiv paid recommendations: each subscriber is tagged "Rec: Pending Verification", "Rec: Verified" or "Rec: Failed Verification"; funds are consumed only for verified subscribers from the countries the buyer selects; an auto-pause rule "automatically pauses a paid partner if the open rate of the subscribers they drive falls below a threshold you set", applied after a minimum number of verified subscribers from that partner (minimum 100); the article's example threshold is 20%; beehiiv takes a 20% fee (a $2.50 CPA shows as $2.00 to the recommending publisher). Sources: [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-20](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/14194737991319-faqs-about-grow-and-monetize-boosts); [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-01](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/41830646352535-verification-outcomes-for-paid-recommendation-subscribers) [first-hand vendor].

### Inferences
- The unit that large operators actually optimize is cost per opened email or per engaged subscriber, because ad revenue is sold on opens (1440: $0.05 per open). Cost per subscriber is only an input.
- Open rate by source after 30+ days on the list is the standard judging metric; the only explicit numeric floors published are operator-set (beehiiv's example of 20%) or relative (benchmark against your other channels' first 2 to 4 weeks; expect paid about 5 points below the list average). Nobody publishes a universal "cut below X%" rule.
- SparkLoop's own documentation concedes the mechanism is weaker than "engagement-verified" sounds: unless the operator's own automation unsubscribes non-openers before the window closes, the operator pays for them. McGarry's low rating of SparkLoop-sourced subscribers (30% to 40% open, 1% to 2% CTR) is consistent with that.
- For a list fed by tools and quizzes, the same structure applies to owned sources: one tag per tool or partner, a 30-day cohort read of open and click rate, and a "cost per engaged subscriber" for the paid ones.

### Gaps
- No 1M+ operator publishes its exact threshold for cutting a channel (for example "pause when 30-day open rate is below X%"). "Not found" for Morning Brew, The Hustle, Milk Road, TLDR, The Rundown AI, The Daily Upside.
- The length of beehiiv's verification window and its pass criteria are not disclosed by beehiiv.
- No source describes how Google Performance Max cohorts specifically are judged by any 1M+ newsletter; McGarry covers Google/Bing search ads only.
- A McGarry statement that paid-recommendation subscribers "sign up by accident, have no context about your newsletter, and never buy anything" appeared only in a search summary of [his growth tier list](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/growth-tier-list) [snippet].

## 5. Early-life pruning: removing new subscribers who never open

### Takeaway
The published rules cluster at two to three weeks for never-openers: 1440 removed new subscribers who opened nothing in 14 days or 10 messages; Morning Brew gave three weeks to open a first email, then 48 hours to respond to a last-chance email; Axios asked after one week and removed non-responders after two; SparkLoop tells buyers to unsubscribe referred subscribers with no engagement inside 7 to 14 days. McGarry's looser agency rule is 30 to 60 days. All the named-operator rules are from 2018 to 2020 and none was found restated after Apple MPP.

### Cited Findings
- 1440: new subscribers get 14 days (10 messages) to open an email before they are automatically removed; a separate drip targets people who have temporarily lost interest; the team gets a daily monitoring email on the program's back end; open rate "a consistent 55%+". Steigerwald: "Deliverability is the lifeblood of our business." Source: [Campaign Monitor blog, 2020-10-01](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/) [vendor post with first-hand quotes; older, possibly outdated: 1440 has since changed ESP per the Letterhead article above].
- Morning Brew (Tyler Denk, senior product lead): readers who do not open their first email within three weeks of signing up get a re-engagement email and are removed if they do not respond within 48 hours; the same 48-hour process applies to anyone who goes 60 days without opening; 1.6M active subscribers against more than 3M lifetime sign-ups. "we don't care about the vanity metric of total subscribers. We really care about the total opens". Source: [Digiday, Kayleigh Barber, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers); summarized at [Talking Biz News, 2019-10-17](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/how-morning-brew-focuses-on-total-opens-not-total-subscribers/) [first-hand quotes via Digiday; older, possibly outdated].
- Morning Brew, a year earlier (700,000+ subscribers): six weeks without an open triggered a message from the CEO restating the value. Austin Rief: for someone who signed up and forgot, "everything we send them looks like spam to them"; Facebook ad retargeting of lapsed readers did not work ("If you don't want a daily email, the platform's not going to matter"). Source: [Digiday, 2018-11-05](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers) [older]. The window tightened from six weeks (2018) to three weeks for new sign-ups (2019).
- Axios: starts as early as one week after sign-up with a multiple-choice question on why the person has not opened; a second-week churn process asks anyone with no engagement in two weeks whether they want to stay, and removes those who do not answer; average open rate 43% before and after. Source: [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [second-hand; older].
- Group Nine Media (Noah Keil, SVP audience development): new subscribers are harder to win back than long-time openers, so "If somebody is newer, we're going to try and move them through that process a lot quicker." Source: [Digiday, 2018-11-05](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers) [first-hand quote; older].
- SmartBrief: automated drip begins at 30 days without opens, on its top 50 revenue newsletters. Source: [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [older].
- The Wall Street Journal (Annemarie Dooling): on inherited lists that had never been cleaned, fewer than 1% of people put through a win-back campaign re-engaged ("less than 1,000 for a huge list" of 200,000 to 300,000). Dan Oshinsky in the same piece: aim automated re-engagement at about the 30-day mark; "trying to win them back after five years, that's tough". Source: [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [older].
- Matt McGarry: "remove non-openers after 30-60 days" (2023). Win-back trigger: no opens or clicks for 14 days on a daily newsletter, 30 days on a weekly; 1 to 2 emails, "No more than 3" because "Any more will increase spam complaints"; second email 3 to 5 days after the first (2024). His 2025 version: email 1 "Greatest Hits" after 30 days with no open or click, email 2 "Good Bye" 3 to 5 days later with a stay-subscribed button, then removal; after 60 to 90 days without an open or click "it's extremely hard to win them back". Sources: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-02-18](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/easiest-way-to-increase-open-rate); [Newsletter Operator, 2024-04-13](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/email-sequences); [Newsletter Operator, 2025-05-24](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry, engagement-based sending instead of deletion: a "base sending segment" = signed up in the last 10 days OR 1+ open in the last 30 days OR 1+ click in the last 60 days (week 1), widened weekly to 30/90/180 days by week 4; it should be about 50% to 80% of the full list; exclude any address with a bounce. Note the first rung: a new subscriber with no open drops out of the main send after 10 days. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2025-05-24](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate) [first-hand].
- SparkLoop (paid and partner cohorts): unsubscribe new referred subscribers who show no engagement within 7 to 14 days and do not pay for them: "cut ruthlessly". Source: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide) [first-hand vendor].
- beehiiv (paid recommendation cohorts): subscribers who fail verification "stay on your list by default, and you control what happens next"; the old auto-clean that removed them "has been removed" ("Auto-clean is gone"); beehiiv supplies a pre-built automation template that removes failed subscribers, or sends a re-engagement email first. Sources: [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-01](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/41830646352535-verification-outcomes-for-paid-recommendation-subscribers); [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-20](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/41790356251159-boosts-to-paid-recommendations-what-changed) [first-hand vendor].
- Dan Oshinsky: fast-growing lists should run reactivation series continuously, slower lists monthly or quarterly. Source: [Inbox Collective, 2023-06-14](https://inboxcollective.com/should-i-use-single-opt-in-or-double-opt-in-for-my-newsletter/) [first-hand].

### Inferences
- The faster the list grows through paid or incentive channels, the shorter the never-opened window the operator publishes (1440 at 14 days with about 70% paid growth; Morning Brew three weeks; SparkLoop cohorts 7 to 14 days). Message count is used alongside days (1440: 10 messages), which matters for a newsletter that sends less than daily: 10 messages is 10 weeks for a weekly sender, so a weekly newsletter cannot copy the day counts.
- Morning Brew's 3M lifetime versus 1.6M active implies it had removed or lost close to half of everyone who ever signed up by Oct 2019. Heavy early pruning is how these lists hold 40%+ open rates while buying most of their growth.
- A never-opened rule based on opens is weaker after Apple MPP, because machine opens keep Apple Mail users "active". McGarry's 2025 segments add clicks as a second signal for that reason; none of the 2018 to 2020 operator rules has been re-published with an MPP adjustment.

### Gaps
- No post-2021 statement of Morning Brew's, 1440's or Axios's never-opened rule was found; current policies unknown.
- A quote attributed to Sam Parr ("If someone isn't active for 30 days, we send them an email campaign asking where they've gone. If they don't reply to that, we scrub them.") appeared in a search summary, but it is not in the Digiday 2016 or Practical Ecommerce 2020 articles that were opened; original source not located, so unverified.
- No published early-pruning rule found for theSkimm, Milk Road, The Rundown AI, The Neuron, TLDR, The Daily Upside, Sherwood, NYT, The Washington Post, Industry Dive, Workweek, James Clear, Sahil Bloom or Lenny's Newsletter.
- No published rule specific to giveaway or co-registration cohorts beyond Oshinsky's double opt-in advice and SmartBrief's "probably not".

## 6. Imported and partner-sourced subscribers (acquisitions, swaps, events, lead magnets, product users)

### Takeaway
No 1M+ newsletter was found documenting a formal permission pass or warm-up stream for imported lists. The best documented case is McGarry's own small acquisition: imported straight in with announcement emails, it opened at 39.3% against 60% for his list and unsubscribed at about 10.8% in six weeks, and his lesson was to send 5 to 6 transition emails, three of them from the previous owner. The vendor rules are consent attestation at import (beehiiv), separate welcome tracks for people who never saw your site (SparkLoop), and double opt-in for incentive entrants (Oshinsky).

### Cited Findings
- Matt McGarry, acquiring a newsletter (about 1,600 subscribers, about 1,200 net of the 400 to 500 already on his list): sequence was (1) simultaneous announcement on Twitter, (2) the seller emailed his list about the news, (3) McGarry imported the list and the imports automatically received his welcome email, (4) four hours later he emailed that segment about the acquisition, (5) one day later a reminder went out from the seller's beehiiv account. No opt-in or permission email, no warm-up. Results after 6 weeks and 7 sends: open rate 39.3% vs 60% for his overall list, CTR 8.2% vs 12%, unsubscribe rate listed as "10.8", engagement "lower than I expected". His suspected causes: purchased rather than opted in, too few transition reminders, deliverability, content fit. Lessons: send 5 to 6 transition emails with three from the previous owner ("It's better to overcommunicate"); pay about $0.30 to $1 per subscriber, never more than $1; before buying, get into the seller's ESP and check acquisition sources, engagement, spam reports, geography, list-cleaning history and Apple MPP share. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-12-30](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/i-bought-a-newsletter) [first-hand, but a small list, not a 1M+ import].
- beehiiv import rules: the importer must confirm that "Everyone you're importing knowingly consented and opted in to your newsletter"; a false claim "violates beehiiv's ToS and Privacy Policy and can lead to immediate termination of service"; beehiiv filters duplicate, invalid and inactive addresses; a tag applied at import goes to every subscriber in that batch and import tags are added to, never overwrite, existing tags; imported subscribers are enrolled only in automations that are live. Source: [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-01](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/12231316510871) [first-hand vendor].
- SparkLoop: subscribers who arrive without visiting your site (`RH_SOURCE` = `sparkloop-upscribe`) must be routed to their own welcome sequence and the correct segment before launch, with heavy context and an obvious unsubscribe button, and unsubscribed if they show no opens or clicks inside the screening period. Sources: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-05-26](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/6816463-managing-subscribers-referred-via-your-partner-program); [SparkLoop help center, 2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide) [first-hand vendor].
- Dan Oshinsky: giveaway and sweepstakes entrants "don't necessarily want your newsletter", so send them a double opt-in confirmation even if the main site is single opt-in. Source: [Inbox Collective, 2023-06-14](https://inboxcollective.com/should-i-use-single-opt-in-or-double-opt-in-for-my-newsletter/) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry on co-registration (sign-ups bundled into another flow, often giveaways): $0.20 to $0.35 per subscriber, 25% to 40% open rate, under 1% CTR, "Very low subscriber quality", high unsubscribe and spam rates; ranked last of nine paid channels. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-09-23](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/the-best-paid-acquisition-channels) [first-hand estimates].
- Matt McGarry, partner giveaway format (credited to Stacked Marketer): 3 to 5 newsletters share one prize and one landing page where the entrant signs up for all of them. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-02-18](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/easiest-way-to-increase-open-rate) [first-hand description; no quality data].
- 1440: between 1K and 50K subscribers it grew through newsletter swaps and cross-promotions and giveaways run through DojoMojo. Source: [Newsletter Circle, 2024-02-23](https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/1440-by-tim-huelskamp-and-andrew) [first-hand interview; no description of how those cohorts were screened, though the 14-day/10-message removal rule in section 5 applied to all new subscribers in 2020].
- The Wall Street Journal: 50 inherited newsletter lists, some several years old and never cleaned, recovered fewer than 1% through a win-back campaign. Source: [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [first-hand quote via Digiday; older].
- SmartBrief on incentive sign-ups who never engaged: "probably not" worth keeping. Source: [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [older].
- Kit: single opt-in is acceptable only in narrow cases such as "a live event, sharing a form with only a select group". Source: [Kit help center, 2026-06-25](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2971364-the-all-important-double-opt-in) [first-hand vendor].
- TLDR: early readers partly came from the free plan of the founder's other product, ScraperAPI. Source: [Inc. profile, reposted](https://inc-1-smart-business-story.beehiiv.com/p/the-man-silicon-valley-reads-every-morning) [snippet; no detail on consent or onboarding of those users].
- [generic, not from a 1M+ operator] After a merger, one acquirer sent three separate emails telling subscribers that permission would pass from one brand to the other on a set date. Source: [MarketingProfs, date not captured](https://marketingprofs.com/short-articles/732/you-dont-know-i-want-to-go) [snippet].

### Inferences
- The one measured import (McGarry) shows the cost of merging without a permission step: about one third lower open rate and roughly 1 in 10 unsubscribing within six weeks, even for a same-niche, same-platform list announced by the seller.
- Combining the documented pieces gives the de facto large-operator treatment for any cohort that did not ask for the newsletter: tag the batch at import, give it its own context-heavy welcome, keep it out of pay-or-keep status until it opens or clicks inside 7 to 14 days (or 10 messages), and remove the rest. No source documents a slower volume ramp specifically for imports.
- beehiiv's consent attestation puts the legal and account risk of quiz, partner and bulk imports on the importer; "knowingly consented and opted in to your newsletter" is a stricter standard than "gave an email to get quiz results".

### Gaps
- No documentation found of how Morning Brew, 1440, Industry Dive, Workweek or HubSpot (when it absorbed The Hustle's list) handled acquired or migrated lists: "not found".
- No 1M+ operator account of list swaps with a partner (how swapped subscribers are confirmed, segmented or pruned) was found.
- A search summary attributed to beehiiv a recommendation to exclude unengaged imported subscribers from sends for the first 2 to 4 weeks, a 10-subscriber import cap for new accounts and Stripe identity verification for larger imports; the import article that was opened (updated 2026-10-01) does not contain these, so they are unverified.
- Substack's official import and confirmation policy was not found; only third-party guides.

## 7. Referral programs: policing fraud and low-quality referrals

### Takeaway
The one documented anti-fraud control at a 1M+ newsletter is Morning Brew's 2019 decision to require double opt-in for referred sign-ups, after which it said referred readers were higher quality. The Hustle and theSkimm set a minimum number of referrals before someone counts as an ambassador (4 and 10) but describe no verification. The paid networks police it centrally: SparkLoop claims fraud "below 0.01%", rejects all of a fraudulent partner's referrals and bans the partner.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew: referral program launched 2017, built in-house; double opt-in required for referrals from 2019; referrals 35% of list growth (Oct 2019) and about 1,000 new sign-ups a day with 225,000+ people having referred at least one person (Jul 2020). Reward ladder participation in 2019: about 85,000 reached 3 referrals, 8,500 reached 10, close to 1,000 reached 25, about a dozen reached 1,000+. Cost: about $0.25 per acquisition through merchandise rewards versus $2 to $5 average CPA across channels. Sources: [Digiday, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/); [Digiday, 2020-07-10](https://digiday.com/media/its-an-undervalued-growth-channel-publishers-eager-for-subs-increasingly-see-high-value-in-newsletter-referral-programs/); [Referral Rock, updated 2023-08-01](https://referralrock.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program/) [second-hand; older].
- The Hustle: an "ambassador" is a subscriber with at least four sign-ups; more than 10,000 ambassadors; referrals at least 10% of free-list growth (2019); referred subscribers open at about twice the average and have several times the lifetime value (Adam Ryan, 2020); in 2016 it had 430 ambassadors rewarded with a hoodie or a conference ticket worth $250 to $400. Sam Parr: the ambassador program "helped us get to about 500,000". No fraud control described. Sources: [Digiday, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/); [Digiday, 2020-07-10](https://digiday.com/media/its-an-undervalued-growth-channel-publishers-eager-for-subs-increasingly-see-high-value-in-newsletter-referral-programs/); [Digiday, 2016-07-01](https://digiday.com/media/hustle-built-100000-subscriber-email-newsletter-millennial-bros); [Practical Ecommerce, 2020-07-31](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/1-million-email-subscribers-is-fricking-hard-says-thehustle-co-founder) [older].
- theSkimm: a Skimm'bassador is a member who referred 10 or more sign-ups; more than 30,000 of them; referrals 20% of Daily Skimm growth (2019), 10% of sign-ups (2015). No verification described. Sources: [Digiday, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/); [Digiday, 2015-07-20](https://digiday.com/media/theskimm-relies-readers-audience-development/) [older].
- Milk Road: one successful referral earned a crypto investor PDF, fully automated through beehiiv. Source: [beehiiv case study, 2025-04-07](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/milk-road) [vendor].
- Finimize (close to 1M subscribers in 2020; founder Maximilian Rofagha): referred readers open above the roughly 40% average, but "We noticed that our most engaged users are not necessarily the people who use the referral program"; unincentivized word of mouth remained the main driver. Source: [Digiday, 2020-07-10](https://digiday.com/media/its-an-undervalued-growth-channel-publishers-eager-for-subs-increasingly-see-high-value-in-newsletter-referral-programs/) [first-hand quote; older].
- SparkLoop partner network: genuine fraud "below 0.01%" ("less than 1 in 10'000 referrals", one confirmed instance in the network's history); a "report partner" button; on confirmed fraud "any referrals they've made will be rejected (even confirmed ones)" and the partner is permanently banned. Source: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide) [first-hand vendor claim, not independently verified].
- 1440: the Feb 2024 interview describes about 30% organic/word-of-mouth/referral growth but no formal referral program or fraud control. Source: [Newsletter Circle, 2024-02-23](https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/1440-by-tim-huelskamp-and-andrew).

### Inferences
- The control that large operators documented is structural, not forensic: make a referral count only after the referred address proves it is a real, reachable person (confirmation click at Morning Brew; opens or clicks in a screening window at SparkLoop and beehiiv). Minimum-referral thresholds (4, 10) limit reward cost but do not verify anything.
- Referred readers out-open paid cohorts where measured (The Hustle about 2x, Finimize above average), so the quality risk in referral programs is fake sign-ups for rewards, not real referred readers.

### Gaps
- No description found of specific fraud incidents, IP or device checks, or reward clawbacks at Morning Brew, The Hustle, theSkimm or 1440. Tyler Denk's first-hand write-up could not be opened.
- All referral data for Morning Brew, The Hustle and theSkimm is from 2015 to 2021; current program rules "not found".

## 8. Lead-magnet and quiz funnels at 1M+ scale

### Takeaway
No documented example was found of a 1M+ newsletter that grows mainly through quizzes or tools and explains how it converts those people into readers. The nearest evidence is a 30,000-subscriber case (Generalist World, 22,000+ from one quiz) and agency guidance from McGarry on bridging lead-magnet sign-ups; this is a genuine gap in the public record.

### Cited Findings
- [not a 1M+ operator] Generalist World: a five-minute archetype quiz; "To see results, users enter their email"; Zapier adds each taker to beehiiv with the archetype attached; the welcome sequence opens with "A warm, personal intro email" from the founder, sets expectations and links to key resources; content and offers are segmented by archetype. 30,000+ subscribers, 22,000+ from the quiz. No open or conversion rates given. Source: [beehiiv case study, 2025-04-07](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/generalist-world) [vendor].
- Matt McGarry, bridge pattern for lead-magnet sign-ups: email 2, six hours after the welcome, is a short reminder of the lead magnet or content promised in email 1 (subjects such as "Did you get it?"), built to earn a reply or click; the thank-you page should tell people to look for the gift in the welcome email. Sources: [Newsletter Operator, 2026-09-11](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/welcome-sequence); [Newsletter Operator, 2023-10-14](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/subscriber-flow) [first-hand].
- Matt McGarry, expectation setting: the thank-you page must restate what the newsletter is and when it sends, and the welcome email must state what it covers, when it sends and how it helps, in 1 to 4 sentences. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-10-14](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/subscriber-flow) [first-hand].
- SparkLoop's rule for sign-ups that never saw the newsletter's own page (context-heavy dedicated welcome, obvious unsubscribe, remove if no engagement in 7 to 14 days) is the closest large-network analog to a quiz or tool sign-up. Source: [SparkLoop help center, 2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide) [first-hand vendor].
- The Hustle (Sam Parr): giveaways were used at scale (a $35,000 Tesla giveaway in June 2020, "Those giveaways work"), alongside paid; no account of how giveaway entrants were onboarded. Source: [Practical Ecommerce, 2020-07-31](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/1-million-email-subscribers-is-fricking-hard-says-thehustle-co-founder) [first-hand; older].
- [not a 1M+ operator; snippet] A Growth in Reverse "30 Days of Growth" entry reports a results-gated quiz bringing 1,439 subscribers in a week (daily sign-ups from 17 to 125) with each subscriber tagged by quiz type in beehiiv. Source: [third-party repost on pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/media/news/day-5-the-quiz-that-7xd-their-growth-49c7decf-df75-419f-8dde-2d5ac9e6c6e4/) [snippet, second-hand].

### Inferences
- Since no large operator documents a quiz-to-newsletter path, the applicable evidence is by analogy: giveaway entrants (Oshinsky: confirm them), widget and co-registration sign-ups (SparkLoop: separate welcome, screen in 7 to 14 days) and lead-magnet sign-ups (McGarry: re-deliver the asset, then earn a reply or click). All three treat the person as not yet a newsletter subscriber until they act.
- The Generalist World pattern (result type stored as a field, welcome and offers varied by result) is the only documented case of a dedicated welcome track keyed to the quiz outcome.

### Gaps
- No 1M+ newsletter found that documents quiz, tool, course or gated-content sign-ups and their conversion into readers: "not found" for James Clear (free chapter and habits course), Sahil Bloom, Tim Ferriss, Mark Manson or any large Substack.
- An unverified conference-talk claim of 4.2M subscribers generated by quizzes surfaced in search; no source could be confirmed, so it is excluded.
- McGarry's statements that lead magnets should be finishable in under about 15 minutes and that engagement follows when the lead magnet matches the newsletter's content appeared only in search summaries of newsletteroperator.com; the exact page was not confirmed.

## 9. Retention and engagement benchmarks for new cohorts, and internal reporting

### Takeaway
Stated list-level open rates at the big ad-supported newsletters sit at 40% to 60% (Morning Brew 42% to 45%, The Hustle about 50%, Axios 43%, 1440 55% to 60%+, TLDR 46%), and the cohort expectations that are published are relative: paid cohorts about 5 points below the list, newsletter-sourced cohorts about 2x paid social, an acquired list at about two thirds of the house rate. 1440 is the only one describing internal reporting (a daily monitoring email, opens per subscriber per month, payback period).

### Cited Findings
- 1440: "a consistent 55%+ average open rate" (2020); 60%+ open rate held since week 1 (Feb 2024); the average subscriber opens 15 of about 25 emails a month, retention above 50%, payback about 4 months (Jan 2026, second-hand); go/no-go gates used by the founders: continue the early experiment only at 40%+ open rate and 10% weekly organic growth, and no paid ads until a 45% to 50% open rate; each morning the team receives an email giving oversight of the email program's back end. Sources: [Campaign Monitor blog, 2020-10-01](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/); [Newsletter Circle, 2024-02-23](https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/1440-by-tim-huelskamp-and-andrew); [Letterhead blog, 2026-01-28](https://blog.tryletterhead.com/blog/how-1440-scaled-monetized-4.5-million-subscribers).
- Morning Brew: open rates 42% (daily), 50% (Emerging Tech Brew), 55% (Retail Brew) in Oct 2019; 45% daily reported in 2021; the reported internal north star is unique opens, not subscribers. Sources: [Digiday, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/); [The Growth Playbook, 2021-03-30](https://thegrowthplaybook.substack.com/p/how-morning-brew-grew-their-business); [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers) [older].
- The Hustle: open rate close to 50% with over 1M subscribers (2019); 35% to 40% unique open rate at 100,000 subscribers (2016). Sources: [Digiday, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/); [Digiday, 2016-07-01](https://digiday.com/media/hustle-built-100000-subscriber-email-newsletter-millennial-bros) [older].
- Axios: 43% average open rate (2019). theSkimm: 40% (2015). Finimize: about 40% (2020). Sources: [Digiday, 2019-10-15](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers); [Digiday, 2015-07-20](https://digiday.com/media/theskimm-relies-readers-audience-development/); [Digiday, 2020-07-10](https://digiday.com/media/its-an-undervalued-growth-channel-publishers-eager-for-subs-increasingly-see-high-value-in-newsletter-referral-programs/) [older].
- TLDR: 46% open rate on the flagship per its advertiser pages. Source: [TLDR advertising site](https://advertise.tldr.tech/audiences/ai-engineers/) [snippet; date not captured].
- Cohort expectations (McGarry): ad-sourced subscribers open about 5% less than the overall list; welcome email opens 5 to 10 points above the list; expected first-cohort ranges by channel are in the section 4 table (for example Meta 40% to 55% open, co-registration 25% to 40% open and under 1% CTR); the active "base sending segment" should be 50% to 80% of the list. Sources: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-04-08](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/paid-acquisition-crash-course); [Newsletter Operator, 2023-09-23](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/the-best-paid-acquisition-channels); [Newsletter Operator, 2025-05-24](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/double-your-open-rate) [first-hand estimates].
- Acquired-list benchmark: 39.3% open and 8.2% CTR versus 60% and 12% for the acquirer's list, six weeks after import. Source: [Newsletter Operator, 2023-12-30](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/i-bought-a-newsletter) [first-hand, small list].
- Vendor floors: beehiiv's auto-pause example uses a 20% open-rate threshold after 100 verified subscribers from a partner; SparkLoop describes 50%+ open or 10%+ click targets as "very high" bars that justify a higher CPA. Sources: [beehiiv support, updated 2026-10-20](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/14194737991319-faqs-about-grow-and-monetize-boosts); [SparkLoop help center, 2026-05-27](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/8958147-setting-your-partner-program-cpa) [first-hand vendor].

### Inferences
- The benchmark that matters to these businesses is opens per subscriber per month by cohort (1440: 15 of 25), because it converts directly into revenue per subscriber and payback; a cohort is "good" when its payback is inside about 4 months, not when it hits a fixed open rate.
- Headline open rates at pruned lists are partly an artifact of the pruning (and, since 2021, of Apple MPP). They are not comparable to an unpruned list of 500k, so cohort comparisons inside one's own list (source A versus source B over the same first 30 days) are the transferable part.

### Gaps
- No 1M+ operator publishes day-30, day-60 or day-90 retention curves by acquisition cohort, or a description of its internal cohort dashboard: "not found".
- No current (2025 to 2026) first-hand open-rate figures found for Morning Brew, The Hustle, theSkimm or Axios.

## 10. Where operators disagree

### Takeaway
The disagreements are real and unresolved: double opt-in (Kit, Morning Brew for referrals, HubSpot Media today) versus single opt-in plus pruning (McGarry); the worth of paid-recommendation and partner cohorts (the networks say screened referrals match targets, McGarry rates them low); how fast to cut (7 to 14 days versus 30 to 60 days); and whether referral users are the best readers.

### Cited Findings
- Double versus single opt-in. For double: Kit ("Very rarely, if ever" use single; [2026-06-25](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2971364-the-all-important-double-opt-in)); beehiiv support ("a best practice for email deliverability, and not just a security measure"; [2026-10-09](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/13081072798743-how-to-enable-double-opt-in-and-smart-nudge)); Morning Brew for referrals ([Digiday, 2019-10-14](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-use-free-swag-perks-keep-email-acquisition-costs-lower/)). Against: McGarry ("Don't use double opt-in (20-50% won't confirm)"; [2023-04-08](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/paid-acquisition-crash-course)). Conditional: Oshinsky (depends on growth speed, cleaning habits, ESP and country; double opt-in for giveaways; protect single opt-in forms with verification, reCAPTCHA or a honeypot; [2023-06-14](https://inboxcollective.com/should-i-use-single-opt-in-or-double-opt-in-for-my-newsletter/)); beehiiv blog ("Neither option is right or wrong"; [2024-06-25](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/single-opt-in-vs-double-opt-in)). Note beehiiv's blog and support pages take different tones.
- How much confirmation costs. McGarry: 20% to 40% lost, or 20% to 50% for ad traffic. Oshinsky: 10% to 20% lost at best-in-class organizations, about 50% at average ones. Morning Brew (second-hand): conversion "over 85%" after adding it, or a 15% drop. Sources as above plus [Referral Rock, 2023-08-01](https://referralrock.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program/).
- Quality of paid-recommendation and partner cohorts. SparkLoop: a screened referral at $3 beats a $1 Meta lead that needs five sign-ups per engaged reader ([2026-05-27](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/8958147-setting-your-partner-program-cpa)). McGarry: SparkLoop partner subscribers are low quality (30% to 40% open, 1% to 2% CTR, accidental sign-ups) and Meta is the top channel (40% to 55% open) ([2023-09-23](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/the-best-paid-acquisition-channels)). 1440: paid social is the lower-quality, higher-scale source and other newsletters are the highest quality ([2024-02-23](https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/1440-by-tim-huelskamp-and-andrew)).
- Speed of removal. 14 days / 10 messages (1440, 2020), 3 weeks plus 48 hours (Morning Brew, 2019), 1 to 2 weeks (Axios, 2019), 7 to 14 days (SparkLoop, 2026) versus 30 to 60 days (McGarry, 2023) and suppression from the main send after 10 days without deleting (McGarry, 2025). Sources in section 5.
- Screening window inside SparkLoop's own docs: 14 days "usually optimal" ([2026-05-26](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/6816463-managing-subscribers-referred-via-your-partner-program)) versus "we recommend 10 days" ([2026-06-03](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide)).
- Are referral users the best readers? The Hustle: referred subscribers open at about 2x and are worth several times more. Finimize: "our most engaged users are not necessarily the people who use the referral program". Source: [Digiday, 2020-07-10](https://digiday.com/media/its-an-undervalued-growth-channel-publishers-eager-for-subs-increasingly-see-high-value-in-newsletter-referral-programs/).
- Is open rate the right yardstick at all? Morning Brew and 1440 run the business on opens. A dissenting analyst view is that removing non-openers lifts the rate mechanically (their example: 50% to 71.4%) without making any reader more engaged. Source: [The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/why-i-dont-care-about-your-newsletter-open-rates/) [snippet; date not captured; not from a 1M+ operator].

### Inferences
- The positions line up with business model and traffic mix more than with ideology: ad-revenue newsletters buying most of their growth choose single opt-in and fast pruning because every confirmed-opt-in loss is paid traffic thrown away; platforms that carry the deliverability risk for many senders (Kit, beehiiv support) push confirmation; everyone adds a confirmation or engagement gate where an incentive or a third party sits between the reader and the sign-up.
- For non-newsletter-intent sources (quiz results, partner swaps, bulk imports) every camp, including the anti-double-opt-in one, applies some gate before treating the address as a subscriber. The disagreement is only about whether the gate is a confirmation click before the first email or an open/click test in the first 1 to 4 weeks.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head test published by a 1M+ newsletter comparing confirmed opt-in against single opt-in plus early pruning on long-run engaged subscribers or revenue.
- Positions of The Rundown AI, The Neuron, TLDR, The Daily Upside, Sahil Bloom, James Clear and Lenny's Newsletter on any of these disputes: "not found".
