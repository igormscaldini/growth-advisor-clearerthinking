# List hygiene, engagement measurement, sunsetting and re-engagement at 1M+ newsletters (research notes, compiled 2026-11-06)

Reading guide. Every finding carries a source type and date: [first-hand] = the operator's own statement or post; [trade press] = a journalist quoting the operator; [vendor case study] = published by a vendor about a named customer (self-reported, unaudited); [vendor doc] = product documentation of an ESP or tool that serves large newsletters; [consultant] = practitioner who works with large lists; [generic] = not from a 1M+ operator. Sources dated before September 2021 predate Apple Mail Privacy Protection (MPP) and are flagged "pre-MPP, possibly outdated". Quotes were extracted from the pages by a fetch tool, so spot-check wording against the URL before publishing any quote verbatim. "Not found" means I searched and found no documented policy; it does not mean the newsletter has none.

## 1. How do 1M+ newsletters define an "active" or "engaged" subscriber since Apple MPP?

### Takeaway
No 1M+ newsletter was found publishing a formal post-MPP definition of "active". What is documented is (a) pre-MPP open-based windows of 14 to 60 days at Morning Brew, 1440 and Axios, and (b) post-MPP guidance from the consultants and ESPs that serve large lists, which converges on clicks or "real" (non-machine) opens inside a 30 to 90 day window, plus a separate "opens everything, never clicks" segment treated as inactive.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew (pre-MPP, possibly outdated): a subscriber stays "active" only while they have opened within the last 60 days; the company reported "1.6 million active subscribers" against "over 3 million over its lifetime". Tyler Denk, then senior product lead, called unique opens "the only metric that really matters". [trade press, Oct 15 and Oct 17, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers); [Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/how-morning-brew-focuses-on-total-opens-not-total-subscribers/)
- 1440 (pre-MPP, possibly outdated): engagement test for a new subscriber is at least one open within "14 days (10 messages)". [vendor case study quoting co-founders Drew Steigerwald and Tim Huelskamp, Oct 1, 2020] [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)
- After MPP, Dan Oshinsky (Inbox Collective; former newsletter director at BuzzFeed and The New Yorker) said clients' open rates rose 15 to 20% on average (a 30% rate became 35 to 37%). The Gist said it discounts its reported open rate by about 5% for inflation, landing at 40%. 1440's COO Pierre Lipton said unattributable clicks rose from about 5% to about 9% of click-throughs. [trade press, Jun 16, 2022] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/media-briefing-how-newsletters-publishers-are-dealing-with-a-destabilized-ad-landscape)
- Matt McGarry (Newsletter Operator / GrowLetter, growth consultant): "Do not base your list cleaning and re-engagement on opens alone." Remove subscribers who have not clicked in 45 to 90 days; send win-back emails to those with no click in 7 to 21 days. He ranks metrics: revenue actions and sponsor clicks first, then clicks and CTR, poll responses, reply rate, and open rate last. He cites Litmus: MPP affected 55.82% of opens as of Sep 2023. [consultant, Oct 7, 2023] [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/open-rates-are-dead-apple-mail-privacy-protection)
- McGarry's two inactive segments: (1) no opens in 60 to 90 days; (2) "opens 100% of emails but 0 clicks" in 60 to 90 days, which he treats as likely inactive Apple MPP users. He counts opens, clicks, replies, moving to primary and adding to contacts as engagement. [consultant, Nov 18, 2023] [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/winback-and-clean-email-list)
- Oshinsky: "a reader who always opens but never clicks is probably an inactive user disguised as an active one." He suggests running in-newsletter click prompts for a few weeks first and, where the ESP flags MPP users, targeting only those who appear not to open. [consultant, Apr 5, 2023] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)
- Tyler Denk (beehiiv CEO, ex Morning Brew): open rates are "less reliable, but still mostly directionally correct"; for a free newsletter track unique opens, unsubscribe rate and reader lifetime value; advertisers prefer readers who click. [first-hand interview, dated 19 May, year not shown on page] [Splice Media](https://splicemedia.com/stories/tyler-denk-of-beehiiv-on-audience-engagement-scaling-newsletter-readership)
- beehiiv's re-engagement automation defines unengaged as "90 days without opens or clicks" by default, and lets the sender switch to days without opens, days without clicks, or either, with any day count. [vendor doc, last updated Jun 22, 2026] [beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/16666074781975-how-to-create-an-automation-for-re-engaging-subscribers)
- Sailthru (publisher-focused ESP) "Real Activity" levels count only "Real Opens" and "Real Clicks": Active = one or more real clicks; Passive = real opens but no clicks; New = created inside the window with no real activity; Disengaged = engaged in the past but not inside the window; Dormant = no real opens, clicks or pageviews. "Live users are all users who are Engaged, Active, or Passive." Default "Engagement Days" is 7 on this page; the real-activity-only setting is off by default. [vendor doc, undated] [Sailthru Real Activity](https://products.zetaglobal.com/sailthru/Content/audience/managing-users/user-engagementrealact.html)
- Kit: "Cold subscribers are subscribers who haven't opened an email or clicked any email links in the last 90 days (or 30 days, if they've subscribed to your list for fewer than 90 days)." No MPP handling is described. [vendor doc, page shows Aug 4, 2026] [Kit help](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2537096-how-to-target-engaged-subscribers)
- HubSpot (owner of The Hustle) defines unengaged by send count, not days: "Has never engaged with a marketing email from you and hasn't engaged with the last 11 emails you've sent them" or "Previously engaged ... but hasn't engaged with the last 16 emails you've sent them." Engagement = open or click; no MPP filter is described. [vendor doc, last updated Sep 25, 2026] [HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/marketing-email/what-is-graymail-and-how-can-i-avoid-sending-my-email-to-unengaged-contacts)
- Counter-view on opens: a Cordial deliverability lead writing on Spam Resource says "though MPP negatively affects open tracking accuracy, the data is still useful" for defining inactivity. [deliverability practitioner, May 24, 2023] [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2023/05/the-goal-of-sunsetting.html)

### Inferences
- Two families of definition exist: day windows (Morning Brew 60 days, beehiiv 90, Kit 90/30) and consecutive-send counts (1440 10 messages, HubSpot 11 or 16 sends). Send counts adapt automatically to cadence, which matters for a newsletter that sends less than daily.
- The most copyable post-MPP definition on beehiiv is McGarry's pair: no opens in 60 to 90 days, plus opens-without-any-click in 60 to 90 days, because beehiiv's trigger supports "days without clicks" directly.
- Every published window from a daily newsletter is shorter (14 to 60 days) than the generic 90 to 180 day advice; weekly senders are told to wait longer (Oshinsky: 4 to 6 months).

### Gaps
- Not found: a published post-2021 definition of "active" or "engaged" for Morning Brew, The Hustle, 1440, theSkimm, Axios, The Daily Upside, Milk Road, The Rundown AI, The Neuron, TLDR, Sherwood/Snacks, NYT, Washington Post, Bloomberg, Industry Dive, Workweek, James Clear, Tim Ferriss, Mark Manson, Sahil Bloom or Lenny's Newsletter.
- Whether Morning Brew still uses the 60-day open rule after MPP is undocumented.

## 2. What sunset policies do they run (thresholds, win-back first, purge frequency and size)?

### Takeaway
The only exact, operator-stated policies found are Morning Brew's (60 days without an open, one re-engagement email, removal after 48 hours), 1440's (new subscribers removed after 14 days and 10 unopened messages) and Axios's (two-week check-in, non-responders removed), all from 2018 to 2020. Post-2022 numbers come from beehiiv and consultants: remove after 30 to 60 days (beehiiv webinar), 60 to 90 days (McGarry, beehiiv blog), 90 days (beehiiv default), with a win-back of one to three emails first.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew (pre-MPP, possibly outdated): "If at any given point since signing up a reader hasn't opened the newsletter for 60 days ... a reengagement email will go out and if they don't respond within 48 hours, the name is removed from the list." [trade press quoting Tyler Denk, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Morning Brew a year earlier (700,000+ subscribers): after six weeks without an open the reader gets a message from the CEO restating the value; co-founder Austin Rief said Facebook retargeting of lapsed readers was "minimally effective". [trade press, Nov 5, 2018] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers)
- Morning Brew purge size: "I ran several re-engagement campaigns and churned nearly 100,000 un-engaged subscribers", against the team's wishes, when the open rate was about 20%. [first-hand, Tyler Denk LinkedIn post, about 2018 (page shows only "8y")] [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tyler-denk_when-i-joined-morning-brew8-months-ago-activity-6359020374172200960-86-f). Campaign Monitor repeats "100,000 inactive users" removed. [vendor case study, undated] [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/customers/morning-brew/)
- Morning Brew cadence: Denk described removing unengaged subscribers and anyone who does not opt in "on a day to day basis" (continuous, automated). [trade interview, 2019; page returned 403, wording from a search snippet] [Viral Loops](https://viral-loops.com/blog/millions-of-subscribers-referral-program/)
- 1440 (pre-MPP, possibly outdated): new subscribers get "14 days (10 messages) to open an email before they become automatically removed from the list"; a drip campaign targets subscribers who have "temporarily lost interest" (steps not given). [vendor case study, Oct 1, 2020] [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)
- Axios (pre-MPP, possibly outdated): re-engagement starts one week after sign-up with a multiple-choice question on why the reader has not opened; a "second-week churn process ... asks subscribers who haven't engaged in two weeks since sign-up if they want to stay subscribed and removes those who don't answer." [trade press, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers). Note: some secondary summaries wrongly attribute this two-week process to Morning Brew; the article attributes it to Axios.
- SmartBrief (275 newsletters): automated drip begins at 30 days without an open, applied mainly to its top 50 revenue newsletters. [trade press, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Group Nine Media reviews its automated lapsed-reader programs every three to four months. [trade press, Nov 5, 2018] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers)
- beehiiv webinar (EJ White, Head of Growth): automated workflows should "remove unengaged subscribers after 30-60 days of inactivity". [vendor, Jan 28, 2025] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/inbox-zero-to-hero-mastering-newsletter-deliverability)
- beehiiv's built-in template: trigger at 90 days without opens or clicks, email, 3-day delay, engagement check, repeat, then set status to Inactive; the doc's tip is to segment those subscribers and bulk delete them (export first, deletion is permanent). The doc gives two conflicting defaults for the check (3 days in one step, 7 in the worked example). [vendor doc, Jun 22, 2026] [beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/16666074781975-how-to-create-an-automation-for-re-engaging-subscribers)
- beehiiv blog: inactive = "no opens or clicks in 60-90 days"; standard win-back is 3 emails (day 0, +4 days, +7 days); more than four "typically hurts more than it helps". [vendor, first published Aug 22, 2023, cites 2025 data] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/re-engagement-email-campaign)
- beehiiv blog: clean "every 1-3 months". [vendor, Dec 21, 2023] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/list-hygiene-the-most-underrated-way-to-increase-your-open-rates)
- McGarry: clean "at least once a month"; win-back sequence of 2 to 4 emails ("Any more than that is overkill and will increase spam complaints"; he uses 2); daily newsletters enter win-back 10 to 14 days after zero opens or clicks, weekly newsletters after 21 to 30 days; "If someone hasn't opened your newsletter for 30+ days the odds of getting them to re-engage are low." His article shows win-back email screenshots from The Hustle and Morning Brew (text not extractable). [consultant, Nov 18, 2023] [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/winback-and-clean-email-list)
- Oshinsky: baseline 90+ days without an open; about 60 days for daily senders; 4 to 6 months for weekly or monthly senders; at least three emails over about two weeks, 5 to 7 days apart, sent at a different time of day than usual. [consultant, Apr 5, 2023] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)
- Substack does not sunset by default. Its help page says that when a list has "a high rate of inactive or undeliverable email addresses, we may ask you to remove those emails in order to protect Substack's sending reputation"; the "Prune inactive subscribers" flow targets imported readers who have not engaged with recent posts, lets the writer send a re-engagement email first, and "will never ask you to remove paying subscribers or those who have signed up manually". [vendor doc, undated; page returned 403 to direct fetch, wording from search snippet] [Substack help](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/21145969176212-How-do-I-remove-inactive-or-undeliverable-readers-from-my-publication)
- Google's bulk-sender guidelines say "Consider unsubscribing recipients who don't open or read your messages" and to periodically ask recipients to confirm they want to stay. [mailbox provider, rules effective Feb 1, 2024] [Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126). Yahoo: "Consider sending a reconfirmation email to inactive subscribers periodically." [mailbox provider, enforcement from Feb 2024] [Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)

### Inferences
- Morning Brew's lifetime 3M+ versus 1.6M active (Oct 2019) implies roughly 1.4M addresses had left the list by then through removals plus ordinary unsubscribes and bounces; the split is not published.
- The large daily newsletters used a single last-chance email with a short deadline (48 hours), not a long nurture series; consultants recommend 2 to 3 emails.
- Purging is continuous and automated at the operators that describe it, not an annual event.

### Gaps
- Not found: any sunset rule for The Hustle (only a win-back screenshot exists), theSkimm, Milk Road, The Rundown AI, The Neuron, TLDR, The Daily Upside, Sherwood/Snacks, NYT, Washington Post, Bloomberg, Industry Dive, Workweek, James Clear, Tim Ferriss, Mark Manson, Sahil Bloom, Lenny's Newsletter.
- No post-2021 first-hand statement of Morning Brew's, 1440's or Axios's current thresholds.
- No purge sizes found other than Morning Brew's roughly 100,000.

## 3. Do they keep engagement tiers and email the unengaged tier differently?

### Takeaway
Documented tiering at this scale is mostly about send order and infrastructure, not separate content: send to the most engaged first, keep a dedicated IP, and suppress or remove the bottom tier. No 1M+ newsletter was found documenting a separate domain or IP for its unengaged readers; the closest items are Morning Brew's quarantine accounts for suspicious sign-ups and beehiiv's platform-level IP pools by sender engagement score.

### Cited Findings
- The Hustle: Sam Parr's reported advice is "You should send your email to your power users first", because "If Google sees that they're opening then you will be placed in another user's inbox." [second-hand: Mailmodo newsletter citing Freshworks, undated, refers to 2018 figures; pre-MPP] [Mailmodo](https://mailmodo.com/newsletter/edition-20)
- The Hustle moved off a shared IP pool to a dedicated IP ("We came to get a dedicated IP address and better own our deliverability", co-founder John Havel); reported unique open rate +17.6% and ad CPM +46%. [vendor case study, undated, list then 800,000+; pre-MPP] [Twilio SendGrid](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/the-hustle)
- Morning Brew: moved from a shared to a dedicated IP and a new sending domain, and "implemented an advanced multi-account approach which prevents malicious emails from entering our primary mailing list". [first-hand, about 2018] [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tyler-denk_when-i-joined-morning-brew8-months-ago-activity-6359020374172200960-86-f)
- beehiiv routes mail streams through separate IPs or IP pools according to SendGrid's Engagement Quality Score (a composite of opens, bounces, blocks, spam complaints and engagement recency); it found up to six bad actors a week, and the lowest-performing pool's open rate rose 3% with spam complaints down 50%. This tiers senders, not subscribers. [vendor case study, undated, SEQ launched Aug 2023] [Twilio SendGrid](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/beehiiv)
- Google: when increasing volume, ramp gradually starting with engaged users. [mailbox provider, 2024 rules] [Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)
- Tyler Denk on re-mailing the unengaged: resending to non-openers is "a terrible idea that should be avoided" because inboxes "may heavily factor your latest campaign performance"; his worked example (hypothetical): 10,000 subscribers at 40% open, a resend to the 6,000 non-openers at 5% drags the aggregate to 27%. [first-hand, Jul 12, 2026] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/why-you-shouldn-t-resend-to-subscribers-who-didn-t-open)
- HubSpot graymail suppression: unengaged contacts are automatically excluded from each send (setting on by default) but stay in the database; to mail them, clone the email, turn the setting off and send to that segment. [vendor doc, Sep 25, 2026] [HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/marketing-email/what-is-graymail-and-how-can-i-avoid-sending-my-email-to-unengaged-contacts)
- Inbox Collective (Yanna-Torry Aspraki, deliverability specialist): alternative to unsubscribing is moving inactive readers into a segment that gets mail "a handful of times per year". [consultant, Nov 9, 2022] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/ask-a-deliverability-expert-what-does-my-email-list-say-about-me/)
- SmartBrief's Joe Webster on keeping lapsed readers: "If they've shown value historically, trying to keep them in your audience makes a lot of sense." [trade press, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Sailthru's levels (Engaged, Active, Passive, New, Disengaged, Dormant) are the tier vocabulary available to publishers on that ESP; Hardbounce addresses are never emailed again. [vendor doc] [Sailthru](https://products.zetaglobal.com/sailthru/Content/audience/managing-users/user-engagementrealact.html)

### Inferences
- The operators who describe their practice run two tiers in effect (mail or remove), with a short win-back in between, not a 30/90/lapsed ladder with different frequencies.
- "Send to engaged first" plus "never resend to non-openers" is the consistent thread from The Hustle (2018) to Denk (2026).

### Gaps
- Not found: any 1M+ newsletter documenting 30-day versus 90-day tiers with different send volumes, a reduced-frequency track for lapsed readers, a re-permission campaign, or a separate domain or IP for unengaged readers.
- Word to the Wise (Laura Atkins) and Email Geeks primary posts on tiering were not retrieved; only second-hand summaries surfaced.

## 4. How do they handle bounces, complaints, role addresses, spam traps and bot or fake sign-ups?

### Takeaway
Documented practice is prevention at the door (real-time validation at sign-up, quarantine of suspicious sign-ups, paid-recommendation verification) plus automatic removal on bounce, against the Gmail and Yahoo thresholds of 0.1% target and 0.3% ceiling for spam complaints. The only named 1M+ newsletter tied to a validation vendor is 1440 (Validity).

### Cited Findings
- Gmail bulk-sender rules (5,000+ messages a day, effective Feb 1, 2024): keep spam rates in Postmaster Tools below 0.30% (required) and below 0.10% (target); one-click unsubscribe on marketing and subscribed messages; confirm each address before subscribing; optionally "Automatically unsubscribe recipients who have multiple bounced messages". [mailbox provider] [Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)
- Yahoo: "Keep your spam rate below 0.3%" (calculated on mail delivered to the inbox); honor unsubscribes within 2 days; "Monitor hard and soft bounces as well as inactive recipients"; "Remove invalid recipients from your list promptly". [mailbox provider, enforcement from Feb 2024] [Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)
- Morning Brew: "I also built an API integration to instantaneously validate emails" before they enter the list, plus the multi-account quarantine for malicious sign-ups. Vendor not named. [first-hand, about 2018; pre-MPP] [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tyler-denk_when-i-joined-morning-brew8-months-ago-activity-6359020374172200960-86-f)
- 1440: uses Validity's List Validation and Sender Certification; reports inbox placement "over 99%", a bounce rate "well below the global average" and an immediate 3% open-rate increase; revenue is "99% dependent on email". [vendor case study, PDF file dated Aug 2024] [Validity PDF](https://validity.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/240801-CaseStudy-1440-FINAL.pdf); summary at [casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/validity/case-study/1440-achieves-99-inbox-placement-with-validity). 1440's team also gets a deliverability report every morning. [vendor case study, Oct 1, 2020] [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)
- beehiiv guidance: clean the list if the bounce rate is "over 2%" or if emails are flagged as spam by 0.1% of the audience. [vendor, Dec 21, 2023] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/list-hygiene-the-most-underrated-way-to-increase-your-open-rates). Its 2025 webinar names NeverBounce among tools and recommends double opt-in. [vendor, Jan 28, 2025] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/inbox-zero-to-hero-mastering-newsletter-deliverability). beehiiv reports platform-wide 2025 averages of 98.90% delivery and 0.02% spam complaints. [vendor] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/re-engagement-email-campaign)
- beehiiv paid-recommendation and Boosts subscribers are verified on "some combination of email engagement, location, IP addresses, user agents, behavior, and several additional factors" (criteria deliberately opaque); verification takes 10 to 17 days (normal) or as little as 3 days (relaxed); failures are tagged "Rec: Failed Verification". [vendor doc, Oct 1, 2026, and Boosts FAQ via search snippet] [beehiiv verification outcomes](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/41830646352535-verification-outcomes-for-paid-recommendation-subscribers); [beehiiv Boosts FAQ](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/14194737991319-faqs-about-grow-and-monetize-boosts)
- Adweek on the 2024 Gmail change: Gmail serves more than half the subscribers of many consumer newsletters, and mailbox providers have long punished newsletters sent to many spam traps, meaning abandoned addresses. Rest of article paywalled. [trade press, date not visible, written after the late-2023 Gmail announcement] [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/gmail-message-newsletter-publishers/)
- Aspraki for Inbox Collective: clean out inactive contacts "every three to six months"; names the validator Bouncer and warns no list cleaner catches every harmful address. [consultant, Nov 9, 2022] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/ask-a-deliverability-expert-what-does-my-email-list-say-about-me/)
- SparkLoop says fraud in its partner network is "below 0.01%". [vendor doc, Jun 3, 2026] [SparkLoop](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide)

### Inferences
- Validation happens at the point of sign-up at the operators that describe it, not as a periodic bulk scrub; engagement-based removal then catches traps and dead addresses that validation misses, which is the stated reason Gmail's 2024 change pushed publishers to prune.

### Gaps
- Not found: any 1M+ newsletter's policy on role addresses (info@, admin@), soft-bounce retry counts, or how often it runs a third-party cleaner. No named 1M+ customer was found for Kickbox, ZeroBounce, NeverBounce or Emailable.
- The Validity PDF was too large to read directly; a search snippet says Validity Everest also helps 1440 keep its list "limited to active, engaged contacts", which I could not verify.

## 5. What do they do with new subscribers who never open in their first N emails (early-life pruning)?

### Takeaway
Early-life rules are the best-documented practice: 1440 removes after 14 days and 10 unopened messages, Axios asks at two weeks and removes non-responders, Morning Brew starts its 48-hour removal clock if the first email is unopened within three weeks. For paid cohorts, the networks themselves screen for 7 to 17 days and consultants recommend 21 to 30 day windows.

### Cited Findings
- 1440: "giving new subscribers 14 days (10 messages) to open an email before they become automatically removed from the list". [vendor case study, Oct 1, 2020; pre-MPP] [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/)
- Morning Brew: "for those who sign up and don't open the first email within three weeks, the same 48-hour reengagement process begins, with slightly different messaging." [trade press, Oct 15, 2019; pre-MPP] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Axios: one-week multiple-choice check-in, then a two-week "do you want to stay" email with removal of those who do not answer; it reported holding a 43% average open rate. [trade press, Oct 15, 2019; pre-MPP] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Group Nine Media: "If somebody is newer, we're going to try and move them through that process a lot quicker" (Noah Keil, SVP audience development). [trade press, Nov 5, 2018; pre-MPP] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers)
- Oshinsky: for paid, contest or sweepstakes sign-ups use about 30 days of inactivity, or as few as 21 days for giveaway sign-ups. [consultant, Apr 5, 2023] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)
- SparkLoop (paid recommendations): screening period "anywhere between 7-14 days after sign-up", "we recommend 10 days"; the buyer sets custom engagement conditions and must configure its ESP to unsubscribe non-qualifying subscribers "before the screening period expires", otherwise they are paid for with "no exceptions"; Signup Flow subscribers should get a separate welcome sequence with an obvious "unsubscribe me" button. [vendor doc, Jun 3, 2026] [SparkLoop](https://help.sparkloop.app/en/articles/7005560-partner-program-subscriber-quality-guide)
- beehiiv Boosts and paid recommendations: 10 to 17 day verification (3 days relaxed); funds for failed subscribers are returned; since the auto-clean setting was removed, "Subscribers who don't pass stay on your list by default", and beehiiv suggests an automation on the "Rec: Failed Verification" tag to remove them or send a re-engagement email first (a pre-built template removes them). [vendor doc, Oct 1, 2026, and Boosts FAQ via search snippet] [beehiiv verification outcomes](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/41830646352535-verification-outcomes-for-paid-recommendation-subscribers); [beehiiv Boosts FAQ](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/14194737991319-faqs-about-grow-and-monetize-boosts)
- beehiiv's unengaged trigger "excludes people who signed up within the time window", so the default 90-day rule cannot catch a new subscriber before day 90. [vendor doc, Jun 22, 2026] [beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/16666074781975-how-to-create-an-automation-for-re-engaging-subscribers)
- Kit treats subscribers with under 90 days of tenure as cold after 30 days without an open or click, and ships a template for "cleaning new non-engaging subscribers". [vendor doc] [Kit help](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2537096-how-to-target-engaged-subscribers)
- HubSpot: a contact who has never engaged is suppressed after 11 unengaged sends. [vendor doc, Sep 25, 2026] [HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/marketing-email/what-is-graymail-and-how-can-i-avoid-sending-my-email-to-unengaged-contacts)
- McGarry: track metrics by subscriber source and judge paid sources on cost per clicker, not cost per subscriber (his example: a $1 CPA source at 1% CTR costs $50 per clicker; a $2 CPA source at 8% CTR costs $25). [consultant, Oct 7, 2023] [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/open-rates-are-dead-apple-mail-privacy-protection)
- Milk Road bought "more than 150K subscribers at a cost of $1.00 to $1.30 per subscriber" on Facebook; no pruning rule for that cohort is published. [vendor deck, Jan 8, 2024] [beehiiv](https://good.beehiiv.com/p/milk-road-data)

### Inferences
- For a daily newsletter the documented early-life window is 10 to 15 sends (two to three weeks); translated to a weekly or twice-weekly cadence, the same send count would take far longer, which is why consultants give 21 to 30 days for paid cohorts instead.
- On beehiiv, early-life pruning needs its own automation (for example triggered by sign-up plus a delay and a "days without clicks or opens" check), because the stock 90-day trigger skips recent sign-ups.

### Gaps
- No post-MPP first-hand early-life rule from any named 1M+ newsletter; an open-based 14-day rule would now pass every Apple Mail user automatically.
- A Newsletter Operator line seen only in a search snippet ("It's better to send emails to everyone and remove non-openers after 30-60 days", about welcome sequences) could not be tied to a specific article.

## 6. How do they talk about list size versus engaged size, publicly and to advertisers?

### Takeaway
Morning Brew is the clear documented case of leading with "active subscribers" and unique opens while calling total subscribers a vanity metric; 1440 sells on CPM and quotes opens per subscriber. No named 1M+ newsletter was found reporting "30-day actives" to advertisers; after MPP the documented shift is toward clicks, CPA/ROI and brand-lift studies.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew: "we don't care about the vanity metric of total subscribers. We really care about the total opens"; it publicised "1.6 million active subscribers" alongside 3M+ lifetime sign-ups. [trade press quoting Tyler Denk, Oct 15, 2019; pre-MPP] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Morning Brew has run brand-lift studies before, during and after campaigns since early 2019 (Jason Schulweis, EVP brand partnerships). [trade press, Jun 16, 2022] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/media-briefing-how-newsletters-publishers-are-dealing-with-a-destabilized-ad-landscape)
- 1440: Pierre Lipton said publishers "are sharing their CPAs and ROIs a little more" with advertisers after MPP. [trade press, Jun 16, 2022] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/media-briefing-how-newsletters-publishers-are-dealing-with-a-destabilized-ad-landscape). 1440 charges sponsors on CPM rather than CPC and says "The average subscriber opens 15" of roughly 25 emails a month. [third-party write-up citing a Huelskamp interview, Jan 28, 2026] [Letterhead](https://blog.tryletterhead.com/blog/how-1440-scaled-monetized-4.5-million-subscribers). A third-party teardown puts 1440 at 4.27M subscribers, 60 to 65% open rate (the article gives both) and about $0.05 of ad revenue per open. [third-party analysis, Feb 7, 2025] [EmailBreakdown](https://emailbreakdown.beehiiv.com/p/1440-s-growth-lessons-from-4m-readers)
- The Gist tells advertisers it discounts its open rate by about 5% for MPP inflation. A media buyer (Seth Hargrave, Media Two Interactive) said buyers weigh click-through rate and cost per click most. [trade press, Jun 16, 2022] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/media-briefing-how-newsletters-publishers-are-dealing-with-a-destabilized-ad-landscape)
- Milk Road's advertiser data page lists subscribers (250,000), open rate (40%), click rate (2.4 to 3.9%), CPM ($30 to $45), CPC ($4.25 to $5.75) and average ad clicks (700 to 1,100): total list plus click outcomes, no "actives" figure. [vendor deck, Jan 8, 2024, "updated for Q2 2024"] [beehiiv](https://good.beehiiv.com/p/milk-road-data)
- The Hustle reported ad CPM up 46% after its deliverability overhaul. [vendor case study, undated; pre-MPP] [Twilio SendGrid](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/the-hustle)
- Benchmark for context: beehiiv's platform-wide average open rate in 2025 was 41.24%. [vendor] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/re-engagement-email-campaign)

### Inferences
- "Active subscribers" as the headline number only works if pruning is aggressive enough that list size and engaged size are close; Morning Brew's pruning rule and its messaging were the same policy.
- Advertiser-facing proof has moved from opens to clicks and outcomes, so the audience number matters less than cost per click or per action.

### Gaps
- Not found: any media kit or public statement from Morning Brew, The Hustle, theSkimm, Axios, The Daily Upside, Workweek, Industry Dive, TLDR, The Rundown AI or The Neuron defining the audience they sell (30-day actives, unique openers, or total list).

## 7. What results did they report from pruning?

### Takeaway
Reported results are large but self-reported and confounded: Morning Brew went from about 20% to about 45% opens after removing roughly 100,000 subscribers while also changing ESP, domain and IP; 1440 held 55%+ opens with its 14-day rule. Win-back yields are small (under 1% at the WSJ, 2 to 15% per consultants), and no 1M+ operator published a revenue effect of pruning.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew: open rate from about 20% to "around an astonishing 45%", click-through "nearly 15%", and Gmail moved the newsletter to the primary tab, after churning nearly 100,000 unengaged subscribers plus an ESP switch (Mailchimp to Campaign Monitor), new sending domain, dedicated IP and sign-up validation. [first-hand, about 2018; pre-MPP] [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tyler-denk_when-i-joined-morning-brew8-months-ago-activity-6359020374172200960-86-f). Campaign Monitor's version: "125% open rate improvement", "99% deliverability rate" (no baselines given). [vendor case study, undated] [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/customers/morning-brew/)
- 1440: "a consistent 55%+ average open rate" with the 14-day removal rule in place. [vendor case study, Oct 1, 2020; pre-MPP] [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/). With Validity list validation and certification: inbox placement over 99%, immediate 3% open-rate increase. [vendor case study, 2024] [casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/validity/case-study/1440-achieves-99-inbox-placement-with-validity)
- Axios: average open rate of 43% maintained before and after introducing early re-engagement and two-week removal. [trade press, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- The Wall Street Journal (50 inherited lists, some never cleaned): fewer than 1% of subscribers put through re-engagement started engaging again, "less than 1,000 for a huge list" of 200,000 to 300,000 (Annemarie Dooling). [trade press, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Oshinsky: publishers with automated re-engagement around the 30-day mark "typically saw open rates that were two to three times higher than the industry average"; re-engaging is essentially free versus $0.25 to $3 per newly acquired email. [trade press, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers)
- Oshinsky's win-back benchmarks: 2 to 5% on a first campaign, 5 to 15% when repeated; his own Feb 2023 campaign won back 361 of 1,977 (18.3%). He cites the American Heart Association winning back "half a million subscribers" and raising more than $250,000 in 2021 (a nonprofit, not a newsletter publisher). [consultant, Apr 5, 2023] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)
- beehiiv: expect a re-engagement campaign's open rate to run "5-15 points below your list's average". [vendor] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/re-engagement-email-campaign)
- The Hustle: unique open rate +17.6% and CPM +46% after moving to a dedicated IP (an infrastructure change, not a purge). [vendor case study, undated] [Twilio SendGrid](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/the-hustle)
- Counter-result (generic, not from a 1M+ operator; anonymous publisher): one publisher "deleted 30,000 unengaged contacts in one shot" and paid sign-ups softened within weeks; the article estimates about $300 a month saved against roughly $15,000 a year in lost subscription revenue. [generic, updated May 12, 2026] [Leaky Paywall](https://leakypaywall.com/email-list-cleaning-for-publishers/)

### Inferences
- Part of any post-purge open-rate jump is arithmetic: removing non-openers shrinks the denominator without adding a single reader. The reported gains that are not arithmetic are inbox placement (Gmail primary tab, 99% inbox placement).
- Morning Brew's result cannot be attributed to pruning alone because four other changes shipped in the same eight months.

### Gaps
- Not found: any revenue, inbox-placement test or controlled before/after from a named 1M+ newsletter that isolates pruning; any post-2021 result figures from Morning Brew, 1440, Axios or The Hustle.

## 8. Where do operators of 1M+ newsletters disagree?

### Takeaway
The real disagreements are remove versus keep-and-suppress, how much to trust opens after MPP, and whether win-back is worth the effort. The removal camp is the daily, ad-funded newsletters whose product is opens; the keep camp is platforms and publishers with paid tiers or historically valuable readers.

### Cited Findings
- Remove fast, automatically: Morning Brew (60 days then 48 hours; three weeks for new sign-ups), 1440 (14 days and 10 messages), Axios (two weeks). [trade press 2019, vendor case study 2020; pre-MPP] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers); [Campaign Monitor](https://www.campaignmonitor.com/blog/email-marketing/1440-media-high-open-rates/). beehiiv's own guidance: remove after 30 to 60 days. [vendor, Jan 28, 2025] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/inbox-zero-to-hero-mastering-newsletter-deliverability)
- Delete outright: beehiiv's support doc recommends bulk deleting subscribers the automation marks Inactive (irreversible, export first). [vendor doc, Jun 22, 2026] [beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/16666074781975-how-to-create-an-automation-for-re-engaging-subscribers). Oshinsky: unsubscribe and remove rather than suppress, because ESPs often charge for every contact; larger newsletters can spend tens of thousands of dollars a year on dead contacts. [consultant, Apr 5, 2023] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)
- Unsubscribe, do not delete: McGarry says "I recommend unsubscribing them and excluding them from future newsletter sends." [consultant, Nov 18, 2023] [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/winback-and-clean-email-list)
- Keep and suppress: HubSpot (The Hustle's owner) suppresses unengaged contacts per send by default and keeps them. [vendor doc, Sep 25, 2026] [HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/marketing-email/what-is-graymail-and-how-can-i-avoid-sending-my-email-to-unengaged-contacts). SmartBrief keeps readers who have "shown value historically". [trade press, Oct 15, 2019] [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers). Aspraki allows a low-frequency segment mailed "a handful of times per year". [consultant, Nov 9, 2022] [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/ask-a-deliverability-expert-what-does-my-email-list-say-about-me/). A Cordial deliverability lead: "you want to maximize engagement but not at the cost of throwing away live subscribers". [practitioner, May 24, 2023] [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2023/05/the-goal-of-sunsetting.html)
- Prune only when forced: Substack (home of Lenny's Newsletter and the largest paid publications) offers pruning only to publications it flags, only for imported non-engagers, never for paying or manually signed-up readers. [vendor doc; wording via search snippet] [Substack help](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/21145969176212-How-do-I-remove-inactive-or-undeliverable-readers-from-my-publication)
- Never delete (generic, not from a 1M+ operator): move non-responders to a do-not-send segment and "Do NOT delete them. Keep them for the next big news moment or major promotion", because the free list feeds paid conversion. [generic, updated May 12, 2026] [Leaky Paywall](https://leakypaywall.com/email-list-cleaning-for-publishers/)
- Trust in opens: McGarry ("Do not base your list cleaning and re-engagement on opens alone") and Oshinsky (always-opens-never-clicks is "an inactive user disguised as an active one") versus Spam Resource's guest author (MPP open data "is still useful") and Denk (opens are "mostly directionally correct"). [Oct 2023, Apr 2023, May 2023, undated] [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/open-rates-are-dead-apple-mail-privacy-protection); [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/); [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2023/05/the-goal-of-sunsetting.html); [Splice Media](https://splicemedia.com/stories/tyler-denk-of-beehiiv-on-audience-engagement-scaling-newsletter-readership)
- Is win-back worth it: WSJ recovered under 1%; Austin Rief called Facebook retargeting of lapsed readers "minimally effective"; McGarry says odds are low after 30 days and to spend about ten times more effort on the welcome flow than on win-back; Oshinsky says win-back is nearly free and recovers 5 to 15% when repeated. [2019, 2018, Nov 2023, Apr 2023] [Digiday 2019](https://digiday.com/media/publishers-reengage-dormant-email-subscribers); [Digiday 2018](https://digiday.com/media/newsletter-publishers-re-engage-lapsed-subscribers); [Newsletter Operator](https://www.newsletteroperator.com/p/winback-and-clean-email-list); [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-to-set-up-a-reactivation-campaign-to-win-back-inactive-email-subscribers/)
- Win-back length: one email and a 48-hour deadline (Morning Brew, 2019); two emails, four at most (McGarry, 2023); at least three over two weeks (Oshinsky, 2023); three on days 0, 4 and 7 (beehiiv). [sources as above] [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/re-engagement-email-campaign)

### Inferences
- The split tracks the business model: newsletters that sell opens to advertisers (Morning Brew, 1440, Axios) remove quickly, since an unopened send has no revenue and a deliverability cost; publishers whose free list feeds paid subscriptions or occasional big sends lean toward suppression.
- For a ~500k list with both ad or sponsor revenue and a paid membership, "unsubscribe and exclude, keep an export" (McGarry) sits between the two camps and is reversible, unlike beehiiv's bulk delete.

### Gaps
- No direct, on-the-record debate between two named 1M+ operators was found; the disagreement is reconstructed from separate statements made years apart.
- Positions of The Hustle, theSkimm, TLDR, The Rundown AI, The Neuron, The Daily Upside, Workweek, Industry Dive, NYT, Washington Post, Bloomberg, James Clear, Tim Ferriss, Mark Manson, Sahil Bloom and Lenny Rachitsky on delete versus suppress: not found.
